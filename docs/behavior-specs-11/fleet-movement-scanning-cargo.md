# Fleet Movement, Scanning, and Cargo

Behavior specification for warp-speed movement, fuel, scanning/detection, and cargo logistics in the 1995-2000 4X game *Stars!*, written for a clean-room reimplementation. All facts below are restated in original wording from public community research — primarily the long-standing *starsfaq.com* "Stars! Advanced and Technical FAQ" ("Guts" reference), the community-maintained *Stars!wiki* at wiki.starsautohost.org (accessed through its Wayback Machine mirrors, since the live site sits behind an active bot-detection challenge), a widely-mirrored GameFAQs strategy guide, and TV Tropes' mechanics summary — plus the official Player's Guide manual (hosted as a public-domain scan on archive.org) for UI-level descriptions. The original text of those sources was not reproduced. Later revisions of this document also added findings from analysis of the original executable (cited as evidence pointers by function and line number); those are described in original wording, and no source code, disassembly, variable names or unique strings from the executable are reproduced. Where sources are silent, contradictory, or only inferred, this is called out explicitly under "Open Questions" rather than asserted as fact.

## Overview

A *Stars!* fleet moves by being given one or more **waypoints** — destinations with an optional task (colonize, transport cargo, remote-mine, lay mines, scrap, etc.) — and a **warp speed** (1 through 10). Distance covered per year scales with the *square* of the warp factor, so speed has a very steep fuel cost: doubling from warp 5 to warp 10 roughly quadruples the ground covered but the fuel bill grows much faster still, because fuel use scales with both mass and the distance-to-max-distance ratio at that speed. Ships either burn fuel to move (most engines) or, below a per-engine "free" threshold speed, generate a little extra fuel while moving (ramscoop engines). Detection of other empires' fleets and planets is entirely scanner-range-based and split into two tiers — ordinary scanners that only reveal ships/planets while physically in range, and rarer "penetrating" scanners that also reveal a planet's mineral/population/defense details without a ship actually visiting it. Cargo (minerals, colonists, and fuel) is carried in a hull's built-in cargo hold, optionally enlarged with cargo-pod components, and is moved between planets and fleets with per-waypoint load/unload orders, including conditional orders that stop loading/unloading once a target amount is reached. Longer-range logistics can bypass warp travel altogether via player-built Stargates or naturally occurring Wormholes.

## Mechanics

### 1. Warp speed and distance per turn

*Stars!* has ten warp factors. The maximum distance a fleet can cover in a single year at warp factor *N* is `N²` light-years — so warp 4 tops out at 16 ly/year, warp 9 at 81 ly/year, and warp 10 (the fastest speed any ship can ever be ordered to) at 100 ly/year. This relationship is confirmed both by a community minefield-crossing analysis that explicitly measures "100% chance of travelling only 16ly" at warp 4 and notes ships "sometimes travel as far as 81ly at warp 9" (matching 4² and 9² respectively), and by the fan-made *Planets.nu* reimplementation's own help documentation, which states its "Max Distance Per Turn" constant is "equal to warp speed squared." A fleet given a waypoint closer than its maximum range for the chosen speed simply arrives that same year and can act on its task immediately; a fleet given a farther waypoint covers as much of the distance as its ordered speed and available fuel allow and continues from where it stopped the following year.

**There is no engine-based speed limit on orders (code-confirmed; corrects an earlier sentence here that said a ship cannot be ordered faster than its engine's rated maximum).** Every fleet can be ordered to any warp from 0 to 10, plus the Use Stargate value 11, whatever its engines. The waypoint panel's warp gauge always runs 0 to 11 (gauge painter `FUN_1050_2b3e`, `stars.exe.export.c:32560`-`32577`, maximum fixed at 11), and the comma and full-stop keys step the warp between 0 and 11 with no other test (`:4877`-`4919`). The host accepts the stored warp as written, and every engine's fuel table has an entry for every warp 1-10 (`extracted-game-data/component-stats.tsv`), so a slow engine at a high warp simply burns more fuel (§2). What engines do limit is economy, not permission: the fleet's efficient warp (`FUN_1050_69c2`, the highest warp at most 10 at which every engine uses at most 120% fuel) is what the automatic speed routines choose, and the gauge draws warp 10 in its alternative colour when that efficient warp is below 10 (`:32568`-`32575`). Engines' "free" speeds (the highest warp with zero fuel use in the table, e.g. warp 6 for Settler's Delight and warp 4 for the Fuel Mizer) are only the speeds at which no fuel is burned. **Warp 10 is a special case**: it can be selected for *any* ship regardless of its engine, but unless the engine is one of the small set specifically rated "safe" at warp 10 (e.g., Interspace-10, unlocked via the No Ram Scoop Engines trait, or the endgame Trans-Star 10), every individual ship attempting warp 10 risks outright destruction: a 10% chance per year, rolled independently per ship (not per fleet, and not scaled by how many engines that ship's hull mounts).

A second, unrelated risk applies only to fleets built with the **Cheap Engines** lesser racial trait: any time such a fleet is ordered above warp 6, there is a 10% chance per year that the engines simply fail to engage at all (the fleet does not move that turn, and is not damaged or destroyed — contrast this with the warp-10 destruction risk above, which is a different mechanic keyed to warp 10 specifically rather than "above warp 6"). **Independently re-confirmed by direct observation of the running client** — see `race-traits.md` §3a, which also confirms the exclusive Interspace-10 engine (available to the No Ram Scoop Engines trait) reaches Warp 10 with a full, unconditional exemption from the destruction risk described above, not merely a reduced one.

**Alternate Reality colonist loss (warp-acceleration casualties, message 193) - traced in full.** In the fleet movement sweep (`FUN_10b0_1f8c`, `stars.exe.export.c:73447`-`73470`; raw bytes at segment 23 offsets `0x2640`-`0x26cb`) a fleet of an Alternate Reality race (Primary Racial Trait index 8) that carries **more than 10 units of colonists** loses three percent of (its colonist units plus 11), rounded down to a whole unit; the loss is subtracted from the colonist cargo, and message 193 is posted with that figure. **Unit:** the fleet's colonist cargo field (fleet record offset `0x58`, 32-bit) counts hundreds of colonists, one unit per kT of cargo space; the message displays the figure in colonists (the unit count with two zeros appended), so a loss of 3 units is reported as 300 colonists. **It is not tied to a change of speed.** The trigger is simply that the fleet is about to travel this turn, checked once per fleet per turn, in the first movement pass only (pass 0); it fires at any ordered warp from 1 to 10 and every turn the fleet moves, so a long colonist voyage loses about 3% (of units + 11) each leg. The full gate, in order: the fleet is not flagged destroyed; it has at least two waypoints; the first waypoint's task is neither Transport nor Lay Mines; the leg's warp nibble is non-zero; the race's status-flag refusal roll (`turn-generation-engine.md` 5) and the Cheap Engines balky roll (leg faster than warp 6, 1 in 10) have not already cancelled the fleet's move; the leg is an ordinary engine leg (warp nibble below `0xB0`, so Stargate legs are exempt); pass number is 0. Fuel is not consulted: a fleet too short of fuel to complete the leg still loses colonists. Small loads are safe: the result is 0 for 11 to 22 units (1,100-2,299 colonists), 1 unit for 23 to 55, 2 for 56 to 88, and so on, and a loss of 0 posts no message. Examples: 100 units (10,000 colonists) lose 3 units (300 colonists); 400 units lose 12 units (1,200). (Earlier text called the surrounding condition "not traced"; it is the list above. Whether a follower fleet that first moves in a later pass escapes the loss follows from the pass-0 test and was not separately exercised.)

Separately, scout- and transport-oriented play can push a ship faster than its engine's most fuel-efficient "free" speed simply by accepting a steeper fuel burn, up to warp 10 for any engine (there is no per-engine ceiling, see above) — this is informally summarized in community strategy writing as "fuel is speed."

**The per-waypoint Warp Factor control, confirmed by inspection of the exported client.** Each waypoint's ordered speed is stored in the same byte as that waypoint's task-type code (§5 below) — the task type occupies the byte's low nibble, and the warp factor occupies its **high nibble, encoded as the warp value times 16**. The valid range confirmed in the movement-processing code and independently cross-checked in a fleet-order report/summary routine (which separately formats this same high nibble for display) is exactly the UI's 12-entry range: **0 through 10 as ordinary warp speeds, plus a distinct 12th value (nibble 11, byte value 0xB0) meaning "Use Stargate."** This is a real, separately-stored sentinel, not just a UI label for "attempt a gate automatically": the movement code explicitly branches on whether the stored value is below 0xB0 (ordinary engine movement, using the `N²`-distance formula above) or equal to 0xB0 (routed instead to the Stargate-jump-handling code described in §5's task-dispatcher note below), and a separate overspeed-warning check explicitly excludes the 0xB0 case from its "is this an unusually high warp value" test — confirming 0xB0 is treated as a distinct case throughout, not a large ordinary number. Nibble values 12-15 (0xC0-0xF0) were not observed being produced or checked anywhere and appear unused.

**Warp 0 is a dedicated "hold position" order, not simply the `N²` formula naturally producing zero distance.** The per-turn movement sweep's very first check on a fleet's next waypoint tests whether that waypoint's stored warp nibble is exactly 0 and, if so, skips all of that fleet's movement/arrival processing for the turn outright (the same early-exit branch that also skips fleets on a Transport or Lay-Mine-Field task, both of which are expected to sit still — see §5 below). A fleet can therefore be explicitly ordered to stay put for a turn (e.g. to hold at a waypoint without immediately continuing to the next one), distinct from simply lacking any further waypoints.

### 2. Fuel consumption

**Consumption formula.** A widely-cited community derivation (originally posted to the `rec.games.computer.stars` newsgroup and preserved in the starsfaq.com "Guts" reference) states the baseline rule as: 1 milligram (mg) of fuel moves 200 kilotons (kT) of ship mass 1 light-year, when the relevant engine/speed combination's "Fuel Usage Number" (FUN — a per-engine, per-warp table constant printed in the game's own Guts-of-Engines reference) equals 100. Every other FUN value simply scales this proportionally. In formula form:

- Fuel used per light-year (mg) = (total fleet mass in kT / 200) x (FUN / 100) x the efficiency multiplier.
- The efficiency multiplier is 0.85 if the race has Improved Fuel Efficiency, otherwise 1.0.
- Total fuel for a trip (mg) = fuel per light-year x light-years traveled.

A second, more granular formula is documented by the *Planets.nu* fan reimplementation (a separate, actively-maintained clone of *Stars!*'s ruleset, not the original game):

- Fuel used = FUN x (fleet mass in kT / 10, truncated) x (distance in ly, truncated) / (maximum distance per turn) / 10,000, with the whole result truncated to an integer.
- Maximum distance per turn = warp factor squared.

This integer-truncating version is algebraically the same relationship rescaled and rounded at different points, and is presented here as a plausible closer match to the original fixed-point engine's actual rounding behavior — but since it comes from a compatible clone rather than the original game, it should be treated as a secondary cross-check rather than a confirmed identical implementation (see Open Questions).

**Confirmed formula, resolved by inspection of the exported client.** The actual per-fleet fuel-cost calculator is `FUN_1050_6510` (`stars.exe.export.c:35638`–`35854`), a previously-uncharacterized segment-11 routine — **not** `FUN_10b0_312a`, which earlier passes had flagged as the likely home of this arithmetic and which is now confirmed (see below) to be unrelated to fuel entirely. The calculation takes a fleet, a warp speed (or "use the warp stored on the fleet's current waypoint", per §1's high-nibble convention), a distance (or "compute the actual remaining distance to the next waypoint", using the shared coordinate-delta helper `FUN_1038_2924`), and a mode (usage or reference). Concretely, for every occupied design slot in the fleet it:

1. Looks up that design's mounted engine (engine records via `FUN_1008_513e`) and reads the engine's per-warp fuel-table entry for the chosen warp (one 16-bit entry per warp 0-10, record bytes 54-74) — the exact field this project's `extracted-game-data/component-stats.tsv` already tabulates for every engine (`stars.exe.export.c:35712`-`35713`).
2. **Applies Improved Fuel Efficiency directly to that per-warp table value**, not as a separate final multiplier: when the owning race has lesser-trait bit 0 (read through the generic per-race lesser-trait test `FUN_10e0_226e`), the value is reduced by 15% of itself, rounded down (so the kept value is the table value minus the truncated 15% share), i.e. multiplied by 0.85 (`stars.exe.export.c:35714`-`35723`) — this selector is identified as IFE by its exact behavioral match to IFE's documented −15% fuel effect, not by an independently cross-referenced trait-index table entry. *(Since confirmed by direct code: the race wizard binds lesser-trait checkbox 0, labelled Improved Fuel Efficiency, to bit 0. See `race-traits.md` §3.)*
3. Groups design slots that share an identical (IFE-adjusted) per-warp value into buckets (so multiple ship designs using the same engine/warp combination are combined), and for each bucket sums, over the bucket's design slots, the design's mass times its ship count — the mass term reads each design's own cached hull-mass field at design-record-relative offset `0x28` (`stars.exe.export.c:35794`). This is the **universal 16-category record field `+40`/`0x28` = "Mass (kT)"** that `ship-design-and-components.md` §15b establishes applies to every component/hull record, cached into the owning design's own record at the same relative offset — not the *different*, weaker, explicitly-hedged ("not code-execution-confirmed") guess in that same document's §13 that this identical offset holds a cached cargo-capacity value; §13's guess is superseded by its own later §15b/§15e finding that hull cargo capacity actually lives at `+52`, with base armor at `+56`. This cross-file inconsistency in `ship-design-and-components.md` is worth a follow-up correction pass there, but does not weaken the mass identification used here.
4. Computes the bucket's fuel as (IFE-adjusted per-warp value x distance x bucket mass) / 2000, using a scaled multiply-divide helper (`FUN_1120_0e40`) when the product would be too large for plain arithmetic (`stars.exe.export.c:35792`-`35810`) — this is a precision/overflow accommodation, not a second formula.
5. Sums the bucket figures across all buckets, and — for the usage mode specifically — adds 9 and then divides by 10 (integer), i.e. rounds the tenths up (`stars.exe.export.c:35827`-`35830`).

**Net result: fuel used = the sum over buckets of (IFE-adjusted per-warp value x distance in ly x mass in kT), divided by 20,000** (the divide-by-2000 then divide-by-10 compose into 20,000), rounded up as above — algebraically identical to (mass / 200) x (FUN / 100) x the IFE multiplier x distance, i.e. **this confirms the starsfaq.com-derivation shape (§2's first formula above), not the Planets.nu-derivation shape**: nowhere in this divisor chain does the maximum distance per turn (warp squared) appear as a divisor — the warp-squared distance cap is a wholly separate computation performed upstream, in the caller, to decide how far the fleet travels this turn in the first place (see below), not folded into the per-light-year fuel-rate formula itself the way Planets.nu's documented formula folds it in. This also resolves the version-dependence concern (Open Questions, "Fuel-generation table's patch-version dependence"): a clean-room implementation should simply use this exact binary's own `component-stats.tsv`-recovered per-warp values (confirmed to be exactly what this code reads), sidestepping the question of which wiki/starsfaq snapshot's table matches this particular build.

**Caller-side confirmation of fuel deduction and the fuel-exhaustion rule, resolved by inspection of the exported client.** `FUN_10b0_1f8c` (already documented above as the Stargate/waypoint-task caller) calls `FUN_1050_6510` multiple times per fleet, per turn, around the same `stars.exe.export.c:73400`-`73650` region already cited for the §1 warp² distance clamp:

- First, once in reference mode (a fixed reference distance of 1000) and once in usage mode with the *full* remaining distance to the next waypoint (`stars.exe.export.c:73422`-`73424`), comparing the combined result against the fleet's currently-stored fuel quantity (the fleet record's wide fuel field, cited in earlier passes as offset `0x2e`/`0x2f`) to decide whether the fleet can afford its whole remaining trip (`stars.exe.export.c:73428`-`73434`).
- **If the fleet has enough fuel for the full remaining distance, the ordinary `N²`-warp distance cap (§1) is applied as normal** (`stars.exe.export.c:73436`-`73446`) and the fleet travels as far as its ordered warp speed allows this turn. **If it does not, that clamp is skipped**, leaving the smaller, fuel-derived distance value already computed in place — i.e. **an underfueled fleet's travel this turn is capped to the distance its remaining fuel can actually pay for, rather than completing the full ordered-speed hop and only afterward clamping fuel to zero.** This resolves the "silently completes a partial hop vs. downgrades to an affordable speed" open question in favor of the partial-hop reading. *The sub-formula, traced (`FUN_1050_6510`, `:35639`-`35854`, its tail confirmed in raw segment 11 bytes).* The fuel routine has a reach mode: it prices a reference leg of exactly 1,000 light years at the ordered warp with the fleet's current mass, without the round-up of the normal mode, then returns the fleet's fuel times 1,000 divided by that price, i.e. fuel aboard divided by the cost per light year, kept to a thousandth of a light year by the reference length (when the reference price exceeds 100,000 mg the division is done the other way round, price divided by 1,000, to avoid overflow). A fleet whose engines burn nothing at that warp gets a reach of one billion light years, in effect unlimited. The movement pass (`:73422`-`73446`, `:73573`-`73624`) asks for the reach and for the exact fuel the whole remaining leg would cost; when the fuel aboard covers the whole leg the reach is replaced by the larger of itself and the warp-squared yearly distance, so it never limits the hop; otherwise the reach stands. The year's travel is then the smallest of the warp-squared distance (or the remaining yearly budget for a fleet that already moved this year), the distance to the waypoint, and the reach; when the reach is the smallest the fleet moves exactly that far and its fuel is set to zero, otherwise the fuel for the distance actually moved is deducted (never below zero). A fleet left with zero fuel then gets message 39 (out of fuel), or 139 with its waypoint warp lowered to the slowest warp from 1 up at which the leg costs nothing, when such a warp exists (`:73649`-`73678`).
- Once the turn's actual travel distance is finalized, `FUN_1050_6510` is called again in usage mode with that actual distance (`stars.exe.export.c:73630`), and its result is **subtracted directly from the fleet's stored fuel field**, clamped to zero rather than allowed to go negative (`stars.exe.export.c:73639`-`73647`) — confirming fuel is deducted turn-by-turn by direct subtraction of a freshly-recomputed cost, not by consulting a precomputed range.

**`FUN_10b0_312a` itself is confirmed to have no role in fuel consumption at all.** A full line-by-line read of its ordinary-movement call path (its other caller is the detonating-minefield sweep; it has no Stargate path) (`stars.exe.export.c:74080`-`74701`) — including the design-mass-accumulation-shaped loop at `74297`-`74316` and `74404`-`74476` that an earlier pass had flagged as a promising fuel-formula candidate — shows that loop instead computes a **total fleet ship-count** (used to gate a minefield-hit adjustment term) and a **per-design mass/armor-quantization figure** consumed by mine-hit ship-loss arithmetic (the division by 500 at `stars.exe.export.c:74449`-`74451` matches this project's already-confirmed 1/500 armor-quantization scheme from `combat-resolution.md`, not a fuel divisor). The function's warp-nibble-indexed table lookups at `74376`-`74382` index into fixed mine-damage-constant tables by a minefield-tier/type bucket selector, not by the fleet's ordered warp speed. Every code path in this function's non-Stargate call is minefield-transit hit-chance, damage, and ship-loss arithmetic, exactly as the earlier pass's "dominated by minefield-transit arithmetic" characterization already concluded — this pass extends that from a hedge to a confirmed negative result specifically for the fuel question, now that the real fuel calculator (`FUN_1050_6510`) has been found elsewhere.

**Fuel generation (ramscoops).** Below its rated "free" warp threshold, a ramscoop-type engine produces fuel instead of consuming it, and every engine (ramscoop or not) produces a small amount of fuel at warp 1 specifically. Per-engine fuel generated over a distance `D` (at full-throttle travel for that speed) follows a step pattern relative to how far below the engine's free-travel speed it is running:

| Speed relative to the engine's free-travel warp | Fuel generated over distance D |
|---|---|
| Above the free-travel warp | 0 (fuel is being consumed, not generated) |
| Exactly at the free-travel warp | D |
| 1 warp factor below | 3 x D |
| 2 warp factors below | 6 x D |
| 3 or more warp factors below | 10 x D |

This is per engine, not per ship — a hull with 2, 3, or 4 engines generates (or burns) proportionally more fuel. The Stars!wiki's own generation table (reproduced below, in mg, current as of the "JRC4" game patch — the wiki explicitly warns older patches had different, buggier values) shows every conventional (non-scoop) engine generating exactly 1 mg at warp 1, which is the mechanical basis for treating warp 1 as an always-available, self-sustaining crawl speed regardless of remaining fuel:

| Quick Jump 5 / Long Hump 6 / Daddy Long Legs 7 / Alpha Drive 8 / Trans-Galactic Drive / Interspace-10 / Trans-Star 10 | - | - | - | - | - | - | - | - | 1 |

**Improved Fuel Efficiency (IFE)** lesser racial trait reduces all fuel consumption by 15% across every engine, and unlocks the Fuel Mizer, Trans-Galactic Mizer Scoop, and Galaxy Scoop engines.

**Radiation hazard of the Radiating Hydro-Ram Scoop, traced in full (`FUN_10b0_1f8c`, `:73979`-`74004`; raw segment 23 `0x2e5a`-`0x2f62`; engine test in `FUN_1050_6510`, raw segment 11 `0x651d` and `0x6663`-`0x666c`).** Code-confirmed.
- *Which fleets.* The fuel routine `FUN_1050_6510` clears a "radiating engine" mark (DS `0x078a` bit `0x40`) on entry and sets it when any design in the fleet that has ships mounts engine subtype 10, the Radiating Hydro-Ram Scoop. The movement sweep calls that routine for every fleet that actually starts an engine leg this pass, and explicitly clears the mark on the Stargate branch (raw `0x210a`). So the loss applies only to a fleet that moves under its own engines: a fleet with no further waypoint, a warp-0 hold, a Transport or Lay Mines task, a refused or balky move, a Stargate jump, a fleet that has no usable movement left this pass, a fleet destroyed during the move, and every fleet not moving at all take no loss. One ship with the scoop exposes all the fleet's colonists, whatever engines the other ships have. Warp, distance and fuel do not matter.
- *Exemption.* With C = (low end + high end of the race's Radiation band) ÷ 2, integer division (race record bytes `+0x15` and `+0x18`; the stored band centre is not used), there is no loss when the race is radiation-immune (high end stored as 255) or when C is 85 or more (the code tests low + high below 170).
- *Rate.* Otherwise the year's loss is P = (86 − C) ÷ 2 percent, rounded down (so 1% at C = 84, 18% at C = 50, 35% at C = 15, 43% at C = 0). The loss in cargo units (hundreds of colonists, fleet offset `0x58`) is the colonist cargo × P ÷ 100, rounded down, **at least 1 unit** (100 colonists) whenever any colonists are aboard, and never more than are aboard. Message 116 tells the owner how many colonists engine radiation killed in the named fleet, and the units are subtracted.
- *Timing.* It is applied at the end of the fleet's movement processing (after the position update and any minefield hit), so the arrival leg counts too. It runs in movement passes 0 and 1 only (the pass counter is tested below 2). A fleet moves in a later pass only when it pursues a fleet that has not finished moving; such a fleet that moved part of its distance in pass 0 and the rest in pass 1 is irradiated twice that year, and one that first moves in pass 2 or later is not irradiated. Every other moving fleet is irradiated exactly once a year.
- Example: a fleet carrying 500 units (50,000 colonists) for a race with Radiation band 15-85 (C = 50) loses 500 × 18 ÷ 100 = 90 units (9,000 colonists) each year it travels; 3 units aboard lose 1 unit, not 0.

**Running out of fuel.** Fleet fuel is depleted during the normal movement phase of turn processing, alongside minefield hits, stargate jumps, and wormhole transits. Because every known engine generates a small positive amount of fuel at warp 1 (see table above), a fleet that exhausts its fuel reserve is not permanently stranded — warp 1 remains available indefinitely as a self-sustaining minimum speed. **The precise turn-by-turn behavior when a fleet is ordered to a speed its remaining fuel cannot fully pay for is now resolved** — see the "Caller-side confirmation of fuel deduction and the fuel-exhaustion rule" note above: the fleet's travel distance for the turn is capped to whatever its remaining fuel can afford (a silent partial hop), rather than completing the full ordered-speed jump and only then clamping fuel to zero.

### 3. Scanning and detection

*Stars!* draws a hard line between two scanner tiers:

- **Standard (non-penetrating) scanners** only reveal a fleet or planet's presence and basic identity while a scan-equipped ship or planet is within range of it; they cannot read a distant, un-visited planet's mineral concentration, population, or defenses.
- **Penetrating scanners** additionally reveal a planet's full attributes (and any ships/starbase in orbit there) from range, without ever having to physically visit or orbit it — the effective coverage area is shown as a distinct overlay in the game's Scanner Pane. Penetrating scanner technology first becomes available at Electronics tech level 10; a homeworld's very first ordinary planetary scanner is meaningfully weaker (roughly 150 ly at Electronics tech 3).

**No Advanced Scanners (NAS)** is a lesser racial trait that trades away all penetrating-scanner technology in exchange for doubling the range of every conventional scanner the race builds (e.g., a race's Electronics-tech-3 homeworld scanner reaches 300 ly instead of 150 ly under NAS; the top-tier "Snooper 620X" scanner reaches roughly double its listed range under NAS as well). Some racial abilities keep a narrow slice of penetrating capability even under NAS — e.g., a Jack of All Trades race's built-in Scout/Frigate/Destroyer scanner, a Packet Physics race's mineral-packet scanner, or a Super-Stealth race's Chameleon/Robber Baron components — because those are treated as inherent racial abilities rather than the generic "planet penetrating scanner" technology NAS disables.

**Combining multiple scanners on one design.** When a single ship or starbase design carries more than one scanner component, its effective range is not a simple maximum or sum — it is computed as the fourth root of the sum of each individual scanner's range raised to the fourth power: raise each scanner's range to the fourth power, add those together, and take the fourth root of the total.

For example, a design carrying two 100-ly scanners and one 60-ly scanner has a combined range equal to the fourth root of (100 to the fourth + 100 to the fourth + 60 to the fourth), about 120 ly — noticeably more than any single component alone, but far short of a simple sum. The same combination rule is described as applying to penetrating scanners as well.

**Design and fleet scanner ranges, exact (`FUN_1038_337e` `:19841`, fleet wrapper `FUN_1038_32be` `:19787`; raw segment 8 `0x337e`-`0x3733`, with the floating-point constants read from DGROUP `0x1c82`-`0x1ca2`).** Code-confirmed. A design has two ranges, normal and penetrating, each built as a sum of fourth powers in floating point:
- *Scanner parts* (category `0x0002`), per installed slot with a non-zero quantity q: the normal sum gains q × (listed range)⁴. The penetrating sum gains q × P⁴, where P comes from the part's penetration class: class 0 adds nothing; class 1 gives 50 ly (Ferret), class 2 100 ly (Dolphin), class 3 or more 200 ly (Elephant). Three subtypes override the class: Chameleon 45 ly, Robber Baron 120 ly, Pick Pocket 0 ly. So the ship scanners' (normal / penetrating) ranges are: Bat 0 / –, Rhino 50 / –, Mole 100 / –, DNA 125 / –, Possum 150 / –, Pick Pocket 80 / 0, Chameleon 160 / 45, Ferret 185 / 50, Dolphin 220 / 100, Gazelle 225 / –, RNA 230 / –, Cheetah 275 / –, Elephant 300 / 200, Eagle Eye 335 / –, Robber Baron 220 / 120, Peerless 500 / –.
- *Three non-scanner parts* also scan, per unit: Mega Poly Shell 80 / 40, Multi Contained Munition 150 / 75, Langston Shell 50 / 25.
- *Jack of All Trades built-in scanner.* For a race whose primary trait is JOAT, a design on the Scout, Frigate or Destroyer hull (hull indices 4, 5, 6) starts its sums at (20 × E)⁴ normal and (10 × E)⁴ penetrating, E being the race's current Electronics level. It is one extra term in the same sums, so it combines with installed scanners by the fourth-power rule like any part. In the tutorial game (game-option bit `0x08`) the terms are fixed at 40 ly and 20 ly instead.
- *Result.* If the normal sum is not above 0 and the design has no scanner part, the design has no scanner (range −1). Otherwise the normal range is the fourth root of the sum (two square roots), truncated to an integer, and then **doubled** for a race with No Advanced Scanners (lesser trait 10). The doubling applies to the whole combined normal range, the JOAT term included. The penetrating range is the fourth root of its sum, truncated, and is never doubled; No Advanced Scanners does not remove the JOAT penetrating term or any part's penetrating figure here (it acts only through part availability).
- *Fleet.* A fleet's normal and penetrating ranges are the largest of its designs' values among designs that have ships; ranges do not combine across designs or ship counts. The theft bits (§4, "Theft") are OR-ed across the same designs.
- Example: a JOAT race at Electronics 8 has a bare Scout with 160 ly normal and 80 ly penetrating; with a Rhino Scanner the normal range becomes the fourth root of 160⁴ + 50⁴, which is 160.6, truncated to 160; under No Advanced Scanners it is 320, and the penetrating range stays 80.

**Cloaking vs. scanning.** A cloak reduces the effective range at which a scanner can detect the cloaked fleet by the cloak's percentage — e.g., an 80%-cloaked fleet is only detectable once an observer is within 20% of that scanner's normal rated range (so a 150-ly scanner would only spot it inside 30 ly). Because planetary scanners are stationary, an opponent who knows (or conservatively estimates) their range can plan routes that stay just outside it; mobile scout ships are correspondingly more effective at catching cloaked fleets specifically because their position is not predictable turn to turn. Minefields belonging to a Space Demolition race act as an additional, distinct detection layer against cloaked fleets that enter them: the chance of detecting a cloaked fleet inside such a minefield in a given year is `100% − cloak%` (e.g., an 80%-cloaked fleet is spotted 20% of the time per year it sits in the minefield), independent of ordinary scanner range.

**Packet Physics** races get a race-specific scanning shortcut: every mineral packet they fling carries a built-in penetrating scanner whose range equals the square of the packet's own travel warp speed, letting them "scan ahead" of their own mineral shipments.

**Scanning formula, verified against the exported client.** The turn-generation engine's actual detection test (run once per turn for every fleet-vs-fleet and planet-vs-fleet pair) is "the squared distance is at most the squared scan range", with the scanning side's effective range multiplied by (100 minus the target's cloak percentage) / 100 before squaring — consistent with, and a more precise restatement of, this document's "effective range reduced by cloak%" claim above. Separate range lookups exist for a fleet's own scan range versus a planet's, though both use the same distance-squared test. ~~**Minefield detection** is confirmed to use a distinct formula from ordinary scanning: the minefield's stored warning/size value plus 4, squared, as a flat detection radius — independent of the cloak-based scanner test. **Wormhole (and similar unusual-object) detection** is confirmed to be genuinely probabilistic rather than a deterministic range check: a flat radius test is combined with a random roll (0-99) against the observed object's cloak percentage, meaning a wormhole or similar object can go undetected even at close range on any given turn.~~ **Corrected (Route pass): both sentences describe other rules.** The "stored value plus 4, squared" is the **Packet Physics packet scanner**: a mass packet's speed field (bits 2-5 of its byte `+7`) plus 4 is its warp, and the warp squared is its scan range (`FUN_1070_6d7c` `:49900`-`49911`). The 0-99 roll against cloak is the **Space Demolition minefield** test of fleets: for each minefield of an SD race, every fleet not in orbit that lies within the field's radius is detected unless a 0-99 roll is below its cloak percentage (`:50022`-`50057`). Neither replaces nor adds to a scanner's range.

**Minefield detection, complete rule (Route pass; `FUN_1070_5f00` `:49384`-`49425`, `FUN_1070_64fc` `:49655`-`49705`, `FUN_1070_6d7c` `:49943`-`49993`).** A field is a circle of radius √(mine count) around its centre. Each field keeps two per-race masks: *known* (word `+10`) and *seen this generation* (word `+14`, cleared every year by step 14). A field the race has not yet seen this generation is detected:
- by a **ship's** scanners, with normal range r and penetrating range p measured to the field's centre, when (a) the race already knows the field and the centre is within r, or (b) the centre is within p, or (c) the centre is within r/4, or (d) the ship's fleet is inside the field (squared distance to the centre at most the mine count). Test (d) does not depend on the scanner at all, so a fleet without any scanner still sees a field it is inside;
- by a **planet's** scanners only when the centre is within r and (a), (b) or (c) holds; there is no inside-the-field test;
- by a Packet Physics **packet** when the centre is within the packet's range.
Detection sets both masks. A mine hit also marks the field known to the hit fleet's race (`:74690`-`74692`, both masks), and a sweep marks it known, but not seen, to the sweeper's race (`:79773`, `:79855`; `turn-generation-engine.md` §11, mine sweeping, "Effects", which lists every reader and writer of the known mask). Nothing clears the known mask. There is no roll and the field has no cloak percentage; test (c) is the same quarter-range rule that cloaks wormholes, so an unknown field behaves as 75% cloaked and a known one is seen at full normal range. The multi-scanner combination (fourth-root) formula and the per-part scanner ranges have since been read from the executable; see "Design and fleet scanner ranges, exact" above.

**Starbase cloaking in the planet sweeps, confirmed by inspection of the exported client.** A starbase design carries its own cloak percentage, computed once a turn in turn-generation step 36 (`turn-generation-engine.md` §1 step 36): 20% built in for a race with Improved Starbases, 75% for Super Stealth, plus whatever its installed components add, capped at 98% (strictly, these are cloak points, 40 and 300, summed with the components' points before the piecewise curve of `combat-resolution.md` §11 turns the total into a percentage, so the percentages do not simply add). The stored figure is (100 minus the cloak percentage), squared. When a scanner's range already reaches a planet that has an owned starbase, the planet is reported at full detail, starbase included, only if the squared distance is at most that figure times the squared range divided by 10,000, i.e. within range x (100 - cloak%)/100; otherwise the planet is seen but its starbase stays hidden. Ships, planetary scanners and Packet Physics mass-packet scanners all apply this same test (`FUN_1070_5f00`, `FUN_1070_64fc`, `FUN_1070_6d7c`). Unlike a cloaked fleet, a starbase is not subject to the observer's Tachyon Detector counter-cloak multiplier.

**Mineral packets: never cloaked; who sees them (code pass; the per-player visibility passes `FUN_1070_5aa0` `:49218`-`49255`, `FUN_1070_5f00` `:49380`-`49425`, `FUN_1070_64fc` `:49640`-`49711`, `FUN_1070_6d7c` `:49899`-`50011`, and the turn-file writer `:47672`-`47709`).** A packet has no cloak value and no counter-cloak or penetration rule applies to it. For each player, every packet first starts unseen, and is then marked seen when:
- the player's PRT is Packet Physics: every packet in the galaxy, every turn, wherever it is and whoever sent it;
- otherwise, the packet lies within the **normal** (non-penetrating) scanner range of one of the player's fleets that is not destroyed, or of one of the player's planets (its planet scanning range), by the usual test, squared distance at most the squared range, boundary included; a fleet with range 0 sees only a packet at its own position. Penetrating range plays no part.
The turn file then carries exactly the packets marked seen. **This includes the player's own packets**: unlike the player's minefields, an own packet is not written automatically, so a non-Packet-Physics race does not see its own packet once it has flown out of all its scanner ranges (it reappears when it comes back into range, for example near the target if the target is the race's own planet). A Packet Physics race's own packets also act as scanners (the packet scanner above: range equal to the packet's warp squared, in light-years, applied to fleets with the cloak test, to other special objects and to planets; only packets flying faster than warp 4 scan).

### 4. Cargo capacity and cargo transfer

Every hull has a fixed base cargo capacity in kilotons (kT), shared by minerals, colonists, and (on most hulls) fuel is tracked as a *separate* pool from cargo rather than counting against it. Cargo-pod components can be mounted in eligible slots to add further capacity. Representative base cargo capacities documented for stock hulls:

| Hull | Base cargo (kT) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Small Freighter | 70 | |
| Medium Freighter | 210 | requires Construction tech 3 |
| Large Freighter | 1200 | requires Construction tech 8 |
| Super Freighter | 3000 | requires Construction tech 13; Inner Strength race only |
| Privateer | 250 | requires Construction tech 4 |
| Rogue | 500 | requires Construction tech 8; Super-Stealth race only |
| Galleon | 1000 | requires Construction tech 11 |
| Meta Morph | 300 | requires Construction tech 10; Hyper-Expansion race only |
| Mini-Colony Ship | 10 | Hyper-Expansion race only |

Fuel-tanker hulls are a separate special case: the Fuel Transport (750 mg fuel capacity) and Super-Fuel Xport (2250 mg) hulls actively manufacture roughly 200 mg of fuel per year while deployed and additionally raise the healing rate of other ships sharing their fleet.

**Loading and unloading.** Cargo movement is configured per-waypoint as a task, with options including loading/unloading all of a mineral type, loading/unloading a fixed amount, and a conditional form — informally "Set Waypoint to: `<amount>`" — that only loads (or only unloads) as much as needed to bring the source (or destination)'s on-hand quantity up to (or down to) a target level, rather than blindly moving everything available. This conditional form is the documented way to run a fully automated repeat-route between two planets (e.g., a supply run and a mineral-balancing run) without the ship ever over-draining either endpoint. Colonist cargo can also be loaded/unloaded the same way as minerals; Inner Strength race colonists additionally continue to reproduce at a reduced rate while riding in a fleet's cargo hold, spilling any surplus onto a planet the fleet is orbiting.

**Conditional transfers, traced in full: there is no chunk size and no divisor (this replaces an earlier "confirmed formula").** An earlier version of this paragraph described a percent-of-target value over four slots, action on the least complete slot and transfer in bounded chunks, plus a per-race 100-or-25 chunk divisor. Both descriptions came from the production purchase routine `FUN_10b8_0756` (`stars.exe.export.c:76778`-`76840`), not from cargo code. There the four slots are a queue item's three minerals and its resources, and 100-or-25 is the Mineral Alchemy price. The Transport task handler (`FUN_10b0_3f3a`, `:74822`-`75483` and `:76248`-`76281`) has no chunk size, no divisor and no per-race, per-trait or per-quantity step. Each instruction moves its whole computed amount at once, in whole units, through the generic cargo accessor `FUN_1050_3cee`. The algorithm:

- *Passes.* The handler runs four times a turn (`turn-generation-engine.md` §1: twice before movement, twice after the battle). The first and third runs are unload passes and the second and fourth are load passes (`:74948`). Unload instructions act only in unload passes and load instructions only in load passes. The two "set to" instructions act in whichever pass matches the sign of the difference. **What the four passes produce (Route pass).** Each pass walks every fleet, so within each pair all fleets unload before any fleet loads, whatever the fleet order. A fleet reaching a Transport waypoint during movement is served by passes 3 and 4 that same year, after the year's production and the battle, with colonisation and invasion resolved between its unload and its load. A pending task is served again by passes 1 and 2 of each later year, before movement; a fleet whose waypoint 0 still carries Transport does not move (`FUN_10b0_1f8c` `:73378`-`73380`), but one whose task completes in pass 2 has its task cleared and moves in the same year's movement. If still pending it is served once more by passes 3 and 4. So a fleet gets at most two unload-then-load rounds a year, one before movement and one after the battle. A clone running one unload and one load round "per arrival" matches this only if it also (a) performs all fleets' unloads of a round before any fleet's loads, (b) gives a still-pending task a round before movement each later year and lets a task that completes there depart that year, and (c) keeps the post-battle round's different outcomes: there a blocked load ends the task with a message instead of waiting (`:75080`-`75099`), and a Set amount to N that finds too little at the source posts message 289 or 290 while the task stays pending (`:75314`-`75326`); in the pre-movement round both just wait silently.
- *Slots and order.* Five slots are handled in a fixed order: ironium, boranium, germanium, colonists, fuel. Each slot has an instruction code and a 12-bit amount (0 to 4095) in that slot's own units: kT for minerals and colonists, mg for fuel. Free space is recomputed after every slot, so earlier slots get first claim on the shared hold.
- *Instruction codes* (Route pass, the two switches at `:74998`-`75055` and `:75056`-`75353`). The five slot words are waypoint bytes 8-17, one 16-bit word per slot in the order above: the top 4 bits are the code and the low 12 bits the amount N. The codes are the dialog's list positions; dynamic strings 109-118 hold the ten labels in code order and 119 is the fuel-slot label of code 7:

  | Code | Instruction | Acts in |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0 | No action | never |
  | 1 | Load All | load passes |
  | 2 | Unload All | unload passes |
  | 3 | Load exactly N | load passes |
  | 4 | Unload exactly N | unload passes |
  | 5 | Fill up to N% | load passes |
  | 6 | Wait for N% | load passes |
  | 7 | Load Dunnage (cargo slots) / Load Optimal (fuel slot) | load passes |
  | 8 | Set amount to N | load pass if the fleet has less than N, unload pass if more |
  | 9 | Set waypoint to N | load pass if the source has more than N, unload pass if less |

  Codes 10-15 are never produced and do nothing.
- *Amounts* (`:74998`-`75055`, `:75302`-`75352`):
  - Load all: everything the source has.
  - Unload all: everything the fleet carries in that slot.
  - Load or unload exactly N: N. An unload never exceeds what the fleet carries.
  - Fill up to N% and Wait for N%: N percent of the fleet's capacity, rounded down (the whole cargo hold for the four cargo slots, the tank for fuel), minus what the fleet already carries in that slot, never below zero. This is the handler's only rounding step. *(Exact, Route pass, `:75032`-`75054`: the capacity is first clamped to 2,000,000; below 65,536 the figure is N x capacity / 100, truncated, and from 65,536 up it is N x (capacity / 100), the division truncated first.)*
  - Set amount to N: N minus the fleet's amount. A shortfall is loaded and an excess is unloaded.
  - Set waypoint to N: the source's amount minus N. A surplus above N is loaded. A deficit is unloaded, but never more than the fleet carries.
- *Caps on a load.* The amount is cut to the free space (hold capacity minus the four cargo slots' total, or tank capacity minus fuel), then to what the source holds. The accessor never takes a source below zero (`:75064`-`75128`; `FUN_1050_3cee`, `:33394`-`33540`). Planets have no fuel slot. Taking cargo from another race's planet or fleet works only when the fleet has the cargo-theft scanner ability (the scanner-class result of `FUN_1038_32be`: either theft scanner for a fleet, a Robber Baron for a planet; see "Theft" below). Otherwise a load from there, or from deep space, is blocked. In the pre-movement load pass a blocked load just leaves the task pending. In the post-battle load pass the owner is told and the task ends (`:75080`-`75099`). An unload into another fleet is cut to that fleet's free space.
- *Load dunnage* (code 7 on a cargo slot). It does nothing in the first sweep. If every other condition was met and the hold still has room afterwards, a second sweep runs over the dunnage slots only. Each loads as much as the source has and the remaining space allows, in slot order (`:74967`, `:75292`-`75299`, `:75466`-`75471`).
- *Load optimal* (code 7 on the fuel slot). After the sweep the fleet keeps only the fuel its remaining route needs and hands the excess to the target. The need is recomputed until it stops changing, because shedding fuel lowers mass. With no further waypoint, all fuel is handed over. This code never takes fuel in. If the route needs more than the fleet carries, the owner gets a warning instead (`:75362`-`75453`). **Where the excess goes (Route pass).** It is offered to the waypoint's target through the generic accessor, which accepts fuel only into a fleet, up to that fleet's free tank space (`FUN_1050_3cee`, `:33429`-`33431` for planets and deep space, `:33513`-`33528` for fleets). A planet or a deep-space point accepts none, so at a planet Load Optimal hands nothing over and the fleet keeps all its fuel; at a fleet, whatever does not fit stays aboard. The one exception is the substitution at an unowned planet: when the fleet cannot take cargo from the planet and another of the owner's fleets orbiting there carries the not-moved flag (fleet byte 5 bit `0x20`, which the movement pass leaves set on fleets that did not move) and has remote-mining capacity, that fleet stands in as the target for the pass (`:74863`-`74886`, restored at `:76250`-`76253`), and the excess fuel goes to it.
- *Completion.* The task ends in a load pass where every load was allowed, every Set amount to N found enough at the source, and every Wait for N% reached its level or filled the hold. The waypoint's task is then cleared (`:76269`-`76280`). Otherwise the waypoint's orders are written back, the fleet stays, and it tries again in the next pass. Instructions already carried out in an unload pass are struck off when written back (`:75274`-`75275`, `:76249`-`76263`). A Load exactly N that finds less loads what there is and counts as done.
- *Colonists* move one-to-one with no conversion. The handler's only race test is on unloading colonists onto another race's planet. That case goes to the invasion code, or is refused for an Alternate Reality race or when the planet has a starbase (`:75202`-`75218`). Colonists cannot be unloaded into another race's fleet or into deep space.

**Colonist unload outcomes, complete table (gap-report pass).** Colonists reach a planet that is not the fleet owner's by two routes. (1) A waypoint **Unload** task, judged by the Transport handler at the time of the pass (`FUN_10b0_3f3a`, `:75200`-`75218`). (2) An immediate move in the **Transfer dialog**. The host replays it in step 6, takes the colonists off the fleet at once and files them in the pending-landing ledger (`:29776`-`29834`), with the colonise bit set only if the planet has an owner. Both routes end in the landing pass `FUN_10b8_1ea6` (`turn-generation-engine.md` §11, `:77756`-`77767`), which runs right after the task pass that filed the record; Transfer-dialog records are settled in step 12d. No route ever asks about diplomatic relations: unloading onto a friend's planet is an invasion like any other.

| Planet | Starbase | Other race, Unload task | Alternate Reality, Unload task | Other race, Transfer dialog | Alternate Reality, Transfer dialog |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Own | any | Ordinary transfer: added to the population at once | Same | Same | Same |
| Another race's | no | Filed in the ledger, then **invasion** in the landing pass (strength 110%, War Monger 165%, against the population, doubled for Inner Strength; messages 0/1 and 3/4 on failure, 12, 13 and 7 on success) | **Refused at the handler**, message 86; colonists stay aboard | Invasion, as in the first column | **Destroyed** in the landing pass, message 87 |

Notes.
- **Nobody colonises by unloading.** An unowned planet is taken only by a **Colonize** order (task 2, a fleet with a Colonization or Orbital Construction Module, §5 "Colonize resolution"). Message 82 belongs to that order: it is the cancellation when the Colonize target has any owner, the colonising race included. Message 86 is the handler's refusal of an Alternate Reality Unload task onto a planet not its own. Message 87 is the landing pass destroying Alternate Reality colonists, and it is reached through Transfer-dialog drops.
- **Edge case.** Each task pass starts by refreshing a planet's "has an owner" flag (planet word bit `0x2000`, `:72464`-`72480`). A planet that loses its owner later in the same pass still carries the flag. The Unload handler then treats it as another race's planet, so it files a ledger record with the colonise bit set, and the landing pass settles it as a colonisation (message 10, contest rules). An Alternate Reality fleet gets message 86 instead.
- A refused Unload leaves the colonists aboard, posts the message and stops that fleet's sweep for the pass.
- **Client side of the Transfer-dialog route (resolved, code-only; the last two columns are reachable from the shipped client).**
  - *Where the dialog is offered* (`FUN_1050_326a`, `:32812`-`33034`). It has two sides. From the selected fleet (its cargo button, `FUN_1050_1b6e`, raw segment 11 `0x1d16`-`0x1d3c`, or the same command on the fleet pop-up menu, `:111989`-`111999`), side 0 is the fleet. Side 1 is the planet the fleet orbits, **whoever owns it**: the player's own, another race's, or an unowned world. Only the fleet's orbit index is tested. In deep space side 1 is open space instead (jettison, `0x57ac`-`0x57b8`). From the planet panel's list of objects in orbit (`:24981`-`24987`), side 0 is the selected planet and side 1 the chosen fleet or salvage. The planet panel only ever holds one of the player's own planets, because every path that selects a planet into it requires the planet's owner to be the current player (`:26176`). No route reaches a planet the fleet does not orbit.
  - *What the dialog enforces* (`FUN_1050_38e4` `:33226`-`33233`, `FUN_1050_4398` `:33780`-`33896`, `FUN_1050_3fca`, `FUN_1050_3cee` `:33412`-`33446`, `FUN_1050_40a2`). A side has drag bars only if it is not a planet, and a bar can be dragged only on a side the player owns. The per-slot arrow buttons carry no owner test; each is enabled only while its source side has something to give. Amounts stop at zero on the giving side and at free hold space on a receiving fleet. A receiving planet accepts any number of colonists: there is no population cap, owner, habitability, starbase, relations or Alternate Reality check. Fuel cannot go onto a planet. Colonists cannot go into a fleet the player does not fully know (a foreign fleet). When the other side is salvage, only minerals move. So a player can push colonists from their own fleet onto any planet it orbits. The client gives no warning. On OK it shows the colonists as moved at once, and for an Alternate Reality race it only repaints the map (`:33007`-`33016`).
  - *What is written.* Each side's change goes to the change log, the fleet first because it is side 0 (`FUN_1048_5bb0`, `FUN_1048_5f92`). The two halves are paired into one cargo-transfer order (record kinds 1, 2 or 25, depending on how large the amounts are) whose source is the fleet and whose target is the planet, carrying the fleet's signed amounts (`FUN_1048_6314`, from `:29238`). That is the source/target pattern step 6 needs to file the colonists in the landing ledger (fleet source, planet target, owners differ: `:29776`-`29779`). So messages 87 (Alternate Reality, on any planet not its own), 2 (unowned planet), 88 (another race's planet with a starbase) and an invasion (another race's planet without one) are all **reachable from the shipped client**, with no hand-edited order file.
  - *Host side, re-checked.* The Unload task (`:75200`-`75218`) and the step 6 replay (`:29773`-`29834`) are as the table says. The Unload task gives 85 for an unowned planet whose owned-flag is clear, 86 for Alternate Reality, an invasion record (colonise bit set by `FUN_10b8_301e`, `:78376`) when there is no starbase, and 309 otherwise. **Fleet lifecycle, verified against the exported client.** A fleet is a fixed 124-byte record; fleet IDs are capped at **512 per empire** (an 8-bit fleet-number sub-field, plus a design-slot sub-field capped at the well-known 16-designs-per-race limit). ### 5. Waypoints and multi-turn movement orders

A fleet's full route is an ordered list of waypoints, each with its own optional task; the fleet moves toward its next unresolved waypoint every turn at its ordered warp speed and executes that waypoint's task once it actually arrives, then proceeds to the following waypoint the next turn. A **Repeat Orders** flag keeps the route cycling: each reached waypoint is moved to the end of the list instead of being dropped (exact rule, including when it is not, under "Arrival and Repeat Orders" below; the host does not require the final waypoint to match the starting point), which combined with conditional load/unload amounts (see above) is the documented way to build a "set once, forget it" supply route. Distances snap to a fixed one-light-year grid for waypoint placement (diagonal legs are a decimal amount slightly over one light-year per grid step). A route leg that exactly repeats an already-traveled leg (e.g., an out-and-back path) is drawn differently (white) from other legs in the UI.

**Pursuit of a moving fleet: corrected (waypoint-placement pass).** The "no dynamic interception" conclusion this paragraph drew is wrong. The client records a fleet target's id (target kind 2) when a course is plotted onto a fleet, and the host keeps re-aiming such a waypoint at the fleet's new position after every generation's movement. See "Waypoint placement and editing (client)" below, item *Pursuit*. What remains right is that the movement loop itself steers toward the stored x/y. *Earlier text, kept for the record:* Each waypoint record is a fixed 18-byte entry: a target-planet-index field (or a "deep space" sentinel), an x/y coordinate pair, a task-type nibble, and a small block of task-specific parameters (matching the layout independently confirmed via the fleet-creation routine's own initial-waypoint setup, which copies the fleet's spawn planet's position — read from the same planet-position table the map painter uses — directly into this same x/y field once, at creation time). The per-turn movement/task-processing loop (the master turn-generation routine's fleet sweep) reads this stored x/y directly when computing how far the fleet can travel and whether it has arrived; no code path in that loop was found that re-resolves the waypoint's coordinates from a live fleet position before using them. This is consistent with — and reinforced by — the already-documented **Merge With Fleet** queued task (§ above): its target fleet is looked up by ID only "at the same map position" once the merging fleet has already arrived at its stored destination coordinates, i.e. a fleet-ID reference in the data model is used for validating what's present *after* arrival, not for steering *toward* that fleet's current position while it's still moving. Taken together, this supports reading Stars! as having **no automatic pursuit/interception mechanic**: setting a course toward another fleet (however the UI exposes that) most plausibly just captures that fleet's position at the moment the order is given, the same as clicking empty space, and catching a moving target is left entirely to the player re-issuing orders turn by turn as it moves. *(Superseded: the client's placement code has since been traced, see below.)*

**Route-list feasibility display, a UI-side reader of the waypoint array.** *(The placement write path this paragraph could not find is now traced: see "Waypoint placement and editing (client)" directly below.)* `FUN_1038_26ac` (`stars.exe.export.c:19100`-`19240`-ish) is a route/waypoint-list feasibility routine that walks a fleet's stored waypoint array leg by leg (indexing consecutive 18-byte records via the confirmed `+0x12` stride) and, for each leg, calls the same coordinate-delta helper (`FUN_1038_2924`) this document's turn-generation-side finding already relies on, to test whether the leg's distance is coverable at the fleet's warp speed (falling back to a Stargate-capability check, `FUN_1038_4ae4`, when the plain-engine test fails) before setting the report-row's text color — this is, concretely, the "sets a report-row's text color (a 'feasible' vs. 'out of range' red/gray distinction)... for a route/waypoint list cell" function `client-ui-dialog-catalog.md`'s segment-8 sampling pass already independently found and attributed to the UI's route/waypoint list (see that document's "genuine GDI-touching functions" note). Because this function reads each waypoint's x/y directly out of the stored array — the same static-field read pattern already confirmed for the turn-generation side — and never re-resolves a leg's endpoint from a live fleet position, it extends (without yet completing) this section's "no live re-resolution anywhere" claim to a second, genuinely UI-side code path (a route-list display/feasibility check that runs interactively as the player edits orders, not just once per turn). **Waypoint placement and editing (client), traced (waypoint-placement pass; code, with raw-byte checks in segment 12; not exercised in play).**

- *Edit model.* Selecting a fleet copies it into one scratch fleet record (`FUN_1038_14be`, `FUN_1038_1582` called with the fleet's id). Every route edit changes that copy and is committed at once by `FUN_1038_1582` called with id -1. The commit runs the order builder `FUN_1048_5bb0` (`:28727`-`28961`), which compares the copy with the stored fleet, and then stores the copy. The builder writes at most one waypoint order per commit (see *Orders written*), so the client commits after every single-waypoint change. The AI drivers commit their own fleets through the same routine.
- *Waypoint record, 18 bytes.* Bytes 0-3 hold x and y in whole light-years. Bytes 4-5 hold the target id: the planet number, the fleet's id, or the special object's id. For deep space the id is filler with no meaning: the waypoint's own index when set from the map, -1 when set from the destination picker. Byte 6 holds the task in its low nibble (0-9, table below) and the warp in its high nibble (0-11; 11 means "use the stargate", the value the automatic speed routine gives a gate leg). Byte 7 holds the target kind in its low nibble (1 planet, 2 fleet, 4 deep space, 8 special object) and bit `0x20`, the jump freeze mark described under *Pursuit*. Bytes 8-17 hold the task's settings: for Transport, five words, one per cargo slot, each a 4-bit action above a 12-bit amount (§4); Lay Mine Field's duration in the first word; Patrol's speed and range; Transfer Fleet's recipient. The fleet record keeps the waypoint count at `+0x62` and a far pointer at `+0x64` to a block whose 4-byte header holds the capacity (byte 2) and a copy of the count (byte 3). Client and host grow the block three entries at a time. Waypoint 0 is the fleet's own position.
- *Map hit-test* (the map window procedure at raw `1058:0000`, `:37100`-`37633`; nearest-object finder `FUN_1038_2988`, `:19281`-`19502`). The pointer is converted to galaxy coordinates. The finder searches planets, fleets and special objects, plus that fleet's own waypoints when a fleet is the selected object. An ordinary click sets no distance limit, so the nearest object anywhere on the map is found. A strictly nearer object always wins. On an exact tie, and for objects standing at the winner's position, the class searched first keeps first place (planets, then fleets, then special objects, then waypoints). The other objects at that spot are recorded alongside the winner; among co-located fleets the player's own fleet is preferred, and among co-located waypoints the current one. The special-object table holds minefields (at their centres), wormholes, the Mystery Trader and its other entries.
  - *Left click with Shift* (the click's own Shift flag), or with the latched view-option bit `0x10` (`client-interface.md`), while a fleet is the selected object: adds a waypoint (*Adding*). This is the only way to add a waypoint on the map.

If the button stays down and the pointer leaves the marker, the waypoint is dragged (*Dragging*). Clicking the same spot again cycles through the objects there, and an own fleet or own planet becomes the selected object. A double-click acts as a second click.

- *Adding* (`FUN_1058_5388`, `:40715`-`40864`; raw `1058:538e`-`56e1`).
  - *Limit.* A fleet that already has 87 waypoints (its position plus 86) gets a beep and a notice that no more than 86 are allowed (dynamic string 1302), and nothing is added. The host has no such limit.

Converted to light-years at each zoom (`FUN_1058_0ab0`, `:37680`-`37723`), the 20 pixels are 80 ly at 25%, 53 at 38%, 40 at 50%, 26 at 75%, 20 at 100%, 16 at 125%, 13 at 150%, 10 at 200% and 5 at 400%.

  - *Insertion point.* The new waypoint always goes directly after the current waypoint. There is no separate append: the new waypoint becomes the current one, so repeated Shift-clicks build a route in order, and making an earlier waypoint current first inserts into the middle. Mechanically, every entry after the current one moves up one place, the new entry is written at the current index plus one, and the hit record handed to the selection routine carries that index, so the new entry becomes current (`:40741`-`40829`). Selecting waypoint 0 (the fleet itself) and Shift-clicking therefore inserts a new first leg; with the last waypoint current, the click appends. The order written is a type 4 insert at that index (*Orders written*).

Keeping the original after-the-current rule, with the new waypoint becoming current, is the safe choice; if the port's list control cannot show waypoint 0, it can show the fleet's own row as the insertion anchor for "insert as first leg".
  - *Contents.* The new waypoint starts as a full copy of the current waypoint, so it inherits that waypoint's task and settings. Its position, target id and kind are then set, and its warp is replaced by the automatic speed (`FUN_1058_56e2`; rule in `ai-opponent-behavior.md` §12, steps 1-7).

  - *No refusal on fuel.* No fuel, range or stargate test refuses a placement. Feasibility shows only as the route list's text colour (paragraph above).
- *Defaults.* There is no "last used" warp: every new or dragged waypoint gets the automatic speed. Only waypoint 0 gets the plain efficient warp (raw `1058:575c`-`5766`; a waypoint at index 1 or later always runs the full rule). For every later waypoint it is a fuel-aware choice. The start is warp 9 for a one-way leg or one ending at an own or unowned planet. Otherwise the start is the efficient warp, or the previous leg's warp when that is higher (from waypoint 2 on), plus one when fuel is ample. The choice is then lowered to fit the fuel, and then to the slowest warp with the same arrival year (not when the target is a fleet). A leg a stargate can serve gets 11. The task is inherited from the current waypoint, and no target-dependent default task exists; a waypoint on an own planet gets no special task. Picking a task in the task list (`FUN_1050_1b6e`, `:32166`-`32237`) clears the ten settings bytes and then seeds them:
  - Lay Mine Field gets duration 5.
  - Patrol and Transfer Fleet get settings 0.
  - Merge with Fleet, on a waypoint not already aimed at a fleet, is re-aimed at one of the player's own fleets at that spot. The choice prefers a fleet holding this fleet's most numerous design, then one with no further orders.
- *Dragging* (`FUN_1058_5bb2`, `:41117`-`41397`; reached from the left-button branch when the press lands on a waypoint marker, `FUN_1058_5b36`).

- *Deleting* (`FUN_1050_6268`, `:35474`-`35560`; the Delete key, commands 103/104, or Yes on a drop onto a neighbour).
  - It is refused with a beep when the fleet has only waypoint 0 or when waypoint 0 is current.
  - It removes the current waypoint. If the two neighbours then stand at the same position, the later of them is removed too.
  - The current waypoint becomes the previous one (Delete key, command 103, a drop onto the previous waypoint) or the following one (command 104, a drop onto the next waypoint).
  - *Exact rule (planet-view pass, `:35517`-`35538`).* After the removal (and the removal of a now-duplicated later neighbour), the current index is first decremented, which selects the previous waypoint. Only for the "following" variants (command 104, or a drop onto the next waypoint) is it then incremented again, and only if a waypoint exists at that position, so the entry that followed the deleted one becomes current; when the deleted waypoint was the last one, the "following" variants also leave the previous waypoint current. The Delete and Backspace keys both use the "previous" variant, and both act only while a fleet is the selected object. Deleting waypoint 1 therefore leaves waypoint 0 (the fleet's own position) current; this is a normal state in the original, where waypoint 0 can be the current waypoint (the delete guard above tests for exactly that, and clicking the fleet itself makes it current). *The waypoint list, traced.* The Fleet Waypoints tile's list is an ordinary list box. Waypoint 0 is therefore a visible, selectable row; selecting it and pressing Delete gives the beep of the guard above, exactly as when the fleet has only waypoint 0. The client then reselects through the map hit-test at the new current waypoint's position, which keeps that waypoint current because co-located waypoints prefer the current one (*Map hit-test*).
  - *Reimplementation note (recommendation, not original behavior).* A port whose list never shows waypoint 0 should, after deleting the first real leg, select the fleet itself (its own row or the empty-route state) rather than jumping to the following waypoint, so that a following Shift-click still inserts a new first leg as in the original.

  - *Target:* clicking the waypoint's destination field in the fleet panel opens a picker (`FUN_1050_503a`, `:34612`-`34724`). Choosing a fleet for waypoint 0 of a fleet with no further waypoints creates the follow order of turn-generation steps 9-11 (`turn-generation-engine.md` §1).

- *Orders written* (`FUN_1048_5bb0`; the numbers are the record types of `save-turn-file-format.md` §9, decoded by `FUN_1048_68a8`, `:29996`-`30096`). The builder compares the stored fleet with the copy and writes:

  - type 10 (fleet id, new flag) when the Repeat Orders flag changed (fleet byte 5 bit `0x02`);
  - one waypoint order, after finding the first index at which the 18-byte entries differ. If the count shrank, it writes type 3, *delete* (fleet id; index, with the top bit set when two entries went, the duplicate case of *Deleting*). If the count grew, it writes type 4, *insert* (fleet id, index, the new entry). If the count is unchanged, it writes type 5, *replace* (fleet id, index, the entry).
  - Insert and replace payloads drop trailing zero bytes, and the decoder zero-fills the entry before copying.
  - A replace that targets the same fleet and index as the record just before it overwrites that record, so a run of warp nudges leaves one record.
  - Type 11 (`0xb`, set one waypoint's task nibble, value 0-9) is accepted by the decoder but never written by this client: none of the 21 calls of the record appender `FUN_1048_66f0` uses it.
- *Host acceptance* (step 6).
  - Types 3, 4 and 5 check only that the fleet id resolves and that the index is in range: insert takes 0 to the count; delete and replace need an index below the count, and a double delete needs the following entry too. A failing record aborts the whole generation (`turn-generation-engine.md` §1, step 6).
  - Position, target, warp, task and settings are accepted as written. No fuel, range, stargate, ownership or waypoint-count test was found. Insert and replace clear the freeze mark.
  - Normalisation comes afterwards. Step 10 (`FUN_1038_42e2`, `:20713`-`20921`, raw `1038:42e2`-`47cc`) walks every fleet in fleet-table order and does three things:
    - *Waypoint 0.* Its target kind and id are re-pointed: at the planet the fleet orbits (kind planet, that planet's id), or, for a fleet not at a planet, at deep space (id 0). The x/y are not touched. This is skipped when waypoint 0 already names a planet, when its task is Transport or Merge with Fleet, and for a fleet with the follow mark (fleet byte 5 bit `0x80`, a follow order). Destroyed fleets are not exempt from this part.
    - *Bounds clamp* (fleets not flagged destroyed only). Each of the fleet's x and y is clamped separately into 1,000 to 1,000 + the galaxy size (inclusive at both ends). A coordinate that had to be clamped is written into the fleet and into waypoint 0's same coordinate; an in-range coordinate leaves waypoint 0 alone.
    - *Later waypoints* (fleets not flagged destroyed, with two or more waypoints or the follow mark). Waypoints from index 1 (from index 0 for a follow-marked fleet) are visited in order. A special-object target is re-synced: it takes the object's current position, unless the object is gone, or is a wormhole whose per-race bit for the fleet's owner in the record's word at byte offset 8 is clear (a per-race visibility mask, raw `1038:4490`-`44a3`) and that has drifted away from the stored point; then the waypoint becomes deep space at the stored point (id = its own index), with message 248 only in the drifted-wormhole case. A fleet target that does not carry the jump-freeze mark goes through the re-lock under *Pursuit*.
    - Step 10 runs three times a generation: at `:71980` (step 10), at `:72181` (step 29) and at the end of the special-object pass when an object was relocated (`:73171`).

- *Pursuit (corrects the "no dynamic interception" reading above).* A waypoint aimed at a fleet stores the target's id and its position at the moment of the order. The host keeps pursuing it in four ways:
  - *Step 10 re-lock* (`:20812`-`20906`, raw `1038:450c`-`47a5`; code-confirmed, not exercised in play). Two per-fleet working marks take part, both in the fleet record's flag byte at `+0x76`: the **split/merge mark** (bit `0x20`) and the **claim mark** (bit `0x40`).
    - *The split/merge mark.* While orders are applied in step 6 it is set on every fleet touched by its owner's ship-exchange order (record type `0x17`, both fleets, `:30197`-`30206`), split order (type `0x18`; the fleet split from, through the fleet-copy routine `FUN_1038_2180` and `FUN_1048_592a`, `:18799`, `:28506`-`28514`) or merge order (type `0x25`, every fleet in the merge set, `:30248`-`30277`). Step 12(c), the mode-1 task pass, clears both marks on every fleet (`:74802`-`74803`). So only the first call of step 10 in a generation ever sees a split/merge mark; the later calls (step 29 and the special-object pass) start from cleared claim marks. At the first call the only marks are those set since the host file was loaded, because neither mark survives a save and load (traced): the fleet writer `FUN_1070_4e38` (`:48186`-`48362`) writes a full record (report level 7) as the header block, ship counts, cargo, damage words, the two bytes at `+0x60` and `+0x62` and then the waypoint records (`FUN_1070_33de`, `:46828`-`46859`); the four bytes at `+0x74`-`+0x77` are written only for a partial record (report level below 7, `:48333`-`48361`), where byte `+0x76` is the scanned-warp field of another player's fleet (warp in the low nibble, bit `0x10` = known). The host file is written after the visibility reset `FUN_1070_5aa0` has set every live fleet to level 7 (`:49164`-`49167`), so no host-file record carries that byte. The loader `FUN_1070_2440` zero-fills each new record (`:45742`-`45746`) and reads bytes `+0x74`-`+0x77` only for a partial record (`:45836`-`45839`), so after step 2's load every fleet's flag byte is 0 and the marks exist only in the host's memory during the generation. (The same byte's bit `0x80` is a different working flag: the battle set-up marks with it the fleets it leaves out when a battle's token limit is reached, and clears it when room is found for them, `:99567`-`99603`.)
    - *Trigger.* For each fleet-targeted waypoint without the jump-freeze mark, the target is looked up by its stored id. The waypoint is re-locked when the target no longer exists, or stands anywhere but the waypoint's stored x/y, or carries the split/merge mark and belongs to a different player than the pursuer. Otherwise the target is kept and gets the claim mark.
    - *Candidates.* Every fleet standing exactly on the stored x/y whose owner is the original target's owner (taken from the owner bits of the stored id). They are scanned in fleet-table order.
    - *Score.* `FUN_1038_4dbe` (`:21240`-`21273`, raw `1038:4dbe`-`4e4e`) is the fleet's **total mass in kT**: for each of the 16 stacks, ship count × the design's stored empty mass (hull plus components, design `+0x28`), plus the four cargo holds (Ironium, Boranium, Germanium and colonists, fleet `+0x4c`-`+0x5b`; fuel does not count). It is a 32-bit signed value.
    - *Pass 1.* Among candidates without the claim mark that fit the pursuer's battle-plan primary-target type (`FUN_1038_4134` in its strict mode, argument 1: there None, Any and Starbase fit **no** fleet; the other types use the same hull tests as Patrol), keep the heaviest. The running best starts at 0. A candidate replaces it when its mass is greater, or, when equal, on a coin flip (one draw of 0-1, a 0 replaces), so ties favour later fleets unevenly rather than uniformly.
    - *Pass 2* (only when pass 1 chose nothing). The same heaviest-of-the-fitting-type choice, now ignoring the claim mark. At the same time every candidate, of any type, enters a running random pick: the k-th one counted replaces the pick when a draw of 0 to k-1 gives 0, but a claimed candidate other than the first one counted then also needs a coin flip to win. The heaviest fitting candidate is used if there is one, otherwise the random pick.
    - *Result.* The chosen fleet's id and position are written into the waypoint and the chosen fleet gets the claim mark, which steers later pursuers' pass 1 away from it. With no candidate at all, the stale id and the stored x/y are kept. Consequence: with primary target None, Any or Starbase every re-lock is the random pick.
  - *Movement and refresh.* The movement loop steers toward the stored x/y. Then the end-of-movement refresh (`FUN_1080_1060`, `:56699`-`56737`) runs once per generation, after all fleets have moved. It copies each target's new position into waypoints 1 onward that are aimed at that target, on every fleet with more than one waypoint and no follow mark, and turns a waypoint whose target is gone into deep space. Only then is arrival tested (the fleet standing on its waypoint 1). So each turn a pursuer heads for where the target stood before that turn's movement, and catches it only when both end a turn on the same spot. On arrival the waypoint becomes a planet or deep-space point unless its task is Transport or Merge with Fleet.
  - *Jump freeze* (`FUN_1080_133e`, `:56808`-`56859`). When the target jumps through a stargate (`:73866`-`73868`) or enters a wormhole (`:74010`-`74016`), every other player's waypoint aimed at it is set to the point it left and gets bit `0x20`, which the refresh then leaves alone.
  - *Step 39 revalidation* (`:47410`-`47500`, raw `1070:3d0d`-`3f6c`; code-confirmed, not exercised in play). In each player's pass, for each of that player's fleets with two or more waypoints, **unless waypoint 0's task is Transport** (then the whole check is skipped that turn), every fleet-targeted waypoint from index 1 on is checked after the pass's visibility update:
    - Its jump-freeze mark is noted and cleared.
    - Target missing, or flagged destroyed: **message 40**.
    - Target present and seen by this player this turn (fleet byte 5 bit `0x01`): nothing more, the pursuit goes on. A frozen waypoint whose target is still seen therefore keeps chasing; it still holds the point the target left, step 10 of the next generation finds the target elsewhere and re-locks, keeping the id when nobody stands there, and that generation's refresh copies the target's real position in again.
    - Target present but not seen, and orbiting a planet (its orbit field is not -1), and the waypoint was not frozen: **message 41**.
    - Target present but not seen, and either in deep space or the waypoint was frozen: **message 42**.
    - After any of the three messages the waypoint becomes deep space at its stored x/y (id = its own index). If a planet stands exactly at that point (`FUN_1038_2988` in planet mode), it becomes a planet waypoint for that planet. Task, warp and settings are kept. The stored x/y is whatever the end-of-movement refresh last wrote, which takes no account of visibility: for messages 40 and 42 it is the target's true position at the end of this year's movement (or the departure point of a frozen waypoint), not the last place the player saw it; for message 41 it is the planet the target orbits.
    - *Messages* (all to the pursuer's owner, with the pursuing fleet as the message's subject). 40 says the fleet's waypoint target appears to have been destroyed or to have disappeared and that the orders now lead to its last known location; fields: the pursuing fleet, the target fleet. 41 says the tracked fleet seems to have slipped behind a planet and that the orders now lead to that planet; fields: the pursuing fleet, the planet the target orbits. 42 says the tracked fleet has outrun the player's scanners and that the orders now lead to its last known location; field: the pursuing fleet.
  - In short, a pursuit lasts as long as the pursuer's owner can see the target. It stops at a gate or a wormhole mouth for the rest of that generation.
- *Arrival and Repeat Orders* (the waypoint advance; `FUN_1080_1060` `:56738`-`56772` and `FUN_1050_642e` `:35564`-`35631`; code-confirmed, not exercised in play).
  - *Arrival.* It is tested once per generation, in the end-of-movement refresh after all movement passes, for fleets with two or more waypoints and no follow mark (follow-marked fleets drop waypoint 1 there with message 311 instead). A fleet has arrived when its position equals waypoint 1's stored x/y exactly. Waypoint 1 is then copied over waypoint 0; if the copy targets a fleet and its task is neither Transport nor Merge with Fleet, the copy becomes a planet waypoint (the planet the fleet now orbits) or a deep-space point (id 0). Waypoint 1 itself is then handed to the waypoint-advance routine.
  - *Recycling.* It is requested when Repeat Orders is on (fleet byte 5 bit `0x02`), except when the reached waypoint, as it still stands at index 1, carries Patrol and targets a fleet (a pending Patrol intercept leg). It is carried out only when the fleet has three or more waypoints and the last waypoint's x/y differ from the reached waypoint's x/y. Recycling takes the reached waypoint out of index 1 and writes an exact copy of it (task, settings, warp, target kind and id as they stood at index 1, not the converted copy) into the last slot, so the count is unchanged. The departed old waypoint 0 is not recycled: the arrival copy has already overwritten it. Without recycling the reached waypoint is removed and the count drops by one.
  - *Consequences.* A route A, B, C, A started at A cycles for ever: after B it reads B, C, A, B; after C, C, A, B, C; after A, A, B, C, A again. The host does not check that the last waypoint matches the start: a route A, B, C with Repeat Orders cycles between B and C. The last remaining destination is never recycled, so a fleet with one destination stops there either way. A reached waypoint standing on the same point as the last waypoint is dropped rather than duplicated. A fleet-targeted waypoint without Patrol is recycled with its fleet target, so its copy at the end keeps chasing that fleet. A Patrol intercept leg is not recycled while it is still aimed at a fleet, but once the step 39 revalidation has turned it into a fixed point (messages 40-42) it is an ordinary Patrol waypoint and is recycled (Patrol note, item 7).

**Stargates** let a fleet skip warp travel across arbitrary distance in a single turn, provided both the origin and destination planets have a stargate, the fleet carries only fuel (see Cargo, above), and the ship's mass and the distance requested both fall within the *sending* stargate's rated limits (mass checks additionally require the *receiving* gate's rating too). A gate can be pushed up to 5x over its rated distance or mass limit and still sometimes succeed — this "overgating" always damages the ship, and beyond 100% cumulative damage the ship does not survive the jump. **Superseded, history only — do not implement from these bullets.** The community overgating formulas below are kept for comparison; the governing rules are the "Overgating, code-confirmed" block that follows, whose last bullet reconciles them term by term (the vanish chance is one third of the combined damage, not the fitted A-about-68 curve, and Interstellar Traveler ships have zero random loss, not a "reduced" chance). Community formulas:

- **Range damage (percent)** = 100% x (distance minus the gate's maximum range) / (4 x the maximum range).
- **Mass damage (percent)** = 100% x (1 minus the product of two factors), where the sending-gate factor is (5 x the sending gate's maximum mass minus the ship's mass) / (4 x the sending gate's maximum mass), and the receiving-gate factor is the same expression using the receiving gate's maximum mass.
- **Combined damage (percent)** = mass damage + (100% minus mass damage) x range damage.
- **Chance a mass-overgated ship vanishes outright** (community-fitted approximation, races other than Interstellar Traveler), in percent = (100 minus A) x (5 x maximum mass minus the ship's mass) squared / (4 x maximum mass) squared, plus A, with the fitted constant A about 68.
- **For distance overgating**, the vanish chance is roughly one third of the range-damage percentage, as a rule of thumb.

Interstellar Traveler races are documented by the community as having a reduced (but not quantified) chance of losing overgated ships to the void, while suffering the same damage percentages as anyone else (*superseded: the code skips the random vanish roll entirely for them, see the next block*). The client's own description text for this trait independently confirms the existence and framing of this reduced risk (still without a numeric figure) — see `race-traits.md` §3a.

**Overgating, code-confirmed (resolved this pass).** Evidence: `FUN_1080_0962` (`:56317`-`56581`) and its per-design rating test `FUN_1080_0e10` (`:56586`-`56674`), called only from the movement pass at `:73862`. The raw bytes of segment 17, offsets `0x962`-`0xe0e`, were checked as well. This replaces the community approximations above wherever they differ.
- **Inputs.** The sending gate's safe range R and safe mass, and the receiving gate's safe mass (`component-stats.tsv`, category `0x0200`). A stored 65535 means "any": an "any" range counts as 8,000 ly, and an "any" mass limit is never exceeded. The distance d is the straight-line distance in light-years from the fleet to the destination planet, truncated to whole light-years. Each design in the fleet is tested with its own empty mass (hull plus components; cargo and fuel do not count). Range is tested against the sending gate only; mass is tested against both gates.
- **Refusal.** If d is more than 5R, or any design's mass is more than 5 times a limited gate's mass rating, the jump is refused before anything happens (messages 227 and 228) and the fleet stays put. Exactly 5 times is not refused, but it is certain destruction (next bullet).
- **Damage percentage, per design.** Each exceeded limit gives a survival factor in ten-thousandths: 2,500 x (5L - x) / L, truncated, where L is the limit and x the distance or mass (that is, (5L - x) / (4L)). The range factor comes first. Each exceeded mass factor then multiplies the running factor, dividing by 10,000 and truncating. If any single factor is below 1 the design takes 100%. Otherwise its damage is (10,000 - running factor) / 100, truncated to a whole percent; within every limit this is 0. So damage = 1 - (range factor x sending-mass factor x receiving-mass factor), which is the community "combined" formula term for term, computed per design and truncated at each step.
- **Ship losses, per design stack.** A stack at 100% is lost whole. Otherwise each ship vanishes independently with probability floor(damage / 3) percent, one roll out of 100 per ship. **Interstellar Traveler ships never vanish** (the roll is skipped). A vanished ship is counted as one of the stack's already-damaged ships with probability equal to their stored damage figure out of 500.
- **Damage to survivors.** Each surviving ship takes damage percent of the design's armor (truncated, at least 1 point). Already-damaged ships whose old damage plus the new damage reaches the design's armor are destroyed, so a ship never survives past 100% cumulative damage. The survivors then all carry one shared damage figure, stored in the usual 1/500-of-armor unit (at least 1), and the whole stack is marked damaged. That figure is the new damage plus the old damage of the previously damaged ships, spread over the survivors. The old damage of ships just destroyed is still included in that spread. Because of that, the spread can exceed the armor when more damaged ships die than undamaged ones survive; the figure (armor-units × 500 / armor) is then stored in its 9-bit field without a cap and wraps modulo 512 (raw `1080:0c58`-`0ccf`), so such survivors can end up shown lightly damaged. *Reimplementation note (recommendation, not original behavior):* drop the destroyed ships' old damage from the spread, or cap the figure at the armor.
- **Messages.** None if no ship was lost, however badly damaged the fleet is. If ships were lost and some remain, compare the loss with the fleet's ship count before the jump: below a quarter (rounded down) gives message 232; above half (rounded down) gives 234; otherwise 233. Message 235 needs 65,536 or more losses, so it cannot occur in practice. A wiped-out fleet gets message 231 and is removed. Fields (raw `1080:0aa8`-`0adf`, `0d70`-`0d91`, `0de4`-`0e01`; all to the fleet's owner, the fleet as the message's subject): 227 and 231 the fleet, the departure planet and the destination planet; 228 the same plus the design that is too heavy; 232-234 the same three plus the number of ships lost.
- **Counting detail.** The routine decides "wiped out" by a stack count. Stacks below 100% are counted, then that count drops by one for every stack at 100% and for every stack emptied by losses. The fleet is treated as lost (message 231, removed) exactly when that count is zero. So a fleet with one stack at 100% and one stack that survives is reported lost and removed with its survivors. A fleet with more 100% stacks than surviving stacks is kept, even if no ship is left in it. Exactly: let N be the occupied stacks below 100% (stacks within every limit, at 0%, included), H the stacks at 100% and E the stacks below 100% emptied by vanishing or by old damage; the fleet is removed with message 231 when N is 0, or when N - H - E is exactly 0, and is kept otherwise, a negative value included. Examples: one stack within all limits plus one stack at 100% gives 1 - 1 = 0, so the whole fleet is removed, the undamaged ships with it; two stacks at 100% plus one surviving stack gives -1, so the fleet is kept with its survivors; two stacks at 100% plus one stack emptied by losses also gives -1, so a fleet with no ship left is kept. Such an empty fleet gets the ordinary loss notice (all ships lost is more than half, so 234) instead of 231, and its owner's step 39 pass later flags it destroyed without any notice, because that pass flags every one of the player's fleets whose 16 stack counts are all zero (`:47194`-`47205`). *(Re-confirmed from the raw bytes, `1080:09e5`-`0a47` and `1080:0a77`-`0d09`. Raw bytes re-checked, gap-report pass, segment 17: the first loop adds one per occupied stack and takes one back when the rating test reports 100% (`1080:09ec`, `1080:0a2e`), and stores the count (`1080:0a47`); the loss loop then sends a 100% stack to the same decrement used for an emptied stack (`1080:0a88`-`0a8c` to `1080:0cdf`); the fleet is lost when the count is zero (`1080:0d05`). A 100% stack is therefore subtracted twice, and the outcome comes from comparing one stack count with another, not from counting ships. This is original behavior.)* *Reimplementation note (recommendation, not original behavior):* decide "wiped out" by whether any ship survives; reproduce the double subtraction only if exact compatibility is required.

- **Pre-jump cargo dump (the "forced dump"; gap-report pass, `FUN_10b0_1f8c` `:73702`-`73852`).** In the movement pass, a fleet whose next leg is a gate jump is checked in this order: the departure planet must have a gate owned by the fleet's race or by a race whose relation toward the fleet's race is "friend" (else message 230); with no departure gate the fleet must carry Jump Gates (else 222); the destination must be a planet with a gate (else 327 or 226) whose owner is the fleet's race or a friend (else 229). Then, **only when the jump leaves from a planet's gate and the fleet's race is not Interstellar Traveler**:

  - Otherwise all three minerals and all colonists are moved from the fleet onto the **departure planet**: they are added to the planet's surface stock and population in full, with no loss, no cap and no habitability test, and the fleet's holds are zeroed. Fuel stays aboard. The fleet's owner gets message 236 (minerals only), 237 (colonists only) or 238 (both); when the planet belongs to a friend, that friend gets the same message.   - Interstellar Traveler fleets, and any fleet using its own Jump Gates, keep their cargo aboard and arrive with it.
- **Against the community formulas.** The range-damage, mass-damage and combined formulas agree exactly, apart from the game's truncations and the per-design application. The vanish chance is one third of the *combined* damage for range and mass alike, which matches the distance rule of thumb. The fitted mass curve with A about 68 only makes sense read as a survival chance (as written it gives 100% vanishing at the safe limit). Read that way it matches the game at the two ends (no loss at the limit; just under 5 times, a 33% loss chance against the fit's 32%) but not in between: half-way to 5 times, the game's 50% damage gives a 16% loss chance, against 24% for the fit. - **The whole procedure, in order** (movement pass `FUN_10b0_1f8c` `:73702`-`73869`, then `FUN_1080_0962` and `FUN_1080_0e10`).
  1. Gate checks: departure gate owned by the fleet's race or a friend (else 230); with no departure gate, Jump Gates on every occupied design (else 222); destination a planet with a gate (else 327 or 226), owned by the fleet's race or a friend (else 229).
  2. Pre-jump cargo dump, only from a planet's gate and only for races other than Interstellar Traveler: colonists aboard at another race's gate refuse the jump (350); otherwise minerals and colonists go onto the departure planet (236-238).
  3. Distance d: straight line from the fleet to the destination planet, truncated to whole light-years.
  4. Per occupied stack, in stack order, with the design's empty mass m: d above 5R refuses the jump with 227; otherwise m above 5 times a limited mass rating at either gate refuses it with 228. The first failing stack decides, and a refused fleet stays put with nothing else changed (its cargo is already on the departure planet). Otherwise the stack's damage D is the per-design percentage above (range factor, then sending-gate mass factor, then receiving-gate mass factor; any factor below 1 gives 100%).
  5. Let N be the number of stacks with D below 100. If N is 0 the fleet is flagged destroyed with 231 and the routine ends.
  6. Each stack with D above 0, in stack order: at 100% the whole stack is lost and N drops by one. Below 100%: unless the owner is Interstellar Traveler, each ship vanishes on a roll of 0-99 below floor(D / 3), and each vanished ship also removes one of the stack's previously damaged ships when a roll of 0-499 falls below the stack's stored damage figure (while any remain). For the ships left, the new damage is D% of the armor (truncated, at least 1) and the old damage of a damaged ship is figure × armor / 500 (at least 1); if damaged ships remain and new + old reaches the armor, all of them are destroyed. Survivors get the shared figure ((new × survivors + old × previously damaged count) / survivors) × 500 / armor, truncated, at least 1, with every ship marked damaged. A stack left empty drops N by one. Stacks at 0% are untouched.
  7. If N is now exactly 0 the fleet is flagged destroyed with 231. Otherwise it is kept: with any losses the owner gets 232, 233 or 234 by the quarter and half tests (235 at 65,536 or more), the fleet's record is updated and its "saw action" mark is set.
  8. A kept fleet makes the jump: its "has not moved" mark is cleared and every other player's waypoint aimed at it is frozen at its departure point (*Pursuit*, jump freeze).

**Wormholes** are naturally occurring, free, uncapped-mass shortcuts between two points in deep space (they cannot form too close to a planet's gravity well). A fleet given a wormhole as a waypoint enters and exits the same year it reaches the opening. Wormholes are individually cloaked against ordinary detection by 75% until a player has discovered them at least once, and each end independently drifts a little every year, with an overall stability rating (from "Rock Solid," lasting 30+ years in one area, down to "Very Unstable," relocating within about 5 years) describing how long a given wormhole persists before disappearing or relocating. *(Corrected by the complete rule below: the cloak lasts until the race has located that end at its current place and comes back after every relocation; stability gets worse with age; no wormhole ever disappears, it only relocates.)*

**Wormhole lifecycle, complete rule (Route pass; code and raw bytes, not exercised in play).** Each end of a pair is its own 18-byte special-object entry of category hex `40`: position at `+2`/`+4`; a status word at `+6` holding a base stability in bits 0-1 (0-2), an age in years in bits 2-11 (0-1,023, wrapping) and a seen-this-generation scratch bit 13; the per-race **located** mask at `+8`; the per-race **traversed** mask at `+10`; and the id of the other end at `+12`.
1. *Creation, at new-game time only* (`FUN_1078_1334` `:51702`-`51745`, raw segment 16 `0x3564`-`0x3593`). None when "No Random Events" is set. Otherwise the number of pairs is a uniform draw by galaxy size: Tiny 0-2, Small 1-3, Medium 1-5, Large 3-6, Huge 4-8 (spans 3, 3, 5, 4, 5 and minimums 0, 1, 1, 3, 4, read from segment 16 at `0x103` and `0x108`). Each end gets its own base stability, uniform 0-2, age 0, empty masks, and a position from 100 uniform draws over the galaxy: the first draw scoring 0 is kept, otherwise the lowest-scoring draw. No wormhole is created or removed after this.
2. *Placement score* (`FUN_1118_0456` `:115096`-`115232`; lower is better). 15, the worst, when the point lies outside the galaxy (coordinates 1,000 to 1,000 + width, width 400 x (size index + 1)) or exactly on another special object, a planet or a fleet. Otherwise the OR of: 4 when within 10 ly of an edge; for each planet, by squared distance, 8 below 25, 4 below 100, 2 below 400, 1 below 784; for the other end of its own pair, 8 below 25, 4 below 100, 2 below 900, 1 below 4,900; for every other wormhole end, 8 below 16, 4 below 64, 2 below 225, 1 below 900. A score of 0 therefore means at least 28 ly from every planet, 30 ly from other wormholes, 70 ly from its partner and 10 ly from the edge.
3. *Stability tier* (`FUN_1118_0742`, raw segment 36 `0x0742`-`0x0782`): base + (age ÷ 5, truncated) - 2, clamped to 0-6, 0 being the most stable. ~~One step per 20 counts~~: the age field counts in steps of 4 within the status word, so 20 raw counts are 5 years. Every end starts at tier 0; base 2 reaches tier 1 at age 5 and tier 6 at age 30, base 0 reaches tier 1 at 15 and tier 6 at 40. The map's object display shows the tier as one of seven stability names, dynamic strings 967-973 in tier order (`:12323`-`12325`).
4. *Yearly step*, turn-generation step 21 (`FUN_10b0_0f9a` mode 1, `:72569`-`72620`, raw segment 23 `0x0fc4`-`0x1106`), every end separately in table order:
   - A 0-99 roll is made; when it is below the tier the end **jumps**, so the yearly chance is tier percent (0% to 6%). A jump clears the located mask for every race, resets the age to 0 (the base is kept) and places the end anywhere in the galaxy by the 100-draw rule of item 1.
   - Otherwise the age goes up by 1 and the end **drifts**: up to 100 candidates, each offset from the year's starting position by a uniform -12 to +12 ly on each axis (a zero offset is skipped); the first candidate scoring 0 is taken, otherwise the lowest-scoring one. An end thus moves by up to 12 ly per axis nearly every year.
   - The traversed mask and the pairing are never cleared. Mean time between jumps, counted from age 0: about 32 years for base 2, 37 for base 1, 42 for base 0 (medians 29, 34 and 39).
5. *Waypoints aimed at an end* (waypoint synchronisation `FUN_1038_42e2` `:20790`-`20812`, steps 10 and 29). The waypoint is moved to the end's current position when the waypoint owner's located bit is set or the end has not moved; otherwise message 248 is posted and the waypoint becomes a plain deep-space point. Step 29 runs after step 21, and a jump has just cleared every located bit, so a jump turns every waypoint aimed at that end into a plain point in the same generation, each with message 248. Drift is followed silently by every race that has located the end.

**The two per-race wormhole masks and wormhole detection (Route pass).**
- *Located mask* (`+8`). Set when one of the race's scanners detects the end (fleet scanners `FUN_1070_5f00` `:49413`-`49416`, planet scanners `FUN_1070_64fc` `:49693`-`49696`, Packet Physics packet scanners `FUN_1070_6d7c` `:49981`-`49983`), and by a transit, on the **exit end only** (`:74022`). Cleared for all races when that end jumps.
- *Traversed mask* (`+10`). Set only by a transit, on **both ends**, for the transiting fleet's owner (`:74018`, `:74021`), and never cleared, not even by a jump. Detection never sets it. It is what the map uses to draw the line joining the two ends (`:38378`-`38390`) and what the AI's wormhole score calls "known" (`ai-opponent-behavior.md` §12).
- *Detection rule.* A ship or planet scanner with normal range r sees an end lying within r when the race's located bit for that end is set, or the end is within r/4 (squared distance at most r²/16), or within the scanner's penetrating range. A Packet Physics packet sees any end within its packet range (warp squared). There is no random roll. So the 75% cloak "until discovered at least once" is the r/4 rule, it is governed by the **located** mask, not by the traversed mask (word `+10`), and it applies again after each jump.

**Wormhole placement and stability, confirmed by inspection of the exported client.** Candidate wormhole (and similar "special object") positions are validated by a dedicated placement-scoring routine before being accepted: a candidate is rejected outright for landing outside galaxy bounds, exactly on an existing planet, fleet, or other special object, and otherwise scored against every planet and every same-type special object in the galaxy using four squared-distance tiers (very close / close / medium / far), producing a bitmask a caller can use to keep re-rolling until a sufficiently isolated position is found — the same rejection-sampling shape already documented for ordinary star placement in `new-game-setup.md` §3, reused here for special-object spawning. Separately, a stability-rating index is derived from a packed per-object data field and clamped to **exactly 7 tiers (0-6)**, consistent with, and numerically confirming, the "Rock Solid" through "Very Unstable" rating scale described above. A same-location, opposite-race fleet check against a wormhole-type object sums three of the fleet's stored fields — **now confirmed to be specifically the fleet's three mineral cargo pools (Ironium + Boranium + Germanium), not a generic mass/cargo total** — against a flat threshold of **5000**: below the threshold, a single fixed notification message is posted to the fleet's owner (through the report/message-record store described below, gated on a per-fleet "already notified" flag).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- **Which fields count.** Only minefield objects owned by another race whose relation toward the fleet's race is not "friend". A field is a circle centred on its position with radius √(mine count): the stored mine count is compared directly with squared distances (`FUN_1040_3d6e`, `:23918`-; the nearest-field search at `:74532`-`74535`).
- **Distance inside a field.** The fleet's straight path for the year runs from its position toward its waypoint, limited to the distance it actually covers that year. For each field the path crosses, the routine takes the whole-light-year distances along the path at which the path enters and leaves the circle (or the year's travel ends). Overlapping fields of the **same type** are merged into one stretch, up to 8 stretches per type, so overlap adds no extra chance. Different types are kept apart.
- **The speed used.** This is not the ordered warp. The game takes the distance d actually covered this year and uses the smallest whole number w from 3 to 10 with w² ≥ d − 1. Gate jumps (waypoint warp 11 and above) never enter this routine.
- **Racial allowance.** Space Demolition adds 2 to every safe warp and Super Stealth adds 1 (`:74151`-`74152`). If w is no more than 3 plus the allowance, or the fleet does not move, nothing happens.
- **The rolls.** Stretches are taken in order of their entry distance, across all three types. For a stretch of type t, the chance is c = (w − allowance − safe warp of t) × the type's rate, out of 1,000. It applies only when that difference is positive. One roll of 0-999 is made **per whole light-year** of the stretch's length, and a roll below c is a hit at that light-year. So the chance is per light-year travelled inside the field, not per turn and not per field. The first hit ends the routine: a fleet is hit at most once per year by moving.
- **"Scoop fleet" (index 0/1).** The second column of each pair applies when any occupied stack's engine burns no fuel at warp 4 (the engine record's fuel entry for warp 4 is zero; raw `10b0:31e6`). That covers the six ram-scoop engines, and also Settler's Delight, Fuel Mizer and Enigma Pulsar. This index has nothing to do with the mine-layer components: the Speed Trap layers lay type-2 fields.
- **Damage.** The fleet stops at the hit point: its year's travel is cut to the entry distance plus the light-years rolled before the hit. *(Mapping onto movement, Route pass, `FUN_10b0_1f8c` `:73604`-`73648`, `:73885`-`73965`, raw segment 23 `0x2be8`-`0x2d64`.)* The year's distance is fixed and its fuel is charged before the minefield routine runs: the fleet pays for min(warp², distance to the waypoint), or burns all its fuel if that falls short. The routine is then given that distance in whole light-years and, on a hit, returns the shorter stop distance. The fleet is placed on the straight line from its starting point toward the waypoint at that distance, by the same proportional step and per-axis rounding as any partial move, and is left in deep space with its waypoints unchanged, to go on next year. **No fuel is refunded** for the unused part of the year's distance, and the ram-scoop fuel gain of the move (`FUN_1038_373a`, message 243), which is credited only after a move without a mine hit, is skipped for that year. A hit that destroys the whole fleet ends its movement. For each stack, the raw damage is (ships × damage per ship + top-up) × the number of engines in the design's engine slot. The top-up is (fleet minimum − damage per ship × total ships in the fleet), applied to the first damaged stack only and only when the fleet has 4 ships or fewer. The stack's shields absorb up to half of that. *(Exact, Route pass, `:74430`-`74451`: the absorbed amount is the smaller of the stack's total shields, ships × the design's shield points per ship from `FUN_1038_0a0e` (the same figure battle uses, Regenerating Shields' +40% included, `combat-resolution.md` §10c), and half the stack's raw damage, truncated. A fleet that reaches its Route waypoint during movement is routed in pass 3 and leaves the next year. A fleet given Route while it already sits at the planet is routed in pass 1 and leaves the same year.
4. *Effect.* The whole fleet, with its cargo, gets a second waypoint: the destination planet's position, target that planet, task Route, at the warp chosen below. Waypoint 0 is left as it is. Because the new leg carries Route too, a fleet arriving at a planet that has its own destination is forwarded again, so routes chain. Message 295 goes to the owner, or 296 when the chosen warp is 0.
5. *Warp choice*, in order:
   - Start from the fleet's efficient warp (`FUN_1050_69c2`, the highest warp up to 10 at which every engine uses at most 120% fuel).
   - When both planets belong to the fleet's owner and both have starbases: if the fleet carries no minerals or colonists and both starbases have stargates, the overgating test (`FUN_1080_0e10`, §5 "Overgating") is run with the distance and the heaviest design mass present; if it allows the jump with no loss, the warp is 11 (a gate jump, performed by the movement pass) and the remaining steps are skipped. Otherwise, if the warp is below 9 and the destination starbase can dock ships (and so refuels, `turn-generation-engine.md` §1 step 22), the warp becomes the highest of 9, 8, ..., 1 at which the fleet's fuel carries it over its whole route, or 0 if none does.
   - For a warp of 1-10, the slowest warp with the same estimated trip time is taken: with t = (distance ÷ w) ÷ w, both divisions truncated, w is lowered by 1 while it is above 2 and the same figure at w - 1 is not larger than t.
   - Finally w is lowered by 1 while it is above 0 and the fuel this leg needs at w exceeds the fuel aboard. A result of 0 leaves the fleet parked with the leg in place.
6. *Fallback when routing fails*, in mode 4 only and only for a fleet with one waypoint that orbits a planet: the default-orders routine runs.
   - If the planet is unowned, or the fleet's race is Alternate Reality and the planet is its own, and the fleet has remote-mining capacity (`FUN_1080_193e`: ships times the mining rate of their Mining Robot parts, capped at 4,000): at an unowned planet, the first other fleet of the same owner at the same position whose mining capacity is 1 to 3,999 becomes the target of a Merge with Fleet order on waypoint 0, and the Merge handler executes it at once in the same pass (messages as for Merge). With no such fleet, or for Alternate Reality at its own planet, waypoint 0 becomes Remote Mining, which runs from the next year's mode-3 pass while the fleet stays.
   - In every other case the Route task stays on waypoint 0, inert, and is tested again in every pass.
   - In all cases the routine also resets the fleet's battle plan to plan 0, the default (fleet byte `+0x60`, the plan index read by `FUN_10f0_6e2e`). A fleet left on a failed Route at its last planet therefore has its battle plan reset every year. *Reimplementation note (recommendation, not original behavior):* the reset is only meaningful for newly built fleets, which use the same routine; a clone may limit it to them.
   - On arrival at the last waypoint, the end-of-movement refresh posts message 78 (orders completed) unless the task will act; a Route counts as acting only when the planet is the owner's and has a destination (`:56773`-`56786`).
7. *New fleets without a route.* Production calls the same default-orders routine: an Alternate Reality fleet with mining capacity built at its own planet starts with Remote Mining, and every new fleet starts on plan 0.

**Transfer Fleet (9), complete rule (Route pass; code-only, `FUN_10b0_3f3a` `:76035`-`76176`).**
1. *When.* Only in task mode 4 (turn-generation step 23h, after movement, the battle, colonisation and Lay Mine Field), for a fleet whose waypoint 0 carries Transfer Fleet. The fleet has moved normally earlier that year, so a fleet that reaches its Transfer waypoint during movement is handed over the same year. Nothing happens before movement.
2. *Recipient.* Stored in the waypoint's first instruction word (the word that holds the ironium instruction of a Transport order) as an index among the other players: a value v means player v when v is below the giver's own index, otherwise player v + 1.
3. *Checks, in order.* A refusal posts its message to the giver, clears the Transfer task from waypoint 0 (the fleet keeps any later waypoints) and, when that was its last waypoint, also posts message 78.

   - Recipient is a computer player (slot record bit `0x200`), or the recipient's relation toward the giver is **Enemy**: message 332. Neutral and Friend both allow it, and the giver's own opinion of the recipient is not consulted.
   - Colonists aboard: message 329. Minerals and fuel do not block; they go with the ships.

When no slot of the 16 is free: message 330 to the giver and 331 to the recipient. **So the 16-design limit is checked.**

4. *Success.* A new fleet of the recipient appears at the same position, with waypoint 0 there and no other orders, so it holds at the waypoint until its new owner orders it. It receives all three minerals and the fuel. Each stack's ships go to the matched recipient design with their damage state. A design the recipient lacked is copied whole into the free slot, gets design flag bit `0x80` at `+0x7c` (the bit step 36 sets on designs that fail the race's availability check), has its two ship counters started at the number transferred, and raises the recipient's design count by one; a matched design has its counters raised by the number transferred. **Complete rule (Patrol-mark pass; code-only, not exercised in play; `FUN_1070_374a` `:47182`-`47500`, with raw-byte checks in segment 15).** This replaces the earlier four-item outline, which placed the scan in an unnamed pass, said only confirmed enemies could be targets (wrong: the battle plan decides) and said the new waypoint went to the front of the queue (it becomes the next destination, waypoint 1).

0. *When.* Turn-generation step 39 (`turn-generation-engine.md` §1), only while hosting: for each player in index order, right after that player's visibility pass and just before that player's file is written (never for the host file). Targets are therefore chosen from what that player sees at the end of the generation, and the player meets the result in the new turn, with message 255 (a notice that the patrolling fleet has picked a fleet to intercept), and can still change it before the next generation. The player's fleets are handled one by one in fleet-table order.
1. *Which fleets patrol* (`:47228`-`47235`). Waypoint 0 (the fleet's current position) carries Patrol; or it carries no task while the next waypoint carries Patrol, in which case Patrol and its two settings are first copied onto waypoint 0 (the look-ahead promotion). The fleet is skipped while its next waypoint already targets a fleet, so an intercept already under way is not re-scanned.
2. *Search origin* (`:47239`-`47247`). The fleet's own position, except for a fleet in deep space with more than one waypoint and Repeat Orders on (fleet byte 5 bit `0x02`, the flag the waypoint-advance routine `FUN_1050_642e` uses to recycle a reached waypoint), which searches from its next waypoint's position.
3. *Candidates* (`:47256`-`47292`). Every fleet of another player that this player currently sees (the visibility bit the pass just set). For each, in fleet-table order: (a) the preference test against the best so far: a fleet without the mark (fleet byte 5 bit `0x80` clear) beats every marked one at any distance, and within the same mark state only a strictly nearer one wins (straight-line distance from the origin to the candidate's current position; ties keep the earlier fleet); (b) target type: the candidate must fit the patroller's battle-plan **primary target** type, judged on its hulls by the fleet classifier `FUN_1038_4134` (`:20614`-`20709`), called here in the mode where None, Any and Starbase accept every fleet; (c) relations: the patroller's battle-plan **Attack Who** setting, through the same gate as orbital bombardment (`FUN_10f0_6e2e`, `diplomacy-relations.md` §4): Nobody targets no one, Enemies only fleets of races its owner rates Enemy, Neutrals and Enemies everyone not rated Friend, Everyone all races including Friends, a named player only that player. The best is replaced only by a candidate passing all three, so the outcome is the nearest eligible unmarked fleet, or failing that the nearest eligible marked one.
4. *Mark* (`:47295`-`47298`). If the fleet chosen was unmarked it is marked now, before the range test, so a fleet chosen and then found out of range is marked too. No mark is set while the built-in tutorial runs (`new-game-setup.md` §3).
5. *Range test* (`:47300`-`47309`). Range = (stored value + 1) x 50 ly, so stored 0-9 give 50-500 ly, and stored 10 gives 10,000 ly, effectively unlimited. It passes when the distance is at most the range and not zero; a chosen fleet sitting exactly on the origin gives no intercept and, being nearest, also blocks every other target. Because choice comes first, a far unmarked fleet can win and fail the test while a marked one was in range; the patroller then does nothing this turn.
6. *Intercept leg* (`:47310`-`47407`). A new waypoint becomes waypoint 1, the old later waypoints moving down one place (the waypoint block grows if needed). It aims at the chosen fleet: target kind "fleet", that fleet's id, its current position. When the patroller had only its current-position waypoint, the leg carries Patrol with the same two settings; otherwise it is a copy of the old next waypoint with only the target and speed replaced (in the promotion case that is the Patrol waypoint itself, so again Patrol with the same settings). Speed (raw `1070:3be1`-`3cac`): the leg's own first settings word is read; when it is nonzero its low four bits are the warp, otherwise the fleet's efficient warp is used. In the single-waypoint case that word is the Patrol speed setting, copied from waypoint 0 just before. In the other cases it is the copied next waypoint's own first settings word, which is the Patrol speed only when that waypoint carries Patrol (the promotion case); behind any other task it is whatever that task stores there (for example a Lay Mine Field duration of 5 gives warp 5, a Transport word gives the low four bits of its amount). A fleet with only one waypoint and Repeat Orders on also gets its patrol post appended after the leg: waypoint 2, a copy of waypoint 0 (still Patrol, same settings), always at the efficient warp whatever the speed setting, so it returns to its post afterwards. With further waypoints already present no post is appended. Message 255 goes to the owner: it says the patrolling fleet has picked a fleet to intercept; fields: the patrolling fleet, the target fleet; the patrolling fleet is the message's subject (raw `1070:3ce6`-`3d05`).
   - *Efficient warp* (`FUN_1050_69c2`, `:35873`-`35964`, raw `1050:69c2`-`6b7d`). The occupied stacks are taken in order and each can only lower the running result, which starts at 10. For a stack, the engine is the first engine-category slot of its design; a design without one makes the whole result 0. From the running value downward, the first warp whose fuel-table entry for that engine is 120 or less is taken. Then, **only when the second argument is 0** (the free-speed preference), when that entry is not itself 0, and when the engine is neither the Trans-Galactic Mizer Scoop nor the Galaxy Scoop: if the warp is at least 5 and the engine burns nothing one warp lower, the warp drops by 1; otherwise if it is at least 6 and the engine burns nothing two warps lower, by 2; otherwise if it is at least 7 and it burns nothing three warps lower, by 3. Last, a 10 becomes 9 unless the engine is Interspace-10, Enigma Pulsar, Trans-Star 10, Trans-Galactic Mizer Scoop or Galaxy Scoop. **Patrol uses the variant with the free-speed preference** at all three of its call sites (argument 0, raw `1070:3bf5`, `3c46`, `3c83`). Examples: Trans-Galactic Fuel Scoop, warp 8 (entry 100), lowered to 6 because warp 6 is free (the other variant gives 8); Fuel Mizer, warp 6 (entry 120), lowered to 4; Trans-Galactic Drive, 10 (entry 120) cut to 9; Radiating Hydro-Ram Scoop, 6 (free already).
7. *Afterwards.* No new scan while the leg is pending (item 1). The leg is an ordinary fleet-targeted waypoint. After each movement step the end-of-movement refresh (`FUN_1080_1060`, `:56709`-`56733`) copies the target's current position into every fleet-targeted later waypoint of a fleet with more than one waypoint and no follow mark, so the patroller keeps closing on a moving target, turn after turn, at the leg's speed. In the next step-39 pass the waypoint revalidation (`:47411`-`47500`; *Pursuit*, step 39) turns the leg into a fixed point, with message 40, 41 or 42, once the target is destroyed or no longer seen by the owner. Within one pass the scan runs before the revalidation, so the scan resumes the following year, and a new leg is again inserted at index 1, in front of the stale point. When the patroller reaches the leg's point, the leg's copy becomes waypoint 0 (*Arrival and Repeat Orders*): the fleet goes on patrolling there if the leg carried Patrol (the single-waypoint and promotion cases); behind another next waypoint the leg carried that waypoint's task, which now applies at the catch point, and Patrol stops unless the new waypoint 1 carries it (promotion again). A leg still aimed at a fleet is never recycled (`:56765`-`56771`).
   - *Waypoint structure over a full cycle* (single post, Repeat Orders on). On interception the route is post, leg, post copy. If the leg is reached while still aimed at the fleet it is dropped, leaving leg point, post; at the post the count is 2, so nothing is recycled and the fleet is back to its single Patrol waypoint. If the target was lost first, the leg is a fixed Patrol point and is recycled: point, post, point, then post, point, post, and so on, so the patroller shuttles between its post and the point where it lost the target until a new intercept or a new order changes the route.
   - *While travelling on.* Patrol sits on waypoint 0, which is replaced only on arrival, so a fleet leaving a Patrol waypoint for an ordinary destination keeps scanning every year of the trip (from its own position, or from its next waypoint when it is in deep space with Repeat Orders on, item 2), and an intercept found en route is inserted ahead of the destination. A fleet whose waypoint 0 has no task and whose next waypoint carries Patrol patrols from the moment it sets out (promotion, item 1). Patrol itself neither starts nor prevents a fight: when patroller and target meet, the ordinary battle rules decide (`combat-resolution.md`), under the same battle plan.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 — Fuel burn for a loaded Privateer at warp 8 and warp 9 (from the original community derivation)

Setup: a race with Improved Fuel Efficiency (IFE, −15% fuel use) has a Fuel-Mizer-engined Privateer carrying 3 fuel pods (1400 mg total fuel capacity). Empty hull mass is 80 kT; fully cargo-loaded it weighs 330 kT. At warp 8 the Fuel Mizer's documented Fuel Usage Number (FUN) is 235.

- Fuel per light-year = (330 / 200) x 2.35 x 0.85, about 3.3 mg/ly.
- One-way range on 1400 mg = 1400 / 3.3, about 424 ly.

Now suppose the same ship makes a round trip at warp 9 instead (FUN = 360 at warp 9), loaded (330 kT) outbound and empty (80 kT) on the return leg, and we want the total one-way-equivalent range the 1400 mg tank supports averaged over the full loaded+unloaded round trip:

- Mass moved, averaged over the whole round trip = (330 + 80) / 2 x 2 = 410 kT-equivalent.
- Fuel per light-year = (410 / 200) x 3.60 x 0.85, about 6.27 mg/ly.
- Round-trip range = 1400 / 6.27, about 223 ly (i.e., the ship can go 223 ly out and 223 ly back on one tank).

This shows the double lever a heavier load and a higher warp factor both pull on fuel economy — going one warp faster (8→9) and hauling the return trip's weight into the average very nearly doubles the mg/ly cost per light-year versus the one-way loaded-only figure.

Source: [Guts, §4.5.1 "Guts of Fuel Usage" (attributed to Jason Cawley) — starsfaq.com](http://www.starsfaq.com/advfaq/guts2.htm)

### Example 2 — Combining three scanners on one design

A custom scout design mounts two 100-ly scanners and one 60-ly scanner (for illustration; in the real game most hulls only have one scanner slot type, but multi-scanner combining applies whenever more than one is present, e.g., via a built-in hull scanner plus a mounted component):

- The fourth powers of 100, 100 and 60 are 100,000,000 + 100,000,000 + 12,960,000 = 212,960,000.
- The fourth root of 212,960,000 is about 120.8 ly, the combined range.

The combined range (≈121 ly; the executable truncates it to 120) is only marginally better than either single 100-ly scanner alone, illustrating why stacking multiple scanners on one hull gives sharply diminishing returns compared to just using the single best available scanner.

Source: the design-range routine, §3 "Design and fleet scanner ranges, exact" (code-confirmed); the community help-file mirror page [Scanner Technology — Stars!wiki](https://wiki.starsautohost.org/wikinew/hlp/index_web/148.html), which first supplied this example, is an optional cross-reference.

### Example 3 — Detecting a cloaked scout, and a minefield's independent detection roll

A defending empire has a planetary scanner rated at 150 ly (non-penetrating). An enemy scout arrives 80%-cloaked.

Effective detection range = 150 ly x (100% - 80%) = 150 x 0.20 = 30 ly.

The cloaked scout is invisible to that scanner anywhere beyond 30 ly, even though the scanner's rated range is 150 ly. If that same scout instead flies through a Space Demolition race's minefield, a second and independent detection roll applies regardless of distance to any scanner:

Chance of detection by the minefield, per year spent inside it = 100% - 80% = 20%.

So over, say, 3 years spent transiting the minefield, the scout's chance of being spotted by the minefield alone (ignoring re-rolls' independence caveats) is noticeably higher than a single year's 20%, even though the planetary scanner never sees it at all outside 30 ly.

Source: [Chapter 9: Intelligence — Stars!wiki](https://web.archive.org/web/20210731001838/https://wiki.starsautohost.org/wiki/Chapter_9:Intelligence), [Stars! Strategy Guide, §PG2-06 "Space Demolition" — GameFAQs](https://web.archive.org/web/20210615103545/https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/pc/198797-stars/faqs/41043)
