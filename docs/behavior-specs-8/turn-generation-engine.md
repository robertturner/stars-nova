# Turn Generation Engine

Behavior specification for the "Generate New Turn" pipeline in the 1995-2000 4X game *Stars!* — the batch process that runs once per turn, after every player's orders are collected, to advance the game state and produce the next turn's files. This document is derived directly from the exported client's decompiled turn-processing logic; no public source documents the exact phase ordering or the mechanics below, so this is presented as new material rather than a correction to an existing specification. Facts are described in generic terms; no original identifiers or literal code are used.

## Overview

Turn generation is a single, strictly ordered master routine. It does not resolve all of one player's actions and then move to the next player in a naive way — instead, it runs a fixed sequence of *phases*, each phase sweeping across every relevant object (fleet, planet, race) once, before the next phase begins. This phase ordering matters: a fleet that is destroyed in the battle phase, for example, can no longer be acted on by phases that run afterward. The battle phase runs *after* movement and production, in the second of two stages of the routine; see §1 for the reconciled 40-step order.

## Mechanics

### 1. Phase order (reconciled against the master routine's actual call sequence)

The master per-turn routine is `FUN_10b0_0000` (segment 23, `stars.exe.export.c:71627`-`72460`). **The 23-item list that used to stand here was assembled from a coarse reading and is superseded.** Read statement by statement, the routine has 40 steps in five groups: load and validate (steps 1-11), a *pre-movement* stage and fleet movement (12-17), the economy (18-22), a *post-movement* stage that contains the battle pass (23), and wrap-up, victory check and file output (24-40). The two stages are the same driver, `FUN_10b0_0ebe`, called with 0 and with 1 (`:72067`, `:72174`); the parameter selects which of its sub-steps run. Apart from the shared helpers `FUN_1038_42e2` and `FUN_1070_569a`, the callees named in steps 12-35 have no call site anywhere in the export except this routine and its stage driver (checked by search), so nothing in them is reached from another path, and in particular **combat is not triggered from inside any other pass**: the battle scan `FUN_10f0_24c0` is called only from the post-movement stage (`:72486`), and the scan and resolver it uses (`FUN_10f0_1af8`, `FUN_10f0_5950`) have no other caller.

Nothing is written to disk until step 39 apart from three small exceptions: the optional save of the host client's own pending orders in step 1, step 31's one-bit rewrite of the old host file's header, and step 37's deletes, renames and copies of the previous files. Everything else works on the in-memory game, and any fatal file error before step 39 (including a failed order apply, step 6) jumps to the clean-up at step 40 (the error trap of step 4) with a failure result and no new files. Errors while writing in step 39 are handled per file and do not change the result (see step 39).

**What the starbase "rating" is: the design's cloak percentage.** `FUN_1048_57b6` (`:28428`-`28502`) is the single-design version of the fleet cloak aggregator `FUN_1080_1e02` documented in `combat-resolution.md` §11: it uses the same per-component reader `FUN_1080_20c8` and the same piecewise curve. Raw cloak points are the sum of: **40** if the design is a starbase chassis (hull id 32 or higher) and the race has lesser trait 3, Improved Starbases (`race-traits.md`: "starbases carry an inherent 20% cloak"; 40 points is 20%, `:28441`-`28447`); **300** if the race's primary trait index is 1, Super Stealth (`:28448`-`28452`); and every occupied slot's own points (slot count at `+0x7a`, entries from `+0x3a`; `:28456`-`28478`). The points map to a percentage exactly as in `combat-resolution.md` §11: 0-100 points give points/2; 101-300 give 50 + (points-100)/8; 301-612 give 75 + (points-300)/24; 613-1124 give 88 + (points-612)/64; 1125-1611 give 96, or 97 from 1380 points up; 1612 or more gives 98, the ceiling. One edge case is new: a total that is zero, negative or above **25,000** returns 0 (`:28479`-`28481`), so an absurdly overloaded total counts as "no cloak". The function's optional second output (set to 70 or 80 when a scanner-category slot of subtype 5 or 14 is present, `:28465`-`28473`) is not requested here and no consumer of it was found. So the rating is 0-98, walk 1 stores `100 - cloak%` (2-100) and then its square, **4-10,000, where 10,000 means an uncloaked starbase**.

**What walk 2 caches and who uses it.** `FUN_1038_337e` (`:19841`-`19962`) returns the design's combined scanner range (negative when the design has no scanner; doubled for races with lesser trait 10, No Advanced Scanners) and, through its out-parameters, the second range figure (`+0x8f`, the penetrating/planet-scanning range of `ship-design-and-components.md` §15c), the counter-cloak percentage read from the 18-entry Tachyon table (`+0x91`, `combat-resolution.md` §11) and a small flag byte (`+0x92`: bit 0 from scanner subtype 5, bits 0 and 1 from subtype 14). `FUN_1038_4e50` (`:21277`-`21327`) is a legality check: the hull (ship-hull table below id 32, starbase-chassis table at id - 32) and every occupied slot must pass the shared availability resolver `FUN_1008_5194` for that race; a failing ship design gets flag `0x80`, which this step never clears. The block is read by `FUN_1038_31dc` (`:19727`-`19782`), which, when option bit `0x02` of `DAT_1128_078a` is set (the host run), combines the cache over a fleet's occupied stacks (maximum range, maximum penetrating range, minimum counter-cloak percentage, OR of the flag bits) instead of recomputing per design through `FUN_1038_32be`. Its caller in the fleet scan pass is `FUN_1070_5f00` (`:49325`).

**Readers of offset `+0x87`, complete.** Every literal `0x87` access in the export was checked (the other hits at `:7622`, `:51258`, `:51280`, `:119184`, `:121065` belong to unrelated structures). The offset holds two different fields:

- **Starbase table: the squared cloak factor.** The only readers are four planet sweeps of the per-race visibility pass `FUN_1070_5a2e` (`:49000`-`49022`), which the per-player file writer `FUN_1070_374a` runs at `:47183`, i.e. in step 39, right after step 36 refreshed the field: `FUN_1070_5f00` fleet scanners (`:49470`-`49476`), `FUN_1070_64fc` (`:49764`-`49767` and `:49830`-`49834`) and `FUN_1070_6d7c` mass-packet scanners (`:49998`-`50002`). Each sweep takes a planet already inside the observer's range, and if the planet is owned and has a starbase (planet flag `+5` bit `0x02`) it reads the field from the owner's starbase table at slot `(planet byte +0x2c) & 0xf`. **Field of 10,000 or more (uncloaked): the planet is reported at detail level 3. Otherwise level 3 only if distance-squared is at most field x range-squared / 10,000, else level 2.** Since the field is `(100 - cloak%)²`, that is exactly "the range shrinks to range x (100 - cloak%)/100", the same rule fleets obey. `FUN_1070_537e` (`:48594`-`48643`) records the level on the planet and, for any level other than 2, also marks the starbase design as revealed (`+0x7c` bit `0x01`, seen-level byte `+0x7b` raised to 3). So level 2 means "planet seen, starbase concealed". Unlike the fleet branch (`:49359`-`49364`), no Tachyon counter-cloak multiplier is applied to a starbase's cloak.
- **Ship table: the design weapon value.** See `ship-design-and-components.md` §9 and `ai-opponent-behavior.md` §11: writer `FUN_1038_09ae`, unsquared; readers are the three AI fleet-role predicates `FUN_1090_2db6`, `FUN_1090_2e52` and `FUN_1090_2e94`. These are **not** production or auto-build checks and do not read the squared starbase field. Two further readers, `FUN_1090_0d82` (`:61438`, a stack-count-weighted total of the field over a fleet) and `FUN_10f0_241e` (`:99778`, an ordering comparison of two battle tokens' designs, which via `FUN_10f0_23d8` can reach either table) have no caller anywhere in the export.

**Verdict on the earlier accounts.** (1) "Both tables are squared" was wrong; only walk 1 squares. (2) "Starbase designs only" is right. (3) The "capability rating" is a cloak percentage, and its squared complement is a visibility factor. (4) The AI auto-build reading was wrong twice over: the predicates are fleet-role classifiers in the AI's fleet drivers, and they read the ship table's weapon value, which the AI preamble (not step 36) fills. The writer and readers of the *starbase* field are step 36 and the step-39 visibility sweeps; the writer and readers of the *ship* field are `FUN_1038_09ae` and the fleet-role predicates. `victory-conditions.md`'s victory routine (`FUN_10b8_3bbc`, step 35) does not perform any of this, as previously confirmed: the two are separate pieces of logic that merely run back to back.

**Loops and repeated work.** These are why a flat list was misleading.
- Research buy loop: three times (12e, 20, 23g). Only the step 20 call adds the year's income; the other two spend what is already banked.
- Colonisation and invasion resolution: twice (12d, 23f), which is the mechanism behind `research-tech-tree.md`'s "waypoint-zero invasions, then battles, then waypoint-one invasions".
- Waypoint-task pass `FUN_10b0_3f3a`: four times, modes 1 and 2 before movement and modes 3 and 4 after the battle.
- Movement: up to 11 passes (step 16); follow-order propagation: up to 8 (step 11).
- Waypoint synchronisation (`FUN_1038_42e2`): steps 10 and 29, and again at the tail of the special-object pass when that pass moved something (`:73169`-`73172`).
- Special-object pass: twice, but the two calls are different (mode 0: packets and Trader; mode 1: wormholes and fresh packets), not "two identical passes".

**What is conditional.** The master routine calls every step in the same order on every turn; what varies is whether a step's body has anything to do. Three kinds of condition exist. (1) The stage parameter of `FUN_10b0_0ebe` gates the battle pass, the Trader encounters, the planet-word clear (stage 1 only) and the cargo report (stage 0 only), and sets the task modes (1-2 or 3-4) and the research call. (2) Game-option bits, mapped step by step in §1b: "No Random Events" (`DAT_1128_0080` bit `0x80`) removes the random-event wrapper of step 20 (`:76626`) and the planet-artifact research bounty of steps 12d/23f (`:78022`), and nothing else in the run (not the special-object passes of steps 15 and 21, not the salvage rolls); "Public Player Scores" changes what step 39 writes; "Slower Tech Advances" is inside the research buy loop of steps 12e/20/23g and the salvage reward; the other option bits are read by no step of the master routine. (3) Data and turn-number conditions, all inside the callees or the step's own loop: step 7's notice 259 (turn counter above 10, staggered by race); step 19 (only fleets of Inner Strength races); step 26 (`:78184`-`78215`: the body runs only if some race is Claim Adjuster); step 28 (`:98704`-: skipped unless some race has status bit 2 set **and** the counter is above 9); step 35 (the year gate of the victory settings); step 37 (an existence test per file, which sets the no-orders flag); step 39 (per-file header choice and the score-record test, counter above 19); and, inside step 20, the event-year floors of §11 (comet not before 10, Mystery Trader not before roughly 39, §5). Steps 24, 25, 27, 29, 30, 31, 32, 33, 34, 36, 38 and 40 have no condition at all. The relative order never changes with any option, trait or turn number.

### 1a. Step 6 in detail: how each player's order file is loaded and applied

Line numbers below are `stars.exe.export.c`. Everything here was read from the decompile; the DOS-level and header facts were cross-checked against the raw executable where the decompile was ambiguous.

**Who is processed, and in what order.** Step 5 builds a random permutation of the slot indices (each position swaps with a random position at or after it, `:71785`-`71798`) and step 6 walks it (`:71799`-`71821`), so the order in which slots act is fresh every turn; only the two cargo ledgers (below) and orders that depend on state an earlier slot changed can notice. Every slot is tried, humans, computer players and eliminated races alike; the loop contains no skip test, and a slot with nothing to load simply contributes nothing. A slot is matched to its file **by name only**: `<game name>.x<slot+1>` (format string at data offset `0x988`, `:71803`-`71804`). `DAT_1128_014c`, the "acting race" that every setter and ownership test consults, is set to the slot before the load (`:71805`) and reset to -1 after the loop (`:71822`); before each load the slot's identity-table entry is set to "none" (`:71808`-`71809`). The loader does **not** compare the header's own player field with the slot (`FUN_1070_2dfc` and the ready-status poll `FUN_1020_4bae` do, `:46475`-`46476`; step 6 uses neither).

**Computer players.** Their orders reach step 6 as ordinary `.x` files written earlier: after a host client opens a game or finishes generating a turn, `FUN_1020_3fe6` (`:8266`-`8344`; call sites `:5460`, `:5572`, `:7855`, `:8841`, `:9581`, `:9725`, `:9732`; once per load, guarded by bit `0x400` of `DAT_1128_078a`, which the game discard `FUN_1070_2a80` clears again, `:46209`) walks the slots and, for each computer slot (bit `0x200` of the record word at +2) whose `.x` file does not validate (`FUN_1070_2dfc`, purpose 1), runs the personality dispatcher `FUN_1088_0000` (`ai-opponent-behavior.md` §1), which records its decisions through the same edit path a human uses and ends by saving them as that slot's `.x<N>` file (`:58023`). The master routine has no AI-specific code, and an AI slot without a file is treated exactly like an absent human.

**Loading one file: `FUN_1048_7c66` (`:30660`-`30888`).**
1. Resets the shared change buffer (`DAT_1128_0964` = 0, `DAT_1128_0966` = -1) and installs a local error trap (`:30689`-`30694`).
2. *Source.* In a tutorial game (options bit `0x08`), while hosting (`078a` bit 1) and only for slot 0, the loader first looks for a bundled script resource (type 10000, id `0x2711`): if the turn counter is below the resource's leading count byte, it skips that many groups of records (each group starts at a type-8 header) and reads the next group instead of a file (`:30709`-`30739`); otherwise it drops the resource and reads the file. Which slot the tutorial's human occupies, and the script's contents, were not traced. Any other case opens the file with `FUN_1070_329a(name, 0x4020)`: the `0x4000` flag suppresses the "cannot open" message, and while generating (`078a` bit 9) a failed open is retried every 0.5 s for up to 4 s unless the DOS error is "file not found" (`:46706`-`46756`).
3. A file that does not exist is **not an error**: the open longjmps into the trap, no handle is open, and the loader returns 1 with an empty buffer (`:30695`-`30707`). Every rejection in step 5 ends the same way (return 1, nothing buffered).
4. Records are read by `FUN_1070_31f4` (`:46673`-`46690`): a 2-byte record header (type in bits 10-15, payload length in bits 0-9), then the payload, deciphered unless the type is 0 or 8 (`save-turn-file-format.md` §10). A short read calls `FUN_1070_2d9c(3)` and longjmps (`:46817`-`46820`); the trap closes the file and returns **0**.
5. *Header checks* (`:30749`-`30876`), applied to the fields wherever the first record's bytes land (the loader does not verify that the first record is type 8): (a) the file's game id must equal the host's `DAT_1128_0070`, else silent skip; (b) the file's turn number must not be below the host's `DAT_1128_0082`: a lower number is a stale file from an earlier turn and is silently skipped, a higher one posts error 30 ("log file is more recent than the game ... Ignoring log file"); (c) when the turn numbers are equal, the header's nonce (flag bits 13-15) must equal bits 9-11 of `DAT_1128_0080`, else error 29 ("file is not from this game"). Errors are recorded in `DAT_1128_070e` and displayed only when `DAT_1128_070c` is 0 and the host is not generating (`FUN_1070_2d9c`, `:46387`-`46399`); the master routine sets `078a` bit 1 at step 4 (`:71771`; `070c` is forced to 1 only around the step-2 load and restored at `:71722`), so **no dialog ever appears** from these checks. Two checks that other loaders make are absent here: the header's *submitted* flag (flag bit 0) is not tested, so orders saved without pressing "submit" are applied too (a forced generation still uses whatever a player has saved), and the header's version and kind are not tested.
6. *On acceptance*: the header's version word is stored in `DAT_1128_0710` (`:30754`); header flag bit 12 is copied into `DAT_1128_078c` bit 2 and, when hosting, into bit 1 of the acting race's status byte `DAT_1128_5a16[slot]` (`:30755`-`30760`). This build always writes that bit as 0; what sets it in other files, and what it means, is not pursued (it sits in the status family this project deliberately does not detail, §5), and its only effect seen here is that step 7 exempts a race that carries it.
7. The next record must be the 17-byte identity record (type 9). Its first word is the byte length of the order records that follow; if the record is exactly 17 bytes, 15 of them (an identity block whose content is deliberately not described) are copied into the slot's 16-byte identity-table entry (`:30762`-`30787`). Step 7 alone consumes that entry.
8. Order records are read until their total size (header plus payload) reaches the length from item 7, and are appended, deciphered, to the shared 32,000-byte change buffer `DAT_1128_0960`, each as its 2-byte header plus payload, exactly the layout the replay loop walks (`:30789`-`30804`). Type-40 records (player-to-player messages) are then appended to a linked list rooted at `DAT_1128_0aca` (count `DAT_1128_0ad0`; the first four payload bytes are a link that the loader overwrites, `:30807`-`30847`); nothing in the master routine reads that list again (its readers and writers are the client's message dialog, `:16210`-`16340`, and the orders writer, `:31082`-`31095`).
9. The record after those must be type 0, the end marker. If it is, `DAT_1128_0964` receives the byte length and the result stays 1; if not, `DAT_1128_0964` stays 0, the result is **0**, and nothing from this file is applied (`:30851`-`30856`). The file (or resource) is closed and `FUN_1048_67a6(0)` clears the "orders dirty" state (`:30857`-`30868`).

**Applying: `FUN_1048_6824` (`:29589`-`29621`).** Called right after a load that returned nonzero (`:71811`); with an empty buffer it returns 1 at once.
- *Live state, no working copy.* It sets `DAT_1128_0968` = 1 for the whole replay; the shared append primitive (`:29506`) does nothing while that flag is set, so replaying orders records no new orders, and the decoder's cases call the same setters interactive editing uses.
- It walks the buffer record by record (advancing by each record's own length, `:29607`-`29616`), calls the decoder `FUN_1048_68a8(type, length, payload)` for every one, ANDs the results, never stops early, clears a "modified" bit (`078f` bit 2) and returns the AND. A record type outside the decoder's 22 case labels (`save-turn-file-format.md` §9) ends the switch and counts as success (`:30653`).
- *Ownership.* Opcodes `0x22` (research settings), `0x24`, `0x26` and `0x2e` write straight into the acting race's own record (`:30501`, `:30537`-`30543`, `:30632`); `0x1b` (design edit) fails unless the race field in the payload equals the acting race and the slot index is 0-25 (`:30298`-`30312`); `0x1d`, `0x1e` and `0x23` fail or do nothing for another race's records (`:30392`, `:30461`, `:30517`). The fleet-field opcodes look objects up by id (a fleet id embeds its owner's number, `:17876`), and no comparison of that owner with the acting race was seen among the cases read; the one owner test seen there applies to the *second* object of a two-object edit whose lookup failed: a missing foreign object is tolerated, a missing object owned by the acting race fails the record (`:29991`-`29995`). Other refusals return 0 too, for example an out-of-range list index in opcodes 3-5 (`:29996`-`30012`).
- *Cross-owner cargo.* In host mode a cargo edit whose second object belongs to another race is deferred rather than applied: colonists unloaded onto a planet owned by a different race are added to the pending-landing ledger (`:29776`-`29834`), other cargo moved between objects of different owners goes into the 25-byte ledger (`:29874`-`29970`; a two-object cargo edit is processed in two sweeps over its fields, negative amounts first), and the acting race's own side of the edit is applied at once (`:29755`-`29772`, `:29839`-`29872`). Both ledgers were allocated in step 4 and are settled in steps 12d, 12g and 23f. (Read structurally; not traced record by record.)

**What can abort the run.** Only one thing: a load that returned nonzero followed by an apply that returned 0, i.e. at least one record failed. The master routine then shows a stop-icon message box with dynamic string 1 ("Player log file ... appears to be corrupt, unable to load file") and jumps to the clean-up (`:71811`-`71815`): no later step runs, no file is written, the result is 0, and the orders already applied exist only in memory, which the next load discards. Every other problem merely makes the slot contribute nothing:

| Situation | Loader result | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| no `.x<N>` file (never submitted, AI without a file, eliminated race) | 1, empty buffer | slot contributes nothing; step 37 later marks it "no orders" |
| file from another game | 1, empty | silent skip |
| file's turn number below the host's (stale) | 1, empty | silent skip |
| file's turn number above the host's | 1, empty | error 30 recorded, not shown; skipped |
| same turn, nonce differs | 1, empty | error 29 recorded, not shown; skipped |
| truncated or unreadable file | 0 | skipped (error 3 recorded) |
| no end marker after the orders | 0 | skipped |
| readable file, one record fails | 1, then apply returns 0 | **whole run aborted** |
| same file copied to two slots | each loaded under its own slot | no duplicate-order detection; foreign-owner records are refused only by the opcodes listed above |

**Post-processing after the loop.** In order: `DAT_1128_014c` = -1 (`:71822`), step 7 (identity and penalty flags), step 8 (design normalisation), step 9 (fleet flag reset), step 10 (`FUN_1038_42e2`, waypoint synchronisation), step 11 (follow-order propagation). There is no relation post-pass: a relationship change is opcode `0x26`, which writes the acting race's row directly (`:30543`), and nothing in the executable decays or adjusts relations afterwards (`diplomacy-relations.md` §3).

**State set by step 6.** `DAT_1128_014c` (acting race; -1 afterwards); `DAT_1128_0964`/`0966`/`0968` (change-buffer length, last-record index, replay flag); the slot's 16-byte identity entry (-1 = no file, else the 15-byte block); `DAT_1128_0710` (version word of the last accepted file); `DAT_1128_078c` bit 2 and `DAT_1128_5a16[slot]` bit 1 (both from header flag bit 12); the pending-landing ledger and its count `DAT_1128_59b8`; the 25-byte ledger and its count `DAT_1128_569c`; the player-message list `DAT_1128_0aca`/`0ad0`; `DAT_1128_078e` bit 10 cleared after each replay; plus every game-state change the orders themselves make (waypoints, queues, designs, relations, research settings, names) and the messages those setters post into the message buffer.

### 2. Battle resolution engine

Combat is resolved by a dedicated routine invoked once per location with a detected hostile-race conflict. Its structure:

- Two growable, persistent "battle log" record pools are ensured to exist (allocated on first use).
- A scratch table tallying per-race, per-design-slot ship-stack quantities is built from the fleet ring at that location (a same-location "linked stack" of every fleet present), including the defending planet's starbase if it is a valid combat participant.
- The full combatant roster is built by flattening every ship stack present — one aggregate record per (race, design) pair with quantity > 0 — computing each aggregate's derived combat statistics (armor/shield/cloak/jamming totals, etc.) via shared per-design stat functions.
- **The battle runs for up to 16 rounds** — an explicit, hardcoded loop bound. This directly confirms the round cap already documented (from public sources) in `combat-resolution.md`.
- Within each round, a per-ship "rate"/initiative value is computed with rounding that depends on the round number's parity (odd/even), then combatant firing order is reordered by a shared "priority" computation. Targeting scans distances/ranges across up to 3 weapon-range brackets per shot.
- Damage is logged into the growable event log as it's applied (not computed all at once), and after the round's shots resolve, a resource-salvage step calls into the production-planning subsystem (`ai-opponent-behavior.md` §6-adjacent logic) to decide whether destroyed-ship minerals should be pulled toward nearby friendly production needs.
- After the battle concludes, a message/outcome-selection routine picks from roughly 20 distinct message codes describing the aftermath (destroyed participants, salvage available, etc.) per race present.

**This confirms the 16-round cap directly from the executable, independent of the public FAQ material `combat-resolution.md` was originally built from.** A later, dedicated pass over segment 31 (`FUN_10F0_*`) went further than this section's original scope and did fully decode several of the specific formulas flagged here as unconfirmed — see `combat-resolution.md` §§6 and 8 for the details, summarized here:
- **The accuracy formula is now confirmed and arithmetically cross-checked** against `combat-resolution.md`'s own worked numbers (`FUN_10f0_41ca`, segment 31) — it combines base accuracy, computer bonus, and jam via a subtractive-cancellation-then-branch formula, reproducing all three documented percentages exactly.
- **The armor-quantization scheme's apparent mismatch (7-bit field found vs. 1/512 claimed) is resolved**, not just re-confirmed: the field is a packed 16-bit word whose low 7 bits and high 9 bits serve two different purposes, and the high 9 bits (explicitly clamped to 0–499 in code) give a real granularity of 1/500 — close to, and likely the real source of, the documented 1/512 figure.
- **The energy-capacitor beam-damage bonus's mechanism (not its exact percentage) is now confirmed**, and the "one missile, one kill" salvo-accounting aside was checked against the actual missile damage-application path and found to have no distinct code-level counterpart — the whole-ship-kill division formula appears to govern missile kills the same way it governs beam kills.
- **The beam APN's RangeModifier term and the Battleship-specific firing-order quirk's generality were specifically searched for as part of this pass and not found at the time** (the relevant code was large enough that this was reported as "not found" rather than "confirmed absent"). **Both have since been resolved by a later pass — see `combat-resolution.md` §4/§5/§8:** the RangeModifier term is confirmed absent from the APN formula (a full re-read found the distance value used only as a binary in-range gate, discarded before the APN math), and every hull is confirmed to fire its weapon slots in plain stored array order, not a Battleship-specific special case.

Separately, the battle-replay ("VCR") viewer that players use to watch a completed battle is confirmed to be **pure play-back**: it reads the round-by-round event log built above (token position, a "beam fired" or "missile fired" flag, and a raw damage/delta value per event) and only ever *displays* pre-decided outcomes — it performs no targeting, accuracy, or damage computation of its own. The viewer's own grid-drawing and hit-testing code independently confirms the battle board is exactly a 10×10 grid, with each token's position packed as one 4-bit column and one 4-bit row value.

### 3. Minefields and mass packets

Processed once per turn: mass packets move in the special-object passes (§1 steps 15 and 21, impact included), minefield collisions happen during fleet movement (step 16), and packet and minefield decay is step 18:

- **Mass-packet decay in flight**: a percentage of a packet's mineral cargo is lost each turn, scaled by the packet's warp-speed tier (roughly 10%/25%/50% for increasing speed tiers), and halved for a specific racial trait. On arrival (or when flagged for detonation), a packet's remaining cargo is delivered to (or impacts) planets within its blast radius.
- **Minefield decay and collision** (the first clause of this bullet originally said "regrowth"; see the correction right after it): each turn, minefields lose some mine-unit strength, then two collision passes check every free-flying fleet and every fleet stationed at/orbiting a planet against every enemy minefield in range: a squared-distance test against the field's radius, with damage/mine-loss scaled by field "type" (a heavier field type inflicts roughly a third the normal mine-loss-to-damage ratio of an ordinary field), decrementing the field's strength and possibly destroying it if depleted. Both affected races are notified.

  **Per-turn minefield and mass-packet decay, read in full (segment-24 sweep, this pass).** The routine that runs this pass is `FUN_10b8_433a` (`stars.exe.export.c:79509`-`79658`), called from the master routine at `:72166` just before the production pass. It clears every fleet's "already hit by a field this turn" mark, then walks the special-object table (18-byte records): for **mass packets** it applies the in-flight decay described above (`FUN_10b8_4200`, details in `production-queue.md` §10b) and, for a packet whose stored speed nibble is zero (a resting packet), removes 10% of each mineral (at least 10 kT) per year after a one-turn grace flag is used up, and deletes it at zero; for **minefields** it applies the following. A minefield's yearly loss rate is `2 + 4 x S` percent, where S is the number of stars lying inside the field's circle (`FUN_1118_01a0`, `:114949`-`114992`, counts stars in the sorted star table within the squared radius, which equals the mine count) and the multiplier 4 is replaced by 1 when the field's owner is Space Demolition (PRT 5); the rate is capped at 50%, and raised by 25 percentage points if the field carries the owner's "detonate" flag. The absolute loss is that percentage of the field's mine count, but never fewer mines than the percentage figure itself, and never fewer than 10 unless the field is the third (speed-bump) type; a field whose strength is not above the loss is deleted. Fields carrying the detonate flag first damage every not-yet-hit fleet inside the circle through the shared mine/overgate damage routine `FUN_10b0_312a` and mark those fleets. **No per-turn regrowth of a minefield exists in this pass; strength rises only through mine-laying orders** (§3 mine-laying paragraphs below). The collision passes themselves live in the movement code (segment 23), not in segment 24.
- **Minefields are edited via the same order-queuing mechanism used everywhere else** — a minefield-window command issues a turn order that is applied during this pass, rather than mutating the minefield immediately.
- **Mine-laying rate, confirmed by inspection of the exported client.** A fixed lookup table gives a base per-component mine-laying rate keyed by component category and subtype, with observed values **20, 40, 50, 60, and 70** (units per component per turn, before fleet-wide weighting by ship count); one specific component category instead reads a rate directly from that component's own data rather than the fixed table. A fleet's total mine-laying output is a ship-count-weighted average of its installed mine-layers' rates, passed through a further piecewise scaling curve that flattens toward a cap around **98%** for large fleets — the exact real-world meaning of this final scaling step (a diminishing-returns efficiency factor, distinct from the raw per-component rate table) was not conclusively resolved.

  **Follow-up pass — the rate function itself was read in full and contains no such curve; the "98%" claim could not be relocated this pass.** `FUN_1080_1aea` (`stars.exe.export.c:57210`-`57311`), confirmed as this project's own citation for the per-component rate formula (cross-referenced from `fleet-movement-scanning-cargo.md`'s "Lay Mine Field" entry), was read line-by-line in its entirety: it walks each occupied design slot, accumulates `shipCount × perUnitRate` (with a flat ×2 for one mine-type category, `stars.exe.export.c:73296`-`73298` equivalent logic inline at `57296`-`57298`) into a running total, and returns that total ×10 at the end (`57305`-`57308`) — a plain weighted sum with a flat doubling case, no percentage curve, no asymptotic cap, and no reference to ship count except as the linear weight already described. Reading forward from both of its call sites inside `FUN_10b0_3f3a`'s Lay Mine Field handler (`stars.exe.export.c:75916`, `75937`) turned up one nearby scaling step that *is* real but does not match the "flattens toward 98% for large fleets" description: at `75941`-`75943`, the combined per-type rate is unconditionally **halved** (`lVar18 = CONCAT22(uVar34,uVar14) / 2`) unless a specific fleet-status flag (bit `0x20` of the fleet's status byte, set once per fleet the first time `FUN_10b0_1f8c` visits it that turn, `stars.exe.export.c:73370`) is already set — a flat, binary 50% cut tied to a per-fleet "already had its first pass this turn" flag, not a fleet-size-dependent curve. No other scaling step, percentage table, or asymptotic-cap arithmetic was found between the rate computation and the point the value is written into a minefield's strength field. **This pass could not locate the "flattens toward ~98%" mechanism this document previously described**, and offers this as a genuine, checked negative result rather than a further guess: either that finding belongs to a different code path this pass's search of `FUN_1080_1aea` and its two call sites did not cover, or it was a misreading of the flat ÷2 halving documented here (which is a binary flag gate, not a curve, and does not asymptote toward 98% or any other fleet-size-dependent bound). A future pass should treat the "98%" figure as unconfirmed pending a fresh source.

**Where laid mines are actually applied, confirmed by inspection of the exported client.** This rate formula only produces a per-turn mine-unit *quantity* for a fleet executing a Lay Mine Field order; the previously-missing other half — searching for (or creating) the target minefield and adding that quantity to it — is implemented inside segment 23's `FUN_10b0_3f3a`, documented in `fleet-movement-scanning-cargo.md`'s "Segment 23's largest function" note.

### 4. Colonization simultaneous-arrival and fleet/colony exchange

- **Simultaneous colonization tie-break** (resolved by `FUN_10b8_1ea6`, which runs twice a turn: §1 steps 12d and 23f). When multiple fleets from different races arrive at the same uninhabited location in the same turn, the arrival with the largest accumulated population/quantity wins the colonization race; an exact tie between the largest two contenders results in **no one** colonizing that turn. All races present are notified of the win/lose/tie outcome.
- **Fleet-meets-foreign-colony exchange** (`FUN_10f0_6ea2`, run at the very end of the battle pass, §1 step 23b). Once per turn, for every fleet sitting in orbit of a planet it does not own, where the diplomacy/relations system (`diplomacy-relations.md`) permits a "meeting" and the fleet is neither cloaked nor hostile, a probabilistic exchange of colonists/minerals/fuel occurs between the fleet and the planet. The resulting notification message differs depending on whether the colony belongs to the fleet's own race or a foreign one — the game maintains two parallel sets of outcome messages for this distinction.

### 5. Random turn events

**Correction (segment-24 sweep, this pass): the 13-entry and 6-entry tables described in this section are not per-turn random events.** They are *salvage and reverse-engineering rolls* fired by three specific situations, and the "roughly 50%-plus base chance, once per race per turn" gate below is real but applies per situation. The dispatcher `FUN_10f0_61a2` has exactly six call sites, and none of them is in the per-turn random-event passes: (1) the end-of-battle salvage code in segment 31 (three sites, `stars.exe.export.c:103191`, `103316`, `103433`); (2) the Scrap Fleet task when a fleet is dismantled at a starbase (`FUN_10b0_3f3a`, `:75713`, `:75813`, which produces message 316, "...dismantled for kT of minerals at the starbase... In the process you have discovered a new technology"); and (3) planet capture in the colonisation and invasion resolver `FUN_10b8_1ea6` (`:77896`). Every time, the two tables are first **zeroed and then filled from the designs involved**. `FUN_1080_244c` (segment 22, `:57813`-`57931`) takes each destroyed, dismantled or captured design (for a captured planet, the former owner's six tech levels stand in for the design) and (a) raises each of the six tech-level thresholds to the highest level that any part or hull of the design requires, and (b) adds to the matching slot of the 13-entry table one percentage point per installed unit of a rare part, capped at 25%. The parts that feed the table are those of bits 0-7, 9 and 11 (Multi Cargo Pod, Multi Function Pod, Langston Shell, Mega Poly Shell, Alien Miner, Hush-a-Boom, Anti Matter Torpedo, Multi Contained Munition, Enigma Pulsar and Jump Gate); bits 8 (Mini Morph), 10 (Genesis Device) and 12 are never fed by salvage and can only be reached through the wormhole heavy-cargo route (§9 and `ship-design-and-components.md` §14a). The roll then succeeds only for a part the race does not yet have, and the 6-entry roll only for a field in which the race's level is below the threshold. The result reads exactly as the message texts say: 239 ("Wreckage discovered at your battle... has boosted your research"), 314/315 ("Examination of wreckage from the battle... uncovered plans for a new part / a new ship hull"), 316 (dismantled at a starbase) and, for planet capture, the research-boost text. The rest of this section (the 13-slot bit layout, the 0/12 fallback messages, the 6-slot research-field structure and the reward banking) is unaffected; only the *trigger* changes from "a per-turn random event" to "a salvage situation".

Once per race per turn, gated behind a roughly 50%-plus base chance, the game rolls for **at most one** random empire-wide event from two independent tables:

- A **13-entry table** of percent-chance-weighted events, each with its own enable flag and per-race "already triggered" tracking so a given event fires for a given race only once.
- A **6-entry table** of counter-vs-threshold events that, on success, generate and accumulate a numeric reward.

Only one event total fires per race per turn (a flag prevents both tables from firing the same turn for the same race). Concrete, gameplay-recognizable events identified among these include:

- **A "comet strike"-style event** (roughly 1-in-20 base chance per eligible turn): randomly shifts an eligible planet's three environment axes (Gravity/Temperature/Radiation) by magnitudes in roughly the 50-350 range, and separately adjusts mineral concentration. **(Numbers superseded by §11's "Random-event numbers, corrected against the code": the environment steps are 3-5 points per axis, and 50-350 is the baseline mineral drop.)**
- **A simpler, related environmental-shift event** using the same 1-in-20 gate but a single randomized delta rather than three.
- **A "new mineral deposits discovered"-style event**: chance scales with the game's current year (**corrected in §11: it scales with galaxy size, 1 in 15 down to 1 in 11 per year**), bumps a mineral-concentration value up by roughly 5-20 (capped near the top of the concentration scale).
- **A "Mystery Trader"-style wandering-ship spawn**: gated to no earlier than roughly turn 39, with further modulo-100/128 "special turn" windows or an even-turn-only fallback; spawns a new fleet-like object at a random galaxy position with a partially tech-gated component loadout (higher-tier components suppressed in early appearances, becoming more likely to appear as the game year advances), and broadcasts its appearance to every race.

**Per-race "already triggered" tracking and notification, confirmed by inspection of the exported client.** A dedicated routine (`FUN_1118_1196`, segment 36) takes one event's bit-flag value, ORs it into that race's stored per-race event-history bitmask (confirming the "already triggered" mechanism operates as a persistent per-race bitmask, one bit per distinct event, rather than a counter or timestamp), and maps that same bit value to one specific notification-message code plus a packed icon/detail value — 11 distinct single-bit values were recovered with individually mapped message codes, consistent with (though not conclusively proving an exact count for) the 13-entry table described above. This confirms the per-event notification message a player receives is looked up from a fixed bit-to-message table rather than being freely composed per event.

**The table's exact size and the two remaining unmapped bits, resolved by tracing the dispatcher's own caller.** `FUN_1118_1196`'s only "roll and check" call site (`FUN_10f0_61a2`, segment 31) draws its per-turn event index with `FUN_1040_1652(0xd)` — a bounded random draw over exactly **13** values (0-12) — independently confirming the table's size as exactly 13, not merely "roughly." That same call site ORs `1 << index` into the per-race history bitmask before ever reaching the dispatcher, so the dispatcher's own bit-value switch is indexed identically. Reading `FUN_1118_1196` in full shows it explicitly special-cases bit values `0x0002` through `0x0800` (indices 1-11 inclusive, 11 values, each with its own message code) but has **no dedicated arm for index 0 (bit `0x0001`) or index 12 (bit `0x1000`)** — both of those fall through to the same generic default message code the function also uses for any out-of-table value. In other words, the two entries this document previously flagged as "the remaining ~2 unidentified bits" are now pinpointed precisely: they are the table's first and last entries, and structurally they are distinguishable from the other 11 only in that they share a single generic notification code rather than each getting an individually chosen one — they are not missing from the table, just less individually surfaced by it. Which of the concrete gameplay events named above (if any) these two indices correspond to was not established in this pass.

**Follow-up pass — the shared fallback traced forward to its actual displayed text, with a partial, honestly-caveated result.** `FUN_1118_1196`'s return value (this project's "message code," `0x10b` for the default/fallback case) is not itself the final message-store type code: its one caller, `FUN_10f0_61a2`, adds a fixed `0x2f` to it before logging (`FUN_1030_7444(param_1, CONCAT22(local_8, iVar3 + 0x2f))`, `stars.exe.export.c:103009`), so the fallback's real, stored type code is `0x10b + 0x2f = 0x13a` (314 decimal). `extracted-game-data/message-strings.txt` row 314 reads: *"Examination of wreckage from the battle at \n uncovered plans for a new part to place on your ships."* This is a genuine, gameplay-plausible flavor event (salvaging tech from an old battle site) not previously among this document's four named events, and is the concrete text a player would see for indices 0 and 12 if this reading is right. **The honest caveat, checked directly rather than glossed over:** this exact same type code (`0x10b`/314) is *also* what 9 of the *other* 11 individually-mapped bit values (every mapped case except bit `0x100`, which instead yields type code `0x10c`/315 — "...uncovered plans for a new ship hull" — and bit `0x400`, which yields `0x10f`/318 — a ship-dismantled/tech-discovered variant) resolve to as well; only the packed icon/detail parameter passed alongside it (`param_3`, e.g. `0xcb04`, `0xc608`, `0xcc04` for the default case) differs between these 9 slots and the fallback. So at the message-*type* level, indices 0 and 12 are not uniquely distinguished from most of the table's other entries — they share the same generic "battle-wreckage" template text, differing only in a numeric detail/icon parameter whose own meaning (which system, which specific part) was not decoded this pass. This is offered as a real, traced answer to "what text/context does the fallback path carry" (as asked), while being explicit that it does not uniquely fingerprint indices 0/12 against the rest of the table by severity or icon alone.

**Follow-up pass — a systematic review of message-strings.txt confirms no hidden distinct text exists for indices 0/12, but a separate, already-completed cross-document finding answers what they concretely *grant*, which is more informative than text-matching could be.** `FUN_1118_1196`'s own body (`stars.exe.export.c:115815`-onward) was already read in full by a prior pass (see `ship-design-and-components.md` §14a) and shown to be an exhaustive `if`/`else if` chain with individually-assigned messages for bit values `0x02`-`0x800` (indices 1-11) plus one bit-0-and-everything-else fallback — a hard, code-level proof, not an inference, that indices 0 and 12 mechanically cannot reach any text other than the row-314 fallback already traced above; no amount of further message-string review can turn up a "hidden" distinct text the dispatcher itself is not wired to produce. A scan of every other random-event-flavored string near row 314 in `message-strings.txt` (rows 300-320) turns up only the already-attributed rows 314/315/318 and unrelated combat/construction text, confirming this negative directly rather than by code inspection alone.

What the systematic pass *does* newly connect is more useful than a text match: `ship-design-and-components.md` §14a independently discovered (while investigating an unrelated question, the ship-design "ability" gate `FUN_10d8_4b8e`) that this exact per-race bitmask and dispatcher pair is **also** the record of a **one-time, per-race, per-component random component grant** — reused verbatim from a second call site, the wormhole heavy-cargo-transit routine `FUN_1118_0784` (this document's own §9, `stars.exe.export.c:115653`), which independently re-rolls `FUN_1040_1652(0xd)` (0-12) until it lands on a bit not already set in the same field, then calls the identical `FUN_1118_1196`. `FUN_10d8_4b8e` (`stars.exe.export.c:91837`-`91913`) reads the same 13-bit field and maps 12 of its 13 possible bits (`0x0001` through `0x0800`, i.e. **indices 0-11**) to 12 specific, named components (Multi Cargo Pod, Multi Function Pod, Langston Shell, Mega Poly Shell, Alien Miner, Hush-a-Boom, Anti Matter Torpedo, Multi Contained Munition, Mini Morph, Enigma Pulsar, Genesis Device, and Jump Gate, in bit order — see that document's §14a table), each becoming buildable for a race only once its corresponding bit is set. **This directly identifies index 0's real gameplay effect, despite it sharing the generic fallback notification text with 9 other indices: winning index 0 grants the race the Multi Cargo Pod component.** Index 12 (`0x1000`) falls *outside* this 12-bit component table entirely (which only spans bits 0-11), matching `ship-design-and-components.md` §14a's own conclusion that "the 13th slot most plausibly grants a bare tech-level bump with no attached component" — consistent with index 12 carrying no named-component payload and, like index 0, only ever surfacing the generic wreckage-flavor text. This resolves the practical version of the open question (what do indices 0 and 12 *do*, not just what text they print) more concretely than a string-matching pass could have: index 0 = Multi Cargo Pod grant; index 12 = a component-less reward, plausibly a raw tech bump. `ship-design-and-components.md` §14a should be treated as the canonical source for this mechanism's component side; this document's own framing of the 13-entry table as purely "empire-wide flavor events" undersells it — several of its slots are one-time part/hull unlocks, not narrative-only events.

**A fourth confirmed event, reconciled from `combat-resolution.md` §8's "genuinely new, uncategorized mechanic" flag: a racial-trait-gated fleet/planet attrition event, structurally distinct from the two tables above.** A prior pass sampling segment 31 found a per-turn routine that reduces fleet cargo-like fields and planet mineral-concentration-adjacent fields by a random percentage, and flagged it as unreconciled with this section rather than merging it speculatively. Tracing its one call site and internals resolves it as follows:

- **Not part of either the 13-entry or 6-entry table above.** It is invoked directly from the master turn routine as its own step, (`FUN_10f0_111a`, §1 step 28) shortly before the turn/year counter increment (step 33) — i.e., it runs once per turn near the very end of the simulation, after repair and terraforming, and is unrelated to the special-object passes (steps 15 and 21) that the old phase list mislabelled as the home of the random events. It also carries no "already-triggered" bit — unlike every event in the two tables above, it can fire repeatedly for the same race across many turns, not just once per game.
- **Gating.** The whole routine is skipped unless at least one race in the game has a specific stored per-race flag (bit 2 of a per-race status byte, structurally similar to — but a different field from — the per-race relationship/trait bytes documented in `combat-resolution.md`'s "legitimate enemies" note); which named racial trait this corresponds to was not established (no surviving string data). It is further gated on the turn/year counter exceeding a fixed value of 9 — i.e., it cannot fire before turn 10, an early-game grace period consistent in shape with the "roughly 50%-plus base chance...gated" framing used for the two tables above, though this event uses a hard turn floor rather than a percentage chance for its outer gate.
- **Fleet effect.** For every fleet belonging to a gated race that is not already flagged removed: a 1-in-12 roll flags the fleet as destroyed/removed outright and logs one message code; otherwise, three consecutive stored numeric fields on the fleet (a cargo-like triple, offset and stride consistent with a 3-mineral-type cargo hold) are each independently reduced by a random 10-20% (a single per-fleet roll of 10 + random(11), reused for all three fields), with a floor of at least 1 unit lost per field if the field is positive; if any field was reduced, a second, distinct message code is logged.
- **Planet effect.** For every planet whose owner race is gated: most of the time, a further 1-in-15 roll reduces one randomly-chosen field of a different stored triple (offset and stride consistent with mineral-concentration-adjacent storage, distinct from the fleet-side triple) by a random 5-45% (5 + random(41)), logged via its own message code; in the remaining, rarer case (roughly 1-in-8, reachable only when that planet's triple is not already zeroed), a packed sub-field embedded in two adjacent stored bytes is instead reduced by a random 5-35% (5 + random(31)), with a floor of 1, logged via yet another distinct message code.
- **Four distinct message-log notification codes in total** are used across the fleet-destroyed, fleet-field-reduced, and two planet-side branches, each appended through the same message-record-store append routine documented in `client-ui-dialog-catalog.md`'s "Messages" section — confirming this event surfaces to the player exactly like an ordinary logged event, not silently.

This is a real, fourth confirmed per-turn random-effect mechanism, additional to the two event tables already documented above — not a variant or extension of either. `combat-resolution.md` §8 has been updated to note the reconciliation and point here rather than carry an open item.

**Follow-up pass — the gating "racial trait" identified, and the premise revised: it is not a Primary or Lesser Racial Trait at all.** The routine itself is `FUN_10f0_111a` (`stars.exe.export.c:98704`-`98846`), confirmed by direct inspection to match every previously-documented behavior above line-for-line (the per-race gate, the turn-10 floor, the 1-in-12 fleet-destroy roll and its message, the shared-percentage three-field cargo reduction, and the two planet-side branches). Its gate reads, per race: `local_2c[i] = (byte)(DAT_1128_5a16[i*0xc0] >> 2) & 1`, i.e. **bit 2 (mask `0x4`) of the per-race status byte `DAT_1128_5a16`** (`stars.exe.export.c:98736`-`98738`) — confirming the bit position this document already cited. Cross-referencing this exact field against every other reader/writer of `DAT_1128_5a16` in the decompile (60+ sites) shows it is *not* the PRT-index field (that is a separate field, per-player record offset `0x3e`, read through the shared `FUN_10e0_222c`/`FUN_10e0_226e` accessors documented throughout this project — e.g. `race-traits.md` §1a, `ship-design-and-components.md` §8) and is not an LRT bitmask either (LRTs are read through the same `0x4e`-offset 32-bit field `research-tech-tree.md` and `race-traits.md` both cite for Generalized Research/Ultimate Recycling/Bleeding Edge Technology). `DAT_1128_5a16` is instead a general-purpose per-race status byte with several unrelated bits (bit 0 gated dozens of unrelated per-race checks throughout the client; bit 1 is §9's own gating bit, see the revision there; bit 3 is the "already fired a random event this turn" flag `FUN_10f0_61a2` itself sets, `stars.exe.export.c:103011`/`103045`; bit 4 appears once more, unexamined this pass) — a scratch/flags byte, not a trait table.

**What bit 2 concretely does elsewhere strongly suggests it is a copy-protection/integrity status flag, not a gameplay trait.** The identical bit, on the identical field, is independently tested at `stars.exe.export.c:73381` (inside `FUN_10b0_1f8c`, see §10's new note below) where — on a turn-staggered, per-race roll — it logs message type `0x102`, which `extracted-game-data/message-strings.txt` row 258 gives as *"`\s` has refused to move, doubting your authority to rule. Press the 'Goto' button to enter your UNIQUE Stars! serial number and assert your authority."* This is the same "doubting your authority" family as message rows 257 (productivity/growth penalty, also a serial-number prompt) and 259 (fleet-movement strike, also a serial-number prompt) — a cluster of penalty notifications this project has not previously traced to code, sitting alongside the two copy-protection notices (`dynamic-string-table.md`'s identifiers 279/386) this project has already, deliberately, chosen only to note the existence of rather than explain. Bit 2's write site was not traced (consistent with this project's standing house rule never to document how the Serial Number/tamper check itself is computed), but its *read* sites are unambiguous: this is a status/penalty flag family, not a Primary or Lesser Racial Trait, and the attrition mechanic's "racial trait" framing above should be read as superseded by this finding — it is gated by a copy-protection-adjacent per-race status bit, consistent in shape with the other documented penalties in the same message family (reduced productivity, halved growth, refused movement, fleet strikes — fleet/planet attrition is a plausible fifth penalty in the same cluster). Per this project's standing rule, no further detail on how or when the flag is set is pursued here.

**The 6-entry counter-vs-threshold table's contents, resolved by tracing its second roll in full.** `FUN_10f0_61a2`'s second roll (`stars.exe.export.c:103016`-`103049`, immediately following the 13-entry-table roll documented above) draws a field index 0-5 with `FUN_1040_1652(6)`, then compares a per-race, per-index byte counter (stored at `race*0xc0 + index + 0x59dc`) against a global per-index threshold byte (`DAT_1128_257c[index]`). On success, `FUN_10d8_1580(index, counter+1, race)` (`stars.exe.export.c:89438`) computes a reward equal to `(sum of the race's six tech-field counters) * 10 + costTable[counter+1]`, where `costTable` is a per-level dword lookup at `0x630` shaped like the tech level-cost constant referenced by `research-tech-tree.md` §3's `totalLevels` formula; the raw reward is then scaled by a per-race-trait modifier (`FUN_10e0_222c` checked against trait value `index+8`) and doubled outright if a global option bit (`DAT_1128_0080 & 2`) is set. The result is accumulated — not overwritten — into a persistent per-race, per-field 32-bit total (`DAT_1128_59e2`/`DAT_1128_59e4`, word stride `0x30` per race) and a message is logged (type code `0xfffe`) via the shared message-append routine documented in `client-ui-dialog-catalog.md`'s "Messages" section. **Important caveat:** the per-race, per-field counter this roll gates on and reads (`0x59dc`) is never incremented anywhere in this code path — only compared — so this event does not appear to actually grant a tech level; it produces and banks a resource-like reward value whose ultimate real-world consumption (e.g. feeding into the research buy loop, versus being a pure flavor/bounty number) was not traced further this pass. The same `0x59dc` counters and `DAT_1128_59e2`/`DAT_1128_59e4` accumulator are also read (not written) by an unrelated debug/inspection routine (`FUN_10d8_4a86`/`FUN_10d8_49c6`, `stars.exe.export.c:89753`-`91750`) that is not reachable through normal turn generation.

**Resolved — the banked reward is genuinely spent, not merely logged.** `DAT_1128_59e2`/`DAT_1128_59e4` (the accumulator this event banks its reward into) is the same per-race, per-field memory `research-tech-tree.md`'s already-documented ordinary research buy-loop, `FUN_10b8_4ce4`, reads as its **starting spendable pool for that field, that turn**: at `stars.exe.export.c:80069`-`80072`, the buy loop takes `local_20 = (undefined4*)((int)(undefined2*)&DAT_1128_59e2 + iVar10)` and immediately reads `*local_20` as `local_e`, the pool value its "price the next level, subtract, loop" logic (already described in that document's "Exact overflow/carryover rule" note) spends down. ### 7. Research allocation

Several times per turn (the buy loop `FUN_10b8_4ce4` runs at §1 steps 12e, 20 and 23g, and only the step 20 call adds the year's income; the others spend what is already banked), per race, accumulated research resources are applied to whichever field(s) the player has selected (see `research-tech-tree.md`), advancing tech levels and firing a level-up notification. Any resource surplus left over after an initial allocation pass is redistributed in a second pass, so a turn's research spending is not strictly capped by the exact cost of the next level in the primary field — genuine overflow carries within the same turn into the next field or the next level.

### 8. Turn-generation UI entry points, confirmed by inspection of the exported client

The loop polls the keyboard each iteration and lets the player abort the batch early by holding down a key — there is no separate "cancel batch" dialog, just a held key.

### 9. Fleet-linked habitat-tolerance drift (one specific primary trait)

**Correction from the phase-order reconciliation (§1): the routine that triggers this mechanic, `FUN_1118_0784`, is the Mystery Trader encounter routine, not a wormhole cargo check.** It walks the special-object table for entries of category `0x60`, which §10's "Resolved" paragraph identified as the Mystery Trader, and looks for fleets sharing the Trader's position (`stars.exe.export.c:115309`-`115335`). The messages it posts confirm this: 264 ("The Mystery Trader has refused to give the captain of \s an audience. It may be due to an insufficient quantity of minerals", for a fleet under 5,000 kT, `:115343`-`115346`), 265/266 ("...absorbed by the Mystery Trader... given you \i technology advances", `:115430`-`115436`), 270 ("...unable to teach you anything new") and 280 ("...still recovering from the last transaction with you", the per-race "already dealt with the Trader" bit at `:115364`-`115368`). The 5,000 kT threshold, the 1,200 kT tier steps, the 26-or-10 tolerance-sum gate, the 1-in-5 branch and the drift loop described below are therefore parts of the Mystery Trader's mineral-for-technology trade (its reward rolls draw from the one-time component-grant bitmask of §5), and the phrases "wormhole", "wormhole malfunction" and "per-wormhole bitmask" used in this section and in `fleet-movement-scanning-cargo.md` should be read as "Mystery Trader". The routine runs only in the post-movement stage (§1 step 23c); its whole body is skipped when called from the pre-movement stage.

A per-turn pass, gated to run only when at least one race in the game has a specific primary racial trait, drifts that race's stored per-gas habitat-tolerance value by exactly **±1 per turn** (clamped to the 1-99 range) for fleets meeting certain field thresholds, under three combined random gates (roughly 1-in-3, 1-in-10, and 1-in-1000 chance checks together with threshold comparisons), notifying the race when a shift occurs. A related calculation can also adjust per-gas values directly. This reads as a "race's tolerance genome gradually shifts based on fleet exposure" mechanic tied to one specific PRT, not previously documented anywhere; which named trait this is was not confirmed (no string data survives to check against `race-traits.md`'s PRT table).

**Trigger identified, cross-confirmed against `fleet-movement-scanning-cargo.md`'s "Heavy-fleet transit arithmetic" note.** This is the *same* drift routine invoked from the fleet-vs-wormhole heavy-cargo check documented there: a fleet carrying 5000+ kT of minerals through/at a wormhole computes a tier (6-10, one step per further 1200 kT, adjusted down by the transiting race's own gas-tolerance-sum), and that tier becomes this pass's per-iteration count for the drift loop described above — heavier mineral cargo means more drift iterations for that race this turn. The "gated to run only when at least one race has a specific primary racial trait" condition in that same code path corresponds to a race whose *entire* 6-value gas-tolerance array already exceeds a threshold (a broadly gas-tolerant race), which is instead diverted to a separate 20%-chance fleet-relocation branch rather than the drift loop — so the drift mechanic itself runs for *ordinary* (non-exempt) races, triggered specifically by heavy mineral-cargo wormhole transits, not by a flat per-turn roll independent of fleet activity as the "per-turn pass" framing above might suggest.

**Resolved by a later pass, and the "specific primary racial trait" premise overturned — cross-referenced here from `fleet-movement-scanning-cargo.md`, which had already settled this independently.** Reading `FUN_1118_0784` (`stars.exe.export.c:115255`-`115814`) directly at the threshold-selection point (`stars.exe.export.c:115354`) shows the "26 or 10" gas-tolerance-sum threshold used by the "broadly gas-tolerant race" exemption above is not a PRT-index comparison at all: `local_1a = (-(uint)((DAT_1128_5a16[race*0xc0] & 2) == 0) & 0x10) + 10` — the threshold is chosen by **bit 1 (mask `0x2`) of the same per-race status byte, `DAT_1128_5a16`**, this document's §5 "fourth event" gating bit now identifies as a copy-protection/status field rather than a trait table (see §5's new finding there). No call to the confirmed PRT-index accessor (`FUN_10e0_222c`/`FUN_10e0_226e`, reading player-record offset `0x3e`) appears anywhere in this threshold-selection code path, and any race of any PRT can enter the "all six clear" exemption branch if its own rolled tolerance values happen to be wide enough. **This means the drift mechanic documented in this section is not gated by a Primary Racial Trait at all** — the premise this section originally set out to resolve does not hold up under direct inspection. `DAT_1128_5a16` bit 1's own write site was not traced (per this project's standing rule around the copy-protection-adjacent status bits on this same field), but by analogy with bit 2's confirmed role (§5) and this field's other observed uses — gating a reduced research-level cap at `stars.exe.export.c:80138`-`80145`, and a percentage adjustment inside population-growth-adjacent code at `20983`/`76404` — bit 1 is plausibly a second, related status/penalty flag on the same byte rather than an independent gameplay trait; this reading is offered as well-evidenced, not certain. This section's title and framing ("gated to run only when at least one race has a specific primary racial trait") should be read as superseded: the mechanic is a per-race tolerance-data comparison, modulated by this status-byte bit, not keyed to a named PRT.

### 10. The master routine's concrete identity, confirmed by inspection of the exported client

The 40-step pipeline of §1 is not spread across an unidentified set of routines: it is implemented, concretely, by a single function plus five sibling routines all living in one segment, previously catalogued in `code-coverage-report.md` and `new-game-setup.md` §7 under the mischaracterization "new-game start commit." That routine is confirmed (by its four call sites — the manual "Generate Turn" command and the batch 10/100/1000-turn commands of §8 above) to be the client's actual master turn-generation entry point, reused verbatim to produce a new game's first turn and every later turn. Of its five sibling routines in the same segment:

- One is the two-stage driver `FUN_10b0_0ebe` (§1 steps 12 and 23): a per-planet ownership-flag refresh, and, depending on the stage, the battle pass, the Mystery Trader encounter routine (§9 correction; the ~560-line routine formerly labelled a fleet-vs-wormhole cargo-transit routine), the four waypoint-task passes, colonisation and invasion resolution, research, and the cargo-transfer report.
- One implements the per-fleet docking/fuel pass (`FUN_10b0_1d6e`, §1 step 22): a fleet docked at a friendly starbase is refuelled to full, and any other fleet gains 50 mg per Anti-matter Generator and 200 mg per Fuel Transport or Super-Fuel Transport ship. (The earlier reading of this pass as ramscoop-engine fuel was wrong.)
- One hosts the already-documented **waypoint-task execution / Stargate cargo-transit-restriction** mechanic (`fleet-movement-scanning-cargo.md`'s "Waypoint-task feasibility" and Stargate sections), confirmed to be reached through this same master routine rather than a separately-running turn-loop function. **Reconciliation note:** this routine, `FUN_10b0_1f8c`, is the *fleet movement* pass (§1 step 16, which runs before production); the Stargate feasibility check and the forced cargo dump it contains are part of a fleet's movement leg, and the task execution proper (Transport, Scrap, Merge, Lay Mines, remote mining, Transfer) is `FUN_10b0_3f3a`, run four times by the stage driver. It also contains a further large per-fleet block (mining/fuel-capacity-style bookkeeping with a per-slot RNG-gated stochastic pass) whose exact real-world mechanic was **not** conclusively identified this pass; it superficially resembles §9's habitat-drift shape (per-gas-type loop, comparable RNG odds) but is a separate, unconfirmed piece of logic — §9's own drift mechanic's traced call chain runs through segment 36 (`FUN_1118_0784`, see `fleet-movement-scanning-cargo.md`), not through this function, so no correction to §9 is being made here.

  **The stochastic sub-block itself, structurally identified this pass.** This routine is `FUN_10b0_1f8c` (`stars.exe.export.c:73289`-`74079`); its per-slot RNG-gated block sits at `stars.exe.export.c:73472`-`73560`. It is gated to fleets whose hull/design record carries a specific "class" byte (the high nibble of a byte at offset 6 within the hull record reachable from the fleet, read as `*(byte*)(hull+6) & 0xf0`) equal to `0xa0` — a value distinct from the `0xb0` class checked immediately nearby in the same function for starbase-like hulls, but whose real-world hull category was not otherwise identified. For such a fleet, the block walks up to 16 component slots, skipping component-type codes `7`, `8`, `9`, `0xe`, and `0xf` (categories excluded from this effect), and for every remaining installed unit in a slot rolls `FUN_1040_1652(10)` — a flat 1-in-10 chance of that individual unit being lost this turn. Per-slot losses are tallied; if every slot's quantity is reduced to zero across the whole design, the fleet is flagged destroyed/removed (bit `4` of its status byte) and a distinct message (type code `0xe1`) is logged through the shared message-append routine; if only some components are lost, the fleet's derived combat/capability statistics are recomputed (`FUN_1050_6e52`) rather than the fleet being removed. This reads as a genuine, previously-undocumented **per-component attrition/breakdown mechanic** — structurally distinct from all four random events already catalogued in §5 and from §9's habitat-drift mechanic — gated to one specific, unidentified hull class. Which hull class carries byte value `0xa0`, and therefore which ships this attrition actually applies to, remains unresolved.

  **Follow-up pass — the exact byte address was pinned down precisely, but the cross-reference against `extracted-game-data/component-stats.tsv` this raised did not resolve the hull name.** The "`hull+6`" phrasing above is a `long*`-stride artifact of how the decompiler typed the pointer, not a literal byte offset of 6: tracing the pointer chain from the fleet record shows `local_50` (the variable actually indexed) is computed as `designBase + 4` (`stars.exe.export.c:73375`-`73377`, `designBase` itself read as a cached far pointer stored directly in the fleet record at byte offset `0x64`), and `*(byte*)((long*)local_50 + 6)` therefore reads the byte at `designBase + 0x1c` (28 decimal) — the same byte independently confirmed at line `73380` (`*(byte*)(iVar8 + 0x1c) & 0xf0`, `iVar8` being `designBase` itself). So the real target is **record-relative offset `0x1c`** of whatever `designBase` points to, not offset 6.

  Whether `designBase` is the per-owned-design (147-byte, `ship-design-and-components.md` §1) record or the static per-hull-*type* definition table (`extracted-game-data/component-stats.tsv`'s "Ship hulls"/"Starbase chassis" combined 37-entry, 143-byte-stride table, per `ship-design-and-components.md`'s own §"hull-type index" note) was not conclusively determined this pass — the fleet record's cached far pointer could plausibly be either, and both are 143-147 bytes. Checking the raw offset against the static table's own documented layout does not yield a clean answer either way: the table's universal per-category header only defines offsets `0`-`7` (an ordinal plus six tech-prerequisite bytes) before the 32-byte name field begins at offset `8`, so offset `0x1c` (28) falls **inside that name-text region** for every one of the TSV's 32 extracted hull records (whose six prerequisite bytes are all `0`, confirming the name field truly starts at `8` for this category) — not a plausible location for a single classifying "hull class" byte. This suggests either (a) `designBase` is not this static table at all, or (b) the 143-byte hull/starbase-chassis table's internal layout diverges from the generic per-category header past the shared 8-byte prefix (consistent with the TSV's own header note that "hull records additionally carry a slot array" and other extra structure beyond the generic layout). Cross-referencing `component-stats.tsv` as instructed therefore did not identify a named hull this pass — it narrowed the question (the real offset is `0x1c`, not `6`) without answering it, and is recorded here as a well-evidenced negative result rather than a resolution.

  **Resolved by tracing the pointer's origin at its call sites precisely, as instructed — and the premise of the original either/or question turns out to be false.** `designBase` (the fleet record's cached far pointer at byte offset `0x64`) is **neither** the static hull-definition table **nor** the per-owned-design 147-byte record directly — it is a small, **per-fleet, heap-allocated, growable array of design-stack entries**, private to that one fleet record, unrelated to either fixed table by storage location. This was established by tracing every site that allocates, resizes, or copies this exact field (all identifiable by the identical `+0x32`-word/`+0x64`-byte offset into the same global owned-fleet array `DAT_1128_00ba` that `ai-opponent-behavior.md`'s "Process-of-elimination pass" independently confirms this project's fleet table to be):
  - **Fleet creation** (`FUN_1038_1f72`, `stars.exe.export.c:18648`-`18743`) allocates this field fresh for every new fleet via a generic array allocator, `FUN_1060_057e(0x12, 3, 0)` (`stars.exe.export.c:18717`-`18720`) — 3 slots of 18 (`0x12`) bytes each, plus a 4-byte header recording the per-slot size and a slot count/capacity — and immediately populates the first slot (at `designBase+4`) with the new fleet's map position and its raw **design *index*** (`puVar5[3]`, the small integer 0-25 slot number passed in as the design to build, not a pointer) at `designBase+8` (`stars.exe.export.c:18722`-`18727`). A design index, not a pointer, is exactly what one would expect this array to cache if it exists to let a fleet track *which* of a race's design slots each of its (possibly several, once merged) ship stacks belongs to — not to directly address either candidate table.
  - **Fleet splitting** (`FUN_1038_2180`, `stars.exe.export.c:18747`-`18801`) and **fleet merging** (`FUN_1038_1582`, `stars.exe.export.c:17908`-`18000`) both treat this field as fleet-owned memory needing a deep copy, not a shared reference: they read a per-unit-size byte embedded in the block's own header (`*param & 0xff`, set to `0x12` at creation) to compute a new byte count, call a realloc-style allocator (`FUN_1060_053e`, `stars.exe.export.c:42459`-`42472`) to grow the *destination* fleet's own copy of the array, and then `memcpy` the *source* fleet's entries into it slot-for-slot (`stars.exe.export.c:17961`-`17979`, `18779`-`18797`). `FUN_1038_1582` (Merge Fleets) additionally compares the two fleets' `designBase` pointers for simple equality to decide "same design vs. different design" (`stars.exe.export.c:17953`) — this by itself rules out the static hull table as the referent, since two different owned designs sharing one hull *type* would incorrectly compare equal under that scheme, while it is consistent with either the per-owned-design table (which this per-fleet array does *not* turn out to be) or, as traced here, a fleet-private cache addressed by design identity. If `designBase` pointed into either shared, fixed-position table, none of this resize/deep-copy machinery would be needed at all — a bare pointer copy would do, exactly as the surrounding ~100 bytes of each fleet record *are* copied verbatim in the same functions (`stars.exe.export.c:17983`-`17989`).

  Net result: the attrition mechanic's hull-class byte at `designBase+0x1c` is read from inside this private per-fleet array (specifically, from 24 bytes past the start of its first 18-byte entry — which, given the entries' own 18-byte stride, actually falls at the start of the *second* entry, byte offset 6 within it), not from a byte that this project's two extracted tables (`component-stats.tsv`'s static hull table, or the per-owned-design record) can be indexed into directly. This also newly explains the immediately-preceding gate this same block applies, `puVar17[0x31] < 2` (`stars.exe.export.c:73379`): fleet-level word `0x31` mirrors this same array's own entry count, so the gate is plausibly "this fleet must be carrying at least two distinct merged-in ship designs" before the second entry it inspects is even populated — i.e. the attrition mechanic, on this reading, specifically inspects a fleet's *second* merged-in design stack rather than its primary one. This narrower point (which entry index, and why specifically the second one) was not independently confirmed beyond the offset arithmetic above and is offered as a plausible but unverified corollary, not a certainty; the main question this pass was asked to settle — static table vs. per-owned-design record — is resolved: it is neither.

  **Follow-up pass — the exact line pair re-read directly, corollary upgraded from "plausible" to directly confirmed.** `stars.exe.export.c:73378`-`73380` reads as a single compound skip-condition: `if ((*(byte*)(iVar8+10) & 0xf) == 1 || ((*(byte*)(iVar8+10) & 0xf) == 6 || puVar17[0x31] < 2 || (*(byte*)(iVar8+0x1c) & 0xf0) == 0)) goto LAB_10b0_1fd7;` — i.e. `iVar8` (`designBase`) is read fresh at the top of this exact statement (`iVar8 = *(undefined4*)(puVar17+0x32)`, `stars.exe.export.c:73375`, the fleet's cached far pointer at byte offset `0x64` already traced above), and the `puVar17[0x31] < 2` entry-count gate and the `*(byte*)(iVar8+0x1c) & 0xf0` class-byte read sit in the *same* OR-chain, evaluated together rather than in separate, independently-reached statements. This directly confirms — by reading the two conditions side by side rather than by separately reconstructing their offsets — that the class byte at `designBase+0x1c` (the second design-stack entry's own byte 6) is only meaningfully consulted once the entry-count gate has already established that a second entry exists; the corollary in the paragraph above is correct as stated, not merely plausible.

  **Final resolution (segment-24 sweep, this pass): the private array is the fleet's waypoint list, "hull class `0xa0`" is Warp 10, and this block is the warp-10 engine-strain rule.** Three independent facts pin this down. (1) Fleet creation, `FUN_1038_1f72`, receives the *planet id* of the building planet as its second argument (from the queue-completion routine, `stars.exe.export.c:77146`, where it is passed the planet's own id), not a design index, and writes it into the first 18-byte entry beside the spawn position — so the entries are waypoint records (target object id, x/y, task/warp byte; `fleet-movement-scanning-cargo.md`), and the "design index" reading above was a mislabel. (2) `FUN_1080_13f8` (`:56865`-`56989`), called for a newly built ship when the starbase has a rally destination (`:77153`), sets the entry count to two and fills the second waypoint with the rally target and the highest warp the fuel allows, stored in the high nibble of that waypoint's byte 6 — which is byte `0x1c` of the array. The queue code tests that nibble for zero to choose between "routed to" (messages 49/50) and "will not be routed for lack of fuel" (51/52). (3) The attrition block's own gate `entry count < 2` therefore means "no second waypoint", and its class test `high nibble == 0xa0` means **the fleet's next leg is ordered at Warp 10**. For each design slot with ships, unless the slot-0 component subtype is one of `7, 8, 9, 0xe, 0xf` (the five engines rated safe at warp 10: Interspace-10, Enigma Pulsar, Trans-Star 10, Trans-Galactic Mizer Scoop and Galaxy Scoop in `component-stats.tsv`), **each ship** rolls a 1-in-10 chance of being destroyed (`:73493`-`73531`). If every ship in the fleet is lost the fleet is removed with message 225 ("destroyed in a massive reactor accident due to unsafe operating procedures"); if exactly one ship is lost, message 223 ("One of your ships in \s was destroyed when its engines over-reacted trying to achieve Warp 10"); if several, message 224 ("\i of your ships... destroyed due to engine strain"). This is the same 10%-per-ship rule `fleet-movement-scanning-cargo.md` §1 states from public sources, and it now has its code location; there is no separate "hull class" mechanic. The neighbouring test just above it (`:73398`-`73409`) is the Cheap Engines rule from the same document: a leg faster than warp 6 by a Cheap Engines race has a 1-in-10 chance of the fleet not moving at all that turn (message 242, "balky equipment"). Also in this movement pass (`:73449`-`73470`), a fleet of an Alternate Reality race carrying more than 10 units of colonists loses roughly 3% of them, `(cargo + 11) x 3 / 100`, with message 193 ("Due to the rigors of warp acceleration, 00 of your colonists on \s have died"); the surrounding condition was not traced further.

  A structurally related, previously-uncatalogued branch sits immediately after this same gate (`stars.exe.export.c:73381`-`73394`) and is worth flagging here even though it is not part of the attrition mechanic itself: it re-reads `designBase` and, before falling through to the hull-class (`0xa0`) attrition check this section documents, tests bit 2 of `DAT_1128_5a16` for the fleet's owning race (`(((undefined1*)&DAT_1128_5a16)[puVar17[1]*0xc0] & 4) != 0`) — the identical per-race status byte and bit this document's §5 "fourth event" gating condition uses (see the new finding there). When that bit is set, and a turn-counter/race-index staggering condition additionally holds, the fleet has a 1-in-4 chance per relevant turn of being logged with message type `0x102` (`extracted-game-data/message-strings.txt` row 258, "`\s` has refused to move, doubting your authority to rule...") and is unconditionally skipped for the rest of this block (`goto LAB_10b0_1fd7`) regardless of whether that roll succeeds. This is a second, independent call site using the same status bit as §5's fourth event, discovered while re-reading this line pair; it is not part of the design-stack/hull-class question this note was asked to settle and is not analyzed further here.
- One is a per-star pass with an RNG-gated conditional branch, taking a mode parameter that selects between two mutually-exclusive object categories at its two call sites. **Fully identified as `FUN_10b0_0f9a` (`stars.exe.export.c:72509`), called with mode 0 at line 72142 (before fleet movement) and mode 1 at line 72171 (after the production hub, before the post-movement stage that contains the battle pass; corrected by the §1 reconciliation, this text previously placed both calls after `FUN_10b0_1f8c`). It is a special-object pass, not a per-star pass: besides the wormhole and Mystery Trader branches described here it moves mass packets (§1 steps 15 and 21).** The category active when the mode parameter is non-zero walks the special-object (wormhole-type) table and calls directly into segment 36's stability-tier extractor (`FUN_1118_0742`) ahead of a 1-in-100 roll, then — on success — repeatedly re-rolls a candidate position (up to 100 attempts) and re-scores it through segment 36's placement rejection-sampler (`FUN_1118_0456`) before committing the best candidate found; this is consistent with, and plausibly the concrete driver of, the annual wormhole drift/relocation behavior already attributed to those two segment-36 routines in `fleet-movement-scanning-cargo.md`'s "Segment coverage" note. **The complementary, mode-zero branch (`stars.exe.export.c:72623`-`72673`) is now traced and matches the "roughly 1-in-25, 1-in-3, and 1-in-2 gates" description exactly**: it walks the *same* special-object table (`DAT_1128_15fe`, stride 9) as the mode-1 branch, but selects entries whose category byte (`*(byte*)(entry+1) & 0xe0`) reads `0x60` rather than the wormhole branch's `0x40`. For an eligible entry, a `FUN_1040_1652(0x19)` roll (1-in-25) gates entry into the block at all; on success, a `FUN_1040_1652(3)` roll (1-in-3) selects between two sub-paths, and on the "both fail" sub-path a further `FUN_1040_1652(2)` roll (1-in-2) picks between two candidate value pairs — one built from `DAT_1128_0074` (the same per-game scaling constant used elsewhere for galaxy-size-scaled ranges) plus fixed offsets `0x564`/`0x169`, the other a fixed `0x3fc` — writing the chosen pair into the entry's two "stat" fields (`entry[3]`/`entry[4]`). Control then falls through into the *same* shared tail the mode-1 branch also reaches (`LAB_10b0_11c6` onward): a squared nibble-field computation, a position delta via `FUN_1038_2924`, a distance check against two stored double-precision thresholds (`DAT_1128_1d52`/`DAT_1128_1d5a`), and a candidate-scoring call into `FUN_10b8_4200`. Because both category branches feed the identical position/distance/placement tail and operate on the identical table, the mode-zero branch is most plausibly the *creation* half of the wormhole mechanic (materializing a new wormhole-table entry with freshly rolled stat/mass values before the placement tail positions it), complementing the mode-one branch's *relocation* of already-existing entries — but which stored category byte value is "freshly created" versus "already active" was not independently confirmed against a labeled constant, so this reading, while well-evidenced, stops short of certainty.

  **Follow-up pass — additional, corroborating (not conclusive) evidence found elsewhere in the client.** A map-rendering routine (`FUN_1058_3bc6`, code-coverage segment 12, `stars.exe.export.c:39599`-onward) that computes a distance/delta line from a reference point to a selected special-object-table entry branches on this exact same category-byte mask (`stars.exe.export.c:39667`-`39680`): entries whose category reads `0x60` have their position read **directly** from the entry's own two stored coordinate fields (`iVar3+6`/`iVar3+8`), while entries whose category reads `0x20` instead resolve their position **indirectly**, through an index into the separate star-position table (`DAT_1128_2f60`) stored in the entry. In other words, `0x60`-category entries carry their own free-floating galaxy position rather than being anchored to a star — consistent with wormholes, which are placed at arbitrary rejection-sampled galaxy coordinates rather than at a star (per `fleet-movement-scanning-cargo.md`'s "Wormhole placement and stability" note), and inconsistent with a star-attached mechanic. This does not independently prove `0x60` is specifically the wormhole-creation state rather than some other free-floating special-object type (e.g. the Mystery Trader spawn documented in §5), but it is one further piece of evidence pointing the same direction as the existing hypothesis, found via a completely different code path (map rendering) than the one the hypothesis itself was built from (the per-star pass). The category byte's exact real-world label is still not confirmed against any string/constant data; this remains a well-evidenced, not certain, reading.

  **Resolved, and the "wormhole creation" hypothesis overturned — a real associated message string was found this pass.** The instruction to search for *other* code reading/writing category byte `0x60` (beyond the placement/relocation branches above) turned up a waypoint-task validity check, `FUN_1070_374a` (`stars.exe.export.c:47132`-`47765`), whose body (`stars.exe.export.c:47410`-`47450`) resolves a fleet's waypoint target through the same special-object table (`FUN_1038_01a0`, an ID-based lookup into `DAT_1128_15fe`) and branches on the identical `*(byte*)(entry+1) & 0xe0` mask to decide which of three distinct "target has vanished, orders changed to last known location" messages to log: category `0x40` logs type code `0xf8`, category `0x60` logs type code `0x110`, and category `0` logs type code `0x111` (`stars.exe.export.c:47431`-`47447`). `extracted-game-data/message-strings.txt` gives these three identifiers' actual text verbatim: `248` ("The Wormhole \s was heading for has vanished..."), `272` ("The Mystery Trader \s was heading for has vanished..."), and `273` ("The Mine Field \s was heading for has vanished..."). This directly confirms category `0x40` = **Wormhole** (already established) and, decisively, category `0x60` = **Mystery Trader** — not a wormhole-creation state as the previous pass's hypothesis proposed, and category `0` = **Mine Field**. This is fully consistent with, and in hindsight explains, all of the previously-gathered circumstantial evidence rather than contradicting it: the free-floating (non-star-anchored) position storage `FUN_1058_3bc6` showed for `0x60` entries matches the Mystery Trader's own wandering, non-star-anchored spawn position (§5); the mode-zero branch of `FUN_10b0_0f9a` (the "roughly 1-in-25" roll materializing a new `0x60`-category entry with freshly rolled "stat" fields, at `stars.exe.export.c:72623`-`72673`) is therefore most plausibly the concrete **Mystery Trader spawn roll** itself, not a wormhole-creation step — its two candidate "stat" value pairs plausibly encode the spawn's tech-gated component-loadout tier already described qualitatively in §5, rather than a mass/value pair for a new wormhole; and a further, separate walk of the same table filtering on category `0x60` (`stars.exe.export.c:73113`-`73123`, inside the master routine itself) is consistent with a "does a Mystery Trader already exist" singleton check ahead of a new spawn. The map's own icon painter (`FUN_1058_0b46`, segment 12, `stars.exe.export.c:38374`-`38453`) independently corroborates the three-way category split found here: it draws category `0x40` entries with a small fixed 9×9 sprite tile plus (when a paired-endpoint visibility flag is set) a connecting line to the other wormhole end — a wormhole-pair rendering; category `0x60` entries instead get a green-on-black text/tile blit sized from the delta of the entry's own two "stat" fields (`FUN_1058_6b4a`, `stars.exe.export.c:41931`-`41959`) rather than an icon — plausibly a caption or count rendered for the Trader rather than a swirl icon; and the remaining (category `0x20`) entries get a generic diamond-outline or ownership-colored bracket marker. `turn-generation-engine.md`'s and `fleet-movement-scanning-cargo.md`'s existing wormhole-placement material is unaffected by this correction — it was never based on the `0x60` reading, only adjacent to it — but the "plausibly wormhole creation" framing for `FUN_10b0_0f9a`'s mode-zero branch should be read as superseded by this finding.
- The remaining sibling routine was, by a wide margin, the largest function in the entire segment (roughly 1,600 lines — twice the size of the waypoint-task routine above): a per-fleet, task-type-dispatching pass invoked in (at least) four internal modes, heavy with report/message-store traffic and same-location/ally candidate-fleet searches. **Fully characterized this pass — see the "Segment 23's largest function" note in `fleet-movement-scanning-cargo.md`, immediately after that document's "Waypoint-task feasibility and partial-fulfillment" section.** In summary: this routine (`FUN_10b0_3f3a`) is a second, parallel per-fleet task dispatcher alongside `FUN_10b0_1f8c` above, implementing full Transport-task cargo-instruction execution (all five cargo slots, all load/unload/set-amount/optimal-load sub-modes — not just the conditional "Set Waypoint to `<amount>`" case already documented in that file's §4), Scrap Fleet mineral recovery, the turn-generation-time execution of a queued Merge-With-Fleet order, Lay Mine Field's minefield-growth/creation step, and a Stargate-jump completion handler that calls directly into segment 17's gate-execution routine (as distinct from segment 17's own cargo-dump/transit-restriction check already documented). **Colonisation and invasion (`FUN_10b8_1ea6`).** Pending landings are (planet, race, colonists) records. A landing is discarded with a message when: the race is Alternate Reality and the planet is already owned (message 87, colonists "reduced to protoplasmic blobs"; Alternate Reality can still found a colony on an *unowned* planet, message 11, "Orbital Construction Module"); the planet is unowned and the record lacks its colonize-order bit (message 2, a defensive case, because the normal record creator always sets that bit); or the planet has an owner and a starbase, which kills every landing (message 88; because the battle pass runs first in the same stage, destroying the starbase in battle opens the planet to invasion). Otherwise each race's landed colonists are scaled by a strength factor: **165% for War Monger, 110% for every other race** (Alternate Reality 0). On an unowned planet the largest force wins; an exact tie for first place destroys everyone (messages 5, 6); the winner's surviving colonists are scaled by (largest - second largest) / largest, which reproduces the tie-break already documented in §4. On an owned planet the defenders' strength is the planet's population, **doubled for Inner Strength**; if the attackers' combined strength is not at least the defenders', every attacker is destroyed (message 0 or 1 to each attacker, 3 or 4 to the defender), and the defenders' population falls by the proportional share; otherwise the strongest attacker takes the planet with survivors scaled by (attack - defence) / attack (message 12 to the winner, 13 to the other attackers, 7 to the former owner). A planet-defense coverage figure computed by `FUN_1038_0262` first destroys a share of the landing force (messages 1 and 4 quote it). After a change of owner the winner gets the default production queue (`production-queue.md` §10f); when there was a previous owner, the tech-salvage roll of §5 runs with that owner's tech levels as thresholds; and if the planet carries an **artifact** flag (bit `0x40` of the word at planet byte `0x1a`, cleared on use) and "No Random Events" is off, the new owner immediately receives `100 + random(0..300)` research resources in a random one of the six fields (scaled down when the founding population is under 1,000 colonists), message 94 ("...found a strange artifact boosting your research..."). The pass runs once before movement and once after (§11 order), which is the mechanism behind `research-tech-tree.md`'s "waypoint-zero invasions, then battles, then waypoint-one invasions".

**Repair (`FUN_10b8_2922`).** Each fleet that is not flagged destroyed has the damage figure of each of its 16 design stacks reduced, floor zero, by a yearly repair rate: **5** if the fleet moved this year; otherwise **10** in deep space; **15** orbiting a planet it does not own; **25** orbiting its own planet without a starbase; and at its own starbase **40**, or **100** when the starbase hull has a dock (a non-zero docking capacity, the same field messages 206/207 quote). Inner Strength doubles the rate; a stack of Fuel Transport hulls (hull 25) adds 25 and Super-Fuel Transport (hull 26) adds 50 to the fleet's rate. Starbases repair their own damage by **50 points** a year (**75** for Inner Strength) unless flagged as under construction. These figures are new to the specs; the units of the "damage figure" (bits 7-15 of each stack's damage word) were not converted to a percentage of armor.

**Claim Adjuster terraforming (`FUN_10b8_2be8`).** Applies only when some player is Claim Adjuster (PRT 3). For each planet of such a race, once a year: on a random axis where the race is not immune and the planet's original value differs from the race's ideal, a 1-in-10 roll (and, for planets under 1,000 population units, a second roll scaled by population) moves the planet's **original** environment value one point toward the ideal (the population roll passes automatically at 1,000 population units, i.e. 100,000 colonists, and otherwise with probability population/1000), message 348 ("...improve the underlying \e by 1%"); then the planet's current environment is recomputed to the best values the race's terraforming technology allows and message 342 ("\p has been auto terraformed to a value of \i%") is posted. This is the code behind the trait's "free and instantaneous" terraforming and its 10%-a-year permanent drift (`race-traits.md` §2), and it is why the default-template copy skips terraform entries for the race.

**Fleet terraforming (`FUN_10b8_2e04`).** For each fleet in orbit of an owned planet whose terraforming capacity (`FUN_1080_1a34`) is positive: if the planet's owner is the fleet's owner, or the fleet's owner has rated the planet's owner as Friend, the fleet improves the planet toward the *fleet owner's* habitability; if not, and the planet has no starbase, it degrades the planet. One environment point moves per unit of capacity, clamped 1..99, and the messages are 300/301 (improved / cannot improve further) and 346/347 (degraded / cannot degrade further), sent to the fleet's owner and, when the value changed, the planet's owner.

**Mine sweeping (`FUN_10b8_465e`).** A fleet with a sweep rate (`FUN_1080_1ca2`) and a starbase with one (`FUN_1080_1d1c`) each remove mines from every minefield they sit inside whose owner they treat as an enemy (fleets: the battle-plan enemy test; starbases: any owner not rated Friend). The amount is their rate (at least 2; one third of the rate against the third field type), but never more than is needed to shrink the field until the sweeper's own position lies outside it, and never more than the field holds; the sweeper's owner gets message 194 (fleet) or 244 (starbase), and the field's owner message 190 ("Someone has swept N mines..."). A field reduced to nothing is deleted.

**Inner Strength colonist growth (`FUN_10b8_4b38`).** For every fleet of an Inner Strength race carrying colonists, the colonists grow each year by `growth rate % x colonists / 200` (at least 1, with a 2-in-3 chance of no growth when the formula gives zero), added to the cargo; anything that does not fit is landed on the planet the fleet orbits (messages 251 and 344).

**Random-event numbers, corrected against the code.** *Comet* (`FUN_10b8_33ee`): a 1-in-20 chance each year, not before year 10; a random planet is chosen and skipped if it is owned with population of at least 5,100 while the year is 19 or earlier; size class 0-3; up to size+1 environment axes (never more than 3) move by 3-5 points each (a huge comet adds another 3-5), sign random, on both the current and original value (**not** the "50-350" of §5, which is the size range of the baseline mineral drop before division by 16); size+1 minerals get a surface deposit of 3,000-19,999 (divided by 16) and a concentration bump of 50-99 (65-128 for a huge comet, capped at 200), and the owner, unless Alternate Reality, loses 25%, 45%, 65% or 85% of the population (messages 131-138). *Environment shift* (`FUN_10b8_37c8`): 1-in-20, one axis by 4-8 points (mostly 4 or 5; a first roll of 3 is re-rolled to 6-8), both current and original values, message 253 to the owner, with the same owned-population protection as the comet but no minimum year. *Mineral deposit* (`FUN_10b8_38dc`): a yearly chance of **1 in (15 minus the galaxy-size index)**, i.e. 1 in 15 for a tiny galaxy down to 1 in 11 for a huge one, **not** scaled by the year as §5 says; a random mineral of a random planet gains 5-19 concentration if it is below 180; message 254 to the owner. Both disasters and the comet then run the queue cleanup of `production-queue.md` §8.
