# Turn Generation Engine

Behavior specification for the "Generate New Turn" pipeline in the 1995-2000 4X game *Stars!* — the batch process that runs once per turn, after every player's orders are collected, to advance the game state and produce the next turn's files. This document is derived directly from the exported client's decompiled turn-processing logic; no public source documents the exact phase ordering or the mechanics below, so this is presented as new material rather than a correction to an existing specification. Facts are described in generic terms; no original identifiers or literal code are used.

## Overview

Turn generation is a single, strictly ordered master routine. It does not resolve all of one player's actions and then move to the next player in a naive way — instead, it runs a fixed sequence of *phases*, each phase sweeping across every relevant object (fleet, planet, race) once, before the next phase begins. This phase ordering matters: a fleet that is destroyed in the battle phase, for example, can no longer be acted on by phases that run afterward. The battle phase runs *after* movement and production, in the second of two stages of the routine; see §1 for the reconciled 40-step order.

## Mechanics

### 1. Phase order (reconciled against the master routine's actual call sequence)

The master per-turn routine is `FUN_10b0_0000` (segment 23, `stars.exe.export.c:71627`-`72460`). **The 23-item list that used to stand here was assembled from a coarse reading and is superseded.** Read statement by statement, the routine has 40 steps in five groups: load and validate (steps 1-11), a *pre-movement* stage and fleet movement (12-17), the economy (18-22), a *post-movement* stage that contains the battle pass (23), and wrap-up, victory check and file output (24-40). The two stages are the same driver, `FUN_10b0_0ebe`, called with 0 and with 1 (`:72067`, `:72174`); the parameter selects which of its sub-steps run. Apart from the shared helpers `FUN_1038_42e2` and `FUN_1070_569a`, the callees named in steps 12-35 have no call site anywhere in the export except this routine and its stage driver (checked by search), so nothing in them is reached from another path, and in particular **combat is not triggered from inside any other pass**: the battle scan `FUN_10f0_24c0` is called only from the post-movement stage (`:72486`), and the scan and resolver it uses (`FUN_10f0_1af8`, `FUN_10f0_5950`) have no other caller.

Nothing is written to disk until step 39 apart from three small exceptions: the optional save of the host client's own pending orders in step 1, step 31's one-bit rewrite of the old host file's header, and step 37's deletes, renames and copies of the previous files. Everything else works on the in-memory game, and any fatal file error before step 39 (including a failed order apply, step 6) jumps to the clean-up at step 40 (the error trap of step 4) with a failure result and no new files. Errors while writing in step 39 are handled per file and do not change the result (see step 39).

**What the starbase "rating" is: the design's cloak percentage.** `FUN_1048_57b6` (`:28428`-`28502`) is the single-design version of the fleet cloak aggregator `FUN_1080_1e02` documented in `combat-resolution.md` §11: it uses the same per-component reader `FUN_1080_20c8` and the same piecewise curve. Raw cloak points are the sum of: **40** if the design is a starbase chassis (hull id 32 or higher) and the race has lesser trait 3, Improved Starbases (`race-traits.md` documents an inherent 20% starbase cloak for this trait; 40 points is 20%, `:28441`-`28447`); **300** if the race's primary trait index is 1, Super Stealth (`:28448`-`28452`); and every occupied slot's own points (slot count at `+0x7a`, entries from `+0x3a`; `:28456`-`28478`). The points map to a percentage exactly as in `combat-resolution.md` §11: 0-100 points give points/2; 101-300 give 50 + (points-100)/8; 301-612 give 75 + (points-300)/24; 613-1124 give 88 + (points-612)/64; 1125-1611 give 96, or 97 from 1380 points up; 1612 or more gives 98, the ceiling. One edge case is new: a total that is zero, negative or above **25,000** returns 0 (`:28479`-`28481`), so an absurdly overloaded total counts as "no cloak". The function's optional second output (set to 70 or 80 when a scanner-category slot of subtype 5 or 14 is present, `:28465`-`28473`) is not requested here and has no consumer anywhere. The function has exactly two callers, this step and the designer's information panel (`:84936`), and both pass no output location (raw `push 0` at segment 23 `0x0aee` and segment 26 `0x35c5`), so that output is dead in this build. So the rating is 0-98, walk 1 stores `100 - cloak%` (2-100) and then its square, **4-10,000, where 10,000 means an uncloaked starbase**.

**What walk 2 caches and who uses it.** `FUN_1038_337e` (`:19841`-`19962`) returns the design's combined scanner range (negative when the design has no scanner; doubled for races with lesser trait 10, No Advanced Scanners) and, through its out-parameters, the second range figure (`+0x8f`, the penetrating/planet-scanning range of `ship-design-and-components.md` §15c), the counter-cloak percentage read from the 18-entry Tachyon table (`+0x91`, `combat-resolution.md` §11) and a small flag byte (`+0x92`: bit 0 from scanner subtype 5, bits 0 and 1 from subtype 14). `FUN_1038_4e50` (`:21277`-`21327`) is a legality check: the hull (ship-hull table below id 32, starbase-chassis table at id - 32) and every occupied slot must pass the shared availability resolver `FUN_1008_5194` for that race; a failing ship design gets flag `0x80`, which this step never clears. The block is read by `FUN_1038_31dc` (`:19727`-`19782`), which, when option bit `0x02` of `DAT_1128_078a` is set (the host run), combines the cache over a fleet's occupied stacks (maximum range, maximum penetrating range, minimum counter-cloak percentage, OR of the flag bits) instead of recomputing per design through `FUN_1038_32be`. Its caller in the fleet scan pass is `FUN_1070_5f00` (`:49325`).

**Readers of offset `+0x87`, complete.** Every literal `0x87` access in the export was checked (the other hits at `:7622`, `:51258`, `:51280`, `:119184`, `:121065` belong to unrelated structures). The offset holds two different fields:

- **Starbase table: the squared cloak factor.** The only readers are four planet sweeps of the per-race visibility pass `FUN_1070_5a2e` (`:49000`-`49022`), which the per-player file writer `FUN_1070_374a` runs at `:47183`, i.e. in step 39, right after step 36 refreshed the field: `FUN_1070_5f00` fleet scanners (`:49470`-`49476`), `FUN_1070_64fc` (`:49764`-`49767` and `:49830`-`49834`) and `FUN_1070_6d7c` mass-packet scanners (`:49998`-`50002`). Each sweep takes a planet already inside the observer's range, and if the planet is owned and has a starbase (planet flag `+5` bit `0x02`) it reads the field from the owner's starbase table at slot `(planet byte +0x2c) & 0xf`. **Field of 10,000 or more (uncloaked): the planet is reported at detail level 3. Otherwise level 3 only if distance-squared is at most field x range-squared / 10,000, else level 2.** Since the field is `(100 - cloak%)²`, that is exactly "the range shrinks to range x (100 - cloak%)/100", the same rule fleets obey. `FUN_1070_537e` (`:48594`-`48643`) records the level on the planet and, for any level other than 2, also marks the starbase design as revealed (`+0x7c` bit `0x01`, seen-level byte `+0x7b` raised to 3). So level 2 means "planet seen, starbase concealed". Unlike the fleet branch (`:49359`-`49364`), no Tachyon counter-cloak multiplier is applied to a starbase's cloak.
- **Ship table: the design weapon value.** See `ship-design-and-components.md` §9 and `ai-opponent-behavior.md` §11: writer `FUN_1038_09ae`, unsquared; readers are the three AI fleet-role predicates `FUN_1090_2db6`, `FUN_1090_2e52` and `FUN_1090_2e94`. These are **not** production or auto-build checks and do not read the squared starbase field. Two further readers, `FUN_1090_0d82` (`:61438`) and `FUN_10f0_241e` (`:99778`), are **dead code** (settled from the raw executable).
- *What they compute.* `FUN_1090_0d82` sums ship count × weapon value over a fleet's 16 stacks, stopping early if the 32-bit total turns negative. `FUN_10f0_241e` compares two battle tokens by the cached `+0x87` value of their designs (weapon value for ships, cloak factor for starbases, reached through `FUN_10f0_23d8`). It returns "first ranks at or above second" when the second value is no larger and lower in its top bits (`0x7ffff000`). On an exact tie above bit 8, it compares the low nibble of each token's byte `0x1a`.
- *Why they are dead.* Both segments are moveable, and inter-segment calls in this executable go through per-segment relocation records naming only the target segment, with the offset stored in the instruction. A sweep of all 38 segments finds no stored 16-bit copy of either entry offset in a call, push or move instruction. The few byte matches are a relocation-chain link, a conditional-jump displacement and data-segment variable addresses. There is also no near call or jump to either offset within its own segment, no entry-table ordinal and no jump-table word. Both start with a normal far-function prologue right after the previous function's far return, so they are not fall-through tails.

**Verdict on the earlier accounts.** (1) "Both tables are squared" was wrong; only walk 1 squares. (2) "Starbase designs only" is right. (3) The "capability rating" is a cloak percentage, and its squared complement is a visibility factor. (4) The AI auto-build reading was wrong twice over: the predicates are fleet-role classifiers in the AI's fleet drivers, and they read the ship table's weapon value, which the AI preamble (not step 36) fills. The writer and readers of the *starbase* field are step 36 and the step-39 visibility sweeps; the writer and readers of the *ship* field are `FUN_1038_09ae` and the fleet-role predicates. `victory-conditions.md`'s victory routine (`FUN_10b8_3bbc`, step 35) does not perform any of this, as previously confirmed: the two are separate pieces of logic that merely run back to back.

**Loops and repeated work.** These are why a flat list was misleading.
- Research buy loop: three times (12e, 20, 23g). Only the step 20 call adds the year's income; the other two spend what is already banked.
- Colonisation and invasion resolution: twice (12d, 23f), which is the mechanism behind `research-tech-tree.md`'s "waypoint-zero invasions, then battles, then waypoint-one invasions".
- Waypoint-task pass `FUN_10b0_3f3a`: four times, modes 1 and 2 before movement and modes 3 and 4 after the battle.
- Movement: up to 11 passes (step 16); follow-order propagation: up to 8 (step 11).
- Waypoint synchronisation (`FUN_1038_42e2`): steps 10 and 29, and again at the tail of the special-object pass when that pass moved something (`:73169`-`73172`).
- Special-object pass: twice, but the two calls are different (mode 0: packets and Trader; mode 1: wormholes and fresh packets), not "two identical passes".

**What is conditional.** The master routine calls every step in the same order on every turn; what varies is whether a step's body has anything to do. Three kinds of condition exist. (1) The stage parameter of `FUN_10b0_0ebe` gates the battle pass, the Trader encounters, the planet-word clear (stage 1 only) and the cargo report (stage 0 only), and sets the task modes (1-2 or 3-4) and the research call. (2) Game-option bits, mapped step by step in §1b: "No Random Events" (`DAT_1128_0080` bit `0x80`) removes the random-event wrapper of step 20 (`:76626`) and the planet-artifact research bounty of steps 12d/23f (`:78022`), and nothing else in the run (not the special-object passes of steps 15 and 21, not the salvage rolls); "Public Player Scores" changes what step 39 writes; "Slower Tech Advances" is inside the research buy loop of steps 12e/20/23g and the salvage reward; the other option bits are read by no step of the master routine. (3) Data and turn-number conditions, all inside the callees or the step's own loop: step 7's notice 259 (turn counter above 10, staggered by race); step 19 (only fleets of Inner Strength races); step 26 (`:78184`-`78215`: the body runs only if some race is Claim Adjuster); step 28 (`:98704`-: skipped unless some race has status bit 2 set **and** the counter is above 9); step 35 (the year gate of the victory settings); step 37 (an existence test per file, which sets the no-orders flag); step 39 (per-file header choice and the score-record test, counter above 19); and, inside step 20, the event-year floors of §5a (comet and mineral deposit from counter 10, Mystery Trader creation from counter 40, environment shift with no floor; the comet and environment shift also spare owned planets of 51+ population units before counter 20). Steps 24, 25, 27, 29, 30, 31, 32, 33, 34, 36, 38 and 40 have no condition at all. The relative order never changes with any option, trait or turn number.

**Consequences worth reproducing.** Ships completed in step 20 exist before the battle pass and can be fought at their build location the same turn; a starbase destroyed in step 23b is gone before invasion resolution (23f, so it no longer blocks landings, §11) but after the year's production; mines, packets and Stargate jumps act during movement, before any battle; Lay Mine Field, remote mining and Transfer Fleet act after the battle (modes 3 and 4), so a fleet destroyed in battle does none of them; and the year counter is incremented after everything except victory, output and file steps.

### 1a. Step 6 in detail: how each player's order file is loaded and applied

Line numbers below are `stars.exe.export.c`. Everything here was read from the decompile; the DOS-level and header facts were cross-checked against the raw executable where the decompile was ambiguous.

**Who is processed, and in what order.** Step 5 builds a random permutation of the slot indices (each position swaps with a random position at or after it, `:71785`-`71798`) and step 6 walks it (`:71799`-`71821`), so the order in which slots act is fresh every turn; only the two cargo ledgers (below) and orders that depend on state an earlier slot changed can notice. Every slot is tried, humans, computer players and eliminated races alike; the loop contains no skip test, and a slot with nothing to load simply contributes nothing. A slot is matched to its file **by name only**: `<game name>.x<slot+1>` (format string at data offset `0x988`, `:71803`-`71804`). `DAT_1128_014c`, the "acting race" that every setter and ownership test consults, is set to the slot before the load (`:71805`) and reset to -1 after the loop (`:71822`); before each load the slot's identity-table entry is set to "none" (`:71808`-`71809`). The loader does **not** compare the header's own player field with the slot (`FUN_1070_2dfc` and the ready-status poll `FUN_1020_4bae` do, `:46475`-`46476`; step 6 uses neither).

**Computer players.** Their orders reach step 6 as ordinary `.x` files written earlier: after a host client opens a game or finishes generating a turn, `FUN_1020_3fe6` (`:8266`-`8344`; call sites `:5460`, `:5572`, `:7855`, `:8841`, `:9581`, `:9725`, `:9732`; once per load, guarded by bit `0x400` of `DAT_1128_078a`, which the game discard `FUN_1070_2a80` clears again, `:46209`) walks the slots and, for each computer slot (bit `0x200` of the record word at +2) whose `.x` file does not validate (`FUN_1070_2dfc`, purpose 1), runs the personality dispatcher `FUN_1088_0000` (`ai-opponent-behavior.md` §1), which records its decisions through the same edit path a human uses and ends by saving them as that slot's `.x<N>` file (`:58023`). The master routine has no AI-specific code, and an AI slot without a file is treated exactly like an absent human.

**Loading one file: `FUN_1048_7c66` (`:30660`-`30888`).**
1. Resets the shared change buffer (`DAT_1128_0964` = 0, `DAT_1128_0966` = -1) and installs a local error trap (`:30689`-`30694`).
2. *Source.* In a tutorial game (options bit `0x08`), while hosting (`078a` bit 1) and only for slot 0, the loader first looks for a bundled script resource (type 10000, id `0x2711`): if the turn counter is below the resource's leading count byte, it skips that many groups of records (each group starts at a type-8 header) and reads the next group instead of a file (`:30709`-`30739`); otherwise it drops the resource and reads the file. Which slot the tutorial's human occupies, and the script's contents, were not traced. Any other case opens the file with `FUN_1070_329a(name, 0x4020)`: the `0x4000` flag suppresses the "cannot open" message, and while generating (`078a` bit 9) a failed open is retried every 0.5 s for up to 4 s unless the DOS error is "file not found" (`:46706`-`46756`).
3. A file that does not exist is **not an error**: the open longjmps into the trap, no handle is open, and the loader returns 1 with an empty buffer (`:30695`-`30707`). Every rejection in step 5 ends the same way (return 1, nothing buffered).
4. Records are read by `FUN_1070_31f4` (`:46673`-`46690`): a 2-byte record header (type in bits 10-15, payload length in bits 0-9), then the payload, deciphered unless the type is 0 or 8 (`save-turn-file-format.md` §10). A short read calls `FUN_1070_2d9c(3)` and longjmps (`:46817`-`46820`); the trap closes the file and returns **0**.
5. *Header checks* (`:30749`-`30876`), applied to the fields wherever the first record's bytes land (the loader does not verify that the first record is type 8): (a) the file's game id must equal the host's `DAT_1128_0070`, else silent skip; (b) the file's turn number must not be below the host's `DAT_1128_0082`: a lower number is a stale file from an earlier turn and is silently skipped, a higher one posts error 30 (the log file is newer than the game and will be ignored); (c) when the turn numbers are equal, the header's nonce (flag bits 13-15) must equal bits 9-11 of `DAT_1128_0080`, else error 29 (the file belongs to a different game). Errors are recorded in `DAT_1128_070e` and displayed only when `DAT_1128_070c` is 0 and the host is not generating (`FUN_1070_2d9c`, `:46387`-`46399`); the master routine sets `078a` bit 1 at step 4 (`:71771`; `070c` is forced to 1 only around the step-2 load and restored at `:71722`), so **no dialog ever appears** from these checks. Two checks that other loaders make are absent here: the header's *submitted* flag (flag bit 0) is not tested, so orders saved without pressing "submit" are applied too (a forced generation still uses whatever a player has saved), and the header's version and kind are not tested.
6. *On acceptance*: the header's version word is stored in `DAT_1128_0710` (`:30754`); header flag bit 12 is copied into `DAT_1128_078c` bit 2 and, when hosting, into bit 1 of the acting race's status byte `DAT_1128_5a16[slot]` (`:30755`-`30760`). This build always writes that bit as 0; what sets it in other files, and what it means, is not pursued (it sits in the status family this project deliberately does not detail, §5), and its only effect seen here is that step 7 exempts a race that carries it.
7. The next record must be the 17-byte identity record (type 9). Its first word is the byte length of the order records that follow; if the record is exactly 17 bytes, 15 of them (an identity block whose content is deliberately not described) are copied into the slot's 16-byte identity-table entry (`:30762`-`30787`). Step 7 alone consumes that entry.
8. Order records are read until their total size (header plus payload) reaches the length from item 7, and are appended, deciphered, to the shared 32,000-byte change buffer `DAT_1128_0960`, each as its 2-byte header plus payload, exactly the layout the replay loop walks (`:30789`-`30804`). Type-40 records (player-to-player messages) are then appended to a linked list rooted at `DAT_1128_0aca` (count `DAT_1128_0ad0`; the first four payload bytes are a link that the loader overwrites, `:30807`-`30847`); nothing in the master routine reads that list again (its readers and writers are the client's message dialog, `:16210`-`16340`, and the orders writer, `:31082`-`31095`).
9. The record after those must be type 0, the end marker. If it is, `DAT_1128_0964` receives the byte length and the result stays 1; if not, `DAT_1128_0964` stays 0, the result is **0**, and nothing from this file is applied (`:30851`-`30856`). The file (or resource) is closed and `FUN_1048_67a6(0)` clears the "orders dirty" state (`:30857`-`30868`).

**Applying: `FUN_1048_6824` (`:29589`-`29621`).** Called right after a load that returned nonzero (`:71811`); with an empty buffer it returns 1 at once.
- *Live state, no working copy.* It sets `DAT_1128_0968` = 1 for the whole replay; the shared append primitive (`:29506`) does nothing while that flag is set, so replaying orders records no new orders, and the decoder's cases call the same setters interactive editing uses.
- It walks the buffer record by record (advancing by each record's own length, `:29607`-`29616`), calls the decoder `FUN_1048_68a8(type, length, payload)` for every one, ANDs the results, never stops early, clears a "modified" bit (`078f` bit 2) and returns the AND. A record type outside the decoder's 22 case labels (`save-turn-file-format.md` §9) ends the switch and counts as success (`:30653`).
- *Ownership.* Opcodes `0x22` (research settings), `0x24`, `0x26` and `0x2e` write straight into the acting race's own record (`:30501`, `:30537`-`30543`, `:30632`); `0x1b` (design edit) fails unless the race field in the payload equals the acting race and the slot index is 0-25 (`:30298`-`30312`); `0x1d`, `0x1e` and `0x23` fail or do nothing for another race's records (`:30392`, `:30461`, `:30517`). The fleet-field opcodes look objects up by id (a fleet id embeds its owner's number, `:17876`), and no comparison of that owner with the acting race was seen among the cases read; the one owner test seen there applies to the *second* object of a two-object edit whose lookup failed: a missing foreign object is tolerated, a missing object owned by the acting race fails the record (`:29991`-`29995`). Other refusals return 0 too, for example an out-of-range list index in opcodes 3-5 (`:29996`-`30012`).
- *Cross-owner cargo.* In host mode a cargo edit whose second object belongs to another race is deferred rather than applied: colonists unloaded onto a planet owned by a different race are added to the pending-landing ledger (`:29776`-`29834`), other cargo moved between objects of different owners goes into the 25-byte ledger (`:29874`-`29970`; a two-object cargo edit is processed in two sweeps over its fields, negative amounts first), and the acting race's own side of the edit is applied at once (`:29755`-`29772`, `:29839`-`29872`). Both ledgers were allocated in step 4 and are settled in steps 12d, 12g and 23f. (Read structurally; not traced record by record.)

**What can abort the run.** Only one thing: a load that returned nonzero followed by an apply that returned 0, i.e. at least one record failed. The master routine then shows a stop-icon message box with dynamic string 1 (a player log file is corrupt and cannot be loaded) and jumps to the clean-up (`:71811`-`71815`): no later step runs, no file is written, the result is 0, and the orders already applied exist only in memory, which the next load discards. Every other problem merely makes the slot contribute nothing:

| Situation | Loader result | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| no `.x<N>` file (never submitted, AI without a file, eliminated race) | 1, empty buffer | slot contributes nothing; step 37 later marks it "no orders" |
| file from another game | 1, empty | silent skip |
| file's turn number below the host's (stale) | 1, empty | silent skip |
| file's turn number above the host's | 1, empty | error 30 recorded, not shown; skipped |
| same turn, nonce differs | 1, empty | error 29 recorded, not shown; skipped |
| truncated or unreadable file | 0 | skipped (error 3 recorded) |
| no end marker after the orders | 0 | skipped |
| readable file, one record fails | 1, then apply returns 0 | **whole run aborted** |
| same file copied to two slots | each loaded under its own slot | no duplicate-order detection; foreign-owner records are refused only by the opcodes listed above |

**Post-processing after the loop.** In order: `DAT_1128_014c` = -1 (`:71822`), step 7 (identity and penalty flags), step 8 (design normalisation), step 9 (fleet flag reset), step 10 (`FUN_1038_42e2`, waypoint synchronisation), step 11 (follow-order propagation). There is no relation post-pass: a relationship change is opcode `0x26`, which writes the acting race's row directly (`:30543`), and nothing in the executable decays or adjusts relations afterwards (`diplomacy-relations.md` §3).

**State set by step 6.** `DAT_1128_014c` (acting race; -1 afterwards); `DAT_1128_0964`/`0966`/`0968` (change-buffer length, last-record index, replay flag); the slot's 16-byte identity entry (-1 = no file, else the 15-byte block); `DAT_1128_0710` (version word of the last accepted file); `DAT_1128_078c` bit 2 and `DAT_1128_5a16[slot]` bit 1 (both from header flag bit 12); the pending-landing ledger and its count `DAT_1128_59b8`; the 25-byte ledger and its count `DAT_1128_569c`; the player-message list `DAT_1128_0aca`/`0ad0`; `DAT_1128_078e` bit 10 cleared after each replay; plus every game-state change the orders themselves make (waypoints, queues, designs, relations, research settings, names) and the messages those setters post into the message buffer.

### 2. Battle resolution engine

Combat is resolved by a dedicated routine invoked once per location with a detected hostile-race conflict. Its structure:

- Two growable, persistent "battle log" record pools are ensured to exist (allocated on first use).
- A scratch table tallying per-race, per-design-slot ship-stack quantities is built from the fleet ring at that location (a same-location "linked stack" of every fleet present), including the defending planet's starbase if it is a valid combat participant.
- The full combatant roster is built by flattening every ship stack present — one aggregate record per (race, design) pair with quantity > 0 — computing each aggregate's derived combat statistics (armor/shield/cloak/jamming totals, etc.) via shared per-design stat functions.
- **The battle runs for up to 16 rounds** — an explicit, hardcoded loop bound. This directly confirms the round cap already documented (from public sources) in `combat-resolution.md`.
- Within each round, a per-ship "rate"/initiative value is computed with rounding that depends on the round number's parity (odd/even), then combatant firing order is reordered by a shared "priority" computation. Targeting scans distances/ranges across up to 3 weapon-range brackets per shot.
- Damage is logged into the growable event log as it's applied (not computed all at once), and after the round's shots resolve, a resource-salvage step calls into the production-planning subsystem (`ai-opponent-behavior.md` §6-adjacent logic) to decide whether destroyed-ship minerals should be pulled toward nearby friendly production needs.
- After the battle concludes, a message/outcome-selection routine picks from roughly 20 distinct message codes describing the aftermath (destroyed participants, salvage available, etc.) per race present.

**This confirms the 16-round cap directly from the executable, independent of the public FAQ material `combat-resolution.md` was originally built from.** A later, dedicated pass over segment 31 (`FUN_10F0_*`) went further than this section's original scope and did fully decode several of the specific formulas flagged here as unconfirmed — see `combat-resolution.md` §§6 and 8 for the details, summarized here:
- **The accuracy formula is now confirmed and arithmetically cross-checked** against `combat-resolution.md`'s own worked numbers (`FUN_10f0_41ca`, segment 31) — it combines base accuracy, computer bonus, and jam via a subtractive-cancellation-then-branch formula, reproducing all three documented percentages exactly.
- **The armor-quantization scheme's apparent mismatch (7-bit field found vs. 1/512 claimed) is resolved**, not just re-confirmed: the field is a packed 16-bit word whose low 7 bits and high 9 bits serve two different purposes, and the high 9 bits (explicitly clamped to 0–499 in code) give a real granularity of 1/500 — close to, and likely the real source of, the documented 1/512 figure.
- **The energy-capacitor beam-damage bonus's mechanism (not its exact percentage) is now confirmed**, and the "one missile, one kill" salvo-accounting aside was checked against the actual missile damage-application path and found to have no distinct code-level counterpart — the whole-ship-kill division formula appears to govern missile kills the same way it governs beam kills.
- **The beam APN's RangeModifier term and the Battleship-specific firing-order quirk's generality were specifically searched for as part of this pass and not found at the time** (the relevant code was large enough that this was reported as "not found" rather than "confirmed absent"). **Both have since been resolved by a later pass — see `combat-resolution.md` §4/§5/§8:** the RangeModifier term is confirmed absent from the APN formula (a full re-read found the distance value used only as a binary in-range gate, discarded before the APN math), and every hull is confirmed to fire its weapon slots in plain stored array order, not a Battleship-specific special case.

Separately, the battle-replay ("VCR") viewer that players use to watch a completed battle is confirmed to be **pure play-back**: it reads the round-by-round event log built above (token position, a "beam fired" or "missile fired" flag, and a raw damage/delta value per event) and only ever *displays* pre-decided outcomes — it performs no targeting, accuracy, or damage computation of its own. The viewer's own grid-drawing and hit-testing code independently confirms the battle board is exactly a 10×10 grid, with each token's position packed as one 4-bit column and one 4-bit row value.

### 3. Minefields and mass packets

Processed once per turn: mass packets move in the special-object passes (§1 steps 15 and 21, impact included), minefield collisions happen during fleet movement (step 16), and packet and minefield decay is step 18:

- **Mass-packet decay in flight**: a percentage of a packet's mineral cargo is lost each turn, scaled by the packet's warp-speed tier (roughly 10%/25%/50% for increasing speed tiers), and halved for a specific racial trait. On arrival (or when flagged for detonation), a packet's remaining cargo is delivered to (or impacts) planets within its blast radius.
- **Minefield decay and collision** (the first clause of this bullet originally said "regrowth"; see the correction right after it): each turn, minefields lose some mine-unit strength, then two collision passes check every free-flying fleet and every fleet stationed at/orbiting a planet against every enemy minefield in range: a squared-distance test against the field's radius, with damage/mine-loss scaled by field "type" (a heavier field type inflicts roughly a third the normal mine-loss-to-damage ratio of an ordinary field), decrementing the field's strength and possibly destroying it if depleted. Both affected races are notified.

  **Per-turn minefield and mass-packet decay, read in full (segment-24 sweep, this pass).** The routine that runs this pass is `FUN_10b8_433a` (`stars.exe.export.c:79509`-`79658`), called from the master routine at `:72166` just before the production pass. It clears every fleet's "already hit by a field this turn" mark, then walks the special-object table (18-byte records): for **mass packets** it applies the in-flight decay described above (`FUN_10b8_4200`, details in `production-queue.md` §10b) and, for a packet whose stored speed nibble is zero (a resting packet), removes 10% of each mineral (at least 10 kT) per year after a one-turn grace flag is used up, and deletes it at zero; for **minefields** it applies the following. A minefield's yearly loss rate is `2 + 4 x S` percent, where S is the number of stars lying inside the field's circle (`FUN_1118_01a0`, `:114949`-`114992`, counts stars in the sorted star table within the squared radius, which equals the mine count) and the multiplier 4 is replaced by 1 when the field's owner is Space Demolition (PRT 5); the rate is capped at 50%, and raised by 25 percentage points if the field carries the owner's "detonate" flag. The absolute loss is that percentage of the field's mine count, but never fewer mines than the percentage figure itself, and never fewer than 10 unless the field is the third (speed-bump) type; a field whose strength is not above the loss is deleted. Fields carrying the detonate flag first damage every not-yet-hit fleet inside the circle through the shared mine/overgate damage routine `FUN_10b0_312a` and mark those fleets. **No per-turn regrowth of a minefield exists in this pass; strength rises only through mine-laying orders** (§3 mine-laying paragraphs below). The collision passes themselves live in the movement code (segment 23), not in segment 24.
- **Minefields are edited via the same order-queuing mechanism used everywhere else** — a minefield-window command issues a turn order that is applied during this pass, rather than mutating the minefield immediately.
- **Mine-laying rate, confirmed by inspection of the exported client.** A fixed lookup table gives a base per-component mine-laying rate keyed by component category and subtype, with observed values **20, 40, 50, 60, and 70** (units per component per turn, before fleet-wide weighting by ship count); one specific component category instead reads a rate directly from that component's own data rather than the fixed table. A fleet's total mine-laying output is a ship-count-weighted sum of its installed mine-layers' rates. An earlier pass also described a further piecewise scaling curve flattening toward a cap around 98% for large fleets; the follow-up below read the rate function in full and did not find that curve (only a flat halving gated by a per-fleet flag), so the 98% figure is unconfirmed (see Open Questions).

  **Follow-up pass — the rate function itself was read in full and contains no such curve; the "98%" claim could not be relocated this pass.** `FUN_1080_1aea` (`stars.exe.export.c:57210`-`57311`), confirmed as this project's own citation for the per-component rate formula (cross-referenced from `fleet-movement-scanning-cargo.md`'s "Lay Mine Field" entry), was read line-by-line in its entirety: it walks each occupied design slot, accumulates ship count times per-unit rate (with a flat ×2 for one mine-type category, `stars.exe.export.c:73296`-`73298` equivalent logic inline at `57296`-`57298`) into a running total, and returns that total ×10 at the end (`57305`-`57308`) — a plain weighted sum with a flat doubling case, no percentage curve, no asymptotic cap, and no reference to ship count except as the linear weight already described. Reading forward from both of its call sites inside `FUN_10b0_3f3a`'s Lay Mine Field handler (`stars.exe.export.c:75916`, `75937`) turned up one nearby scaling step that *is* real but does not match the "flattens toward 98% for large fleets" description: at `75941`-`75943`, the combined per-type rate is unconditionally **halved** (the 32-bit rate divided by 2) unless a specific fleet-status flag (bit `0x20` of the fleet's status byte, set once per fleet the first time `FUN_10b0_1f8c` visits it that turn, `stars.exe.export.c:73370`) is already set — a flat, binary 50% cut tied to a per-fleet "already had its first pass this turn" flag, not a fleet-size-dependent curve. No other scaling step, percentage table, or asymptotic-cap arithmetic was found between the rate computation and the point the value is written into a minefield's strength field. **This pass could not locate the "flattens toward ~98%" mechanism this document previously described**, and offers this as a genuine, checked negative result rather than a further guess: either that finding belongs to a different code path this pass's search of `FUN_1080_1aea` and its two call sites did not cover, or it was a misreading of the flat ÷2 halving documented here (which is a binary flag gate, not a curve, and does not asymptote toward 98% or any other fleet-size-dependent bound). A future pass should treat the "98%" figure as unconfirmed pending a fresh source.

**Where laid mines are actually applied, confirmed by inspection of the exported client.** This rate formula only produces a per-turn mine-unit *quantity* for a fleet executing a Lay Mine Field order; the previously-missing other half — searching for (or creating) the target minefield and adding that quantity to it — is implemented inside segment 23's `FUN_10b0_3f3a`, documented in `fleet-movement-scanning-cargo.md`'s "Segment 23's largest function" note.

### 4. Colonization simultaneous-arrival and orbital bombardment

- **Simultaneous colonization tie-break** (resolved by `FUN_10b8_1ea6`, which runs twice a turn: §1 steps 12d and 23f). When multiple fleets from different races arrive at the same uninhabited location in the same turn, the arrival with the largest accumulated population/quantity wins the colonization race; an exact tie between the largest two contenders results in **no one** colonizing that turn. All races present are notified of the win/lose/tie outcome. The winner's colonists are scaled down by the runner-up's strength, where the runner-up is only the strongest contender with a lower race index than the winner (§11, "Colonisation and invasion").
- **Orbital bombardment** (`FUN_10f0_6ea2`, run at the very end of the battle pass, §1 step 23b). Corrected reading: this pass is not an exchange of colonists, minerals or fuel. Once per turn it walks every fleet that is not destroyed, is orbiting a planet (not in deep space), and sits over a planet owned by a different race that the fleet's owner's relationship rules allow it to act against, provided the planet has no starbase-protected flag set. For each such fleet it totals the fleet's bombing output (kill rates, installation-destruction rates and terraform-reversal amount) from its installed components, scales those totals down when the target planet's defence figure is below 1.0, then applies them to the planet: the planet's population is reduced (with a minimum of one colonist killed whenever the fleet has a nonzero kill rate and the planet has population), the planet's mines, factories and defences are reduced in proportion to the fleet's installation-destruction rate, and up to 500 points of terraforming are undone, moving each environment axis back toward its original value. The owner of the bombed planet and the bomber each receive a notice; the wording chosen depends on whether the planet was depopulated, and there are separate message sets for the ordinary and the terraform-reversal outcomes (messages 302/303 and 378/379 among them). Earlier drafts described this as a probabilistic colonist/mineral/fuel exchange between fleet and colony; that reading was wrong. The exact kill-rate and installation-loss arithmetic is recorded in `fleet-movement-scanning-cargo.md` (segment-8 section).

**A fourth confirmed event, reconciled from `combat-resolution.md` §8's "genuinely new, uncategorized mechanic" flag: a racial-trait-gated fleet/planet attrition event, structurally distinct from the two tables above.** A prior pass sampling segment 31 found a per-turn routine that reduces fleet cargo-like fields and planet mineral-concentration-adjacent fields by a random percentage, and flagged it as unreconciled with this section rather than merging it speculatively. - **Not part of either the 13-entry or 6-entry table above.** It is invoked directly from the master turn routine as its own step, (`FUN_10f0_111a`, §1 step 28) shortly before the turn/year counter increment (step 33) — i.e., it runs once per turn near the very end of the simulation, after repair and terraforming, and is unrelated to the special-object passes (steps 15 and 21) that the old phase list mislabelled as the home of the random events. - **Gating.** The whole routine is skipped unless at least one race in the game has a specific stored per-race flag (bit 2 of a per-race status byte, structurally similar to — but a different field from — the per-race relationship/trait bytes documented in `combat-resolution.md`'s "legitimate enemies" note); the follow-up pass below shows it is not a racial trait at all but the copy-protection-adjacent penalty status bit set by §1 step 7. - **Fleet effect.** For every fleet belonging to a gated race that is not already flagged removed: a 1-in-12 roll flags the fleet as destroyed/removed outright and logs one message code; otherwise, three consecutive stored numeric fields on the fleet (a cargo-like triple, offset and stride consistent with a 3-mineral-type cargo hold) are each independently reduced by a random 10-20% (a single per-fleet roll of 10 + random(11), reused for all three fields), with a floor of at least 1 unit lost per field if the field is positive; if any field was reduced, a second, distinct message code is logged.
- **Planet effect.** For every planet whose owner race is gated: most of the time, a further 1-in-15 roll reduces one randomly-chosen field of a different stored triple (offset and stride consistent with mineral-concentration-adjacent storage, distinct from the fleet-side triple) by a random 5-45% (5 + random(41)), logged via its own message code; in the remaining, rarer case (roughly 1-in-8, reachable only when that planet's triple is not already zeroed), a packed sub-field embedded in two adjacent stored bytes is instead reduced by a random 5-35% (5 + random(31)), with a floor of 1, logged via yet another distinct message code.
- **Four distinct message-log notification codes in total** are used across the fleet-destroyed, fleet-field-reduced, and two planet-side branches, each appended through the same message-record-store append routine documented in `client-ui-dialog-catalog.md`'s "Messages" section — confirming this event surfaces to the player exactly like an ordinary logged event, not silently.

This is a real, fourth confirmed per-turn random-effect mechanism, additional to the two event tables already documented above — not a variant or extension of either. `combat-resolution.md` §8 has been updated to note the reconciliation and point here rather than carry an open item.

**Follow-up pass — the gating "racial trait" identified, and the premise revised: it is not a Primary or Lesser Racial Trait at all.** The routine itself is `FUN_10f0_111a` (`stars.exe.export.c:98704`-`98846`), confirmed by direct inspection to match every previously-documented behavior above line-for-line (the per-race gate, the turn-10 floor, the 1-in-12 fleet-destroy roll and its message, the shared-percentage three-field cargo reduction, and the two planet-side branches). Its gate reads, per race, **bit 2 (mask 4) of a per-race status byte** (`stars.exe.export.c:98736`-`98738`) — confirming the bit position this document already cited. Cross-referencing this exact field against every other reader/writer of that status byte in the decompile (60+ sites) shows it is *not* the PRT-index field (that is a separate field, byte 14 of the settings array at per-player record offset `0x3e`, read through `FUN_10e0_222c`; its sibling `FUN_10e0_226e` tests the lesser-trait word at `0x4e` instead, both accessors documented throughout this project — e.g. `race-traits.md` §1a, `ship-design-and-components.md` §8) and is not an LRT bitmask either (LRTs are read through the same `0x4e`-offset 32-bit field `research-tech-tree.md` and `race-traits.md` both cite for Generalized Research/Ultimate Recycling/Bleeding Edge Technology). The field is instead a general-purpose per-race status byte with several unrelated bits (bit 0 gated dozens of unrelated per-race checks throughout the client; bit 1 is §9's own gating bit, see the revision there; bit 3 is the "already fired a random event this turn" flag `FUN_10f0_61a2` itself sets, `stars.exe.export.c:103011`/`103045`; bit 4 appears once more, unexamined this pass) — a scratch/flags byte, not a trait table.

**What bit 2 concretely does elsewhere strongly suggests it is a copy-protection/integrity status flag, not a gameplay trait.** The identical bit, on the identical field, is independently tested at `stars.exe.export.c:73381` (inside `FUN_10b0_1f8c`, see §10's new note below) where — on a turn-staggered, per-race roll — it logs message type hex `102`, which `extracted-game-data/message-strings.txt` row 258 gives as a notice (paraphrased) that the named fleet refuses to move because it doubts the player's authority, with an invitation to enter a valid serial number to restore it. This is the same "authority doubted" family as message rows 257 (productivity/growth penalty, also a serial-number prompt) and 259 (fleet-movement strike, also a serial-number prompt) — a cluster of penalty notifications this project has not previously traced to code, sitting alongside the two copy-protection notices (`dynamic-string-table.md`'s identifiers 279/386) this project has already, deliberately, chosen only to note the existence of rather than explain. Bit 2's write site was not traced here (consistent with this project's standing house rule never to document how the Serial Number/tamper check itself is computed; a later pass located it as §1 step 7, the duplicate/invalid-registration pass, whose comparison is deliberately not described), but its *read* sites are unambiguous: this is a status/penalty flag family, not a Primary or Lesser Racial Trait, and the attrition mechanic's "racial trait" framing above should be read as superseded by this finding — it is gated by a copy-protection-adjacent per-race status bit, consistent in shape with the other documented penalties in the same message family (reduced productivity, halved growth, refused movement, fleet strikes — fleet/planet attrition is a plausible fifth penalty in the same cluster). Per this project's standing rule, no further detail on how or when the flag is set is pursued here.

**The 6-entry counter-vs-threshold table's contents, resolved by tracing its second roll in full.** `FUN_10f0_61a2`'s second roll (`stars.exe.export.c:103016`-`103049`, immediately following the 13-entry-table roll documented above) draws a field index 0-5 with the bounded-random helper, then compares a per-race, per-index byte (the six-byte array 26 bytes into each race's 192-byte record, data address hex `59dc` for race 0) against a global per-index threshold byte (a six-entry global table). This is **not** six independent gameplay events: the counter storage at `0x59dc` is the same per-race array the Technology Browser dialog (`BROWSERDLG`, confirmed by cross-reference against `client-ui-dialog-catalog.md`'s "Technology Browser" entry) reads and paints with the labels **"Ener:", "Weap:", "Prop:", "Const:", "Elect:", "Bio:"** (dynamic-string identifiers 91-96, i.e. the six research fields from `research-tech-tree.md` in their documented order) — so the table's six "slots" are simply the six tech fields (the per-race bytes are the race's current **tech levels**, see §5a), each independently eligible once the race's level in that field is below the threshold. On success, the research level-cost function `FUN_10d8_1580` (`stars.exe.export.c:89438`) prices the *next* level in that field: 10 times the sum of the race's six tech levels, plus the base cost for that level from the level-cost table (the same formula as `research-tech-tree.md` §3), scaled by the field's cheap/normal/expensive cost class and doubled if the Slow Tech Advance game option is set. The result is accumulated — not overwritten — into a persistent per-race, per-field 32-bit research pool and a message is logged (type code hex `fffe`) via the shared message-append routine documented in `client-ui-dialog-catalog.md`'s "Messages" section. The event itself never raises the tech level directly; it banks resources equal to one level's price, and the next paragraph shows that pool is spent by the ordinary research buy loop (so the level is normally bought at the next research pass). The same tech-level bytes and research pool are also read (not written) by an unrelated debug/inspection routine (`FUN_10d8_4a86`/`FUN_10d8_49c6`, `stars.exe.export.c:89753`-`91750`) that is not reachable through normal turn generation.

**Resolved — the banked reward is genuinely spent, not merely logged.** The accumulator this event banks its reward into is the same per-race, per-field memory `research-tech-tree.md`'s already-documented ordinary research buy-loop, `FUN_10b8_4ce4`, reads as its **starting spendable pool for that field, that turn**: at `stars.exe.export.c:80069`-`80072`, the buy loop reads that field's pool value and its "price the next level, subtract, loop" logic (already described in that document's "Exact overflow/carryover rule" note) spends it down. This is confirmed to be the identical field, not merely a coincidence of addresses, by cross-checking the *other* known writer into it: the Super Stealth passive-bonus code inside the same function (`stars.exe.export.c:80017`-`80035`) adds its bonus at 32 bytes past the start of the per-race record, which is exactly where the research pool begins — i.e. the Super Stealth bonus (already documented as feeding directly into the ordinary research pool) and this event's banked reward (written at `stars.exe.export.c:103029`-`103033`) both deposit into the same array the buy-loop reads from. **This settles the open question: the banked reward is not a pure flavor/bounty number — it is added to the race's genuine per-field research resource pool and is spent by the same buy-loop, on the same carryover terms, as ordinary turn-to-turn research income**, including the possibility of buying more than one tech level in a turn if the combined pool is large enough (per that document's own confirmed carryover behavior).

**Correction, cross-reference only — a mechanic this document previously suspected might be a hidden third random-event slot turns out not to be a random event at all.** `production-queue.md`'s "Planet Rebirth" item (item-type 13/`0xd` inside the segment-24 production-apply dispatcher, `FUN_10b8_0e68`) was traced backward this pass and resolved: it is the build-completion effect of **Genesis Device**, a real, priced, player/AI-queueable production-queue item (confirmed via the cost-calculator `FUN_10d0_221a`'s own `case 0xd`, which prices it as component category `0x8000` subtype 14 — Genesis Device, 5,000 resources, per `extracted-game-data/component-stats.tsv` row 299). It is not part of either the 13-entry or 6-entry table above (both are rolled and dispatched entirely inside segment 31, `FUN_10f0_61a2`/`FUN_1118_1196`, and never touch the production queue), but neither is it any other kind of random per-turn effect — a race can only queue it once it has unlocked the component at all, via the already-documented **one-time per-race component grant** this section's own item just above cites (Genesis Device is bit index 10 of that 12-component list, which salvage never feeds, so in practice only a Mystery Trader trade grants it, §5a), after which building and completing it on any planet is an entirely deliberate choice, not a roll. See `production-queue.md`'s "Conclusion on this event's trigger" note for the full trace.

### 5a. The four yearly random events: exact rules (random-event pass, resolved from the code and raw bytes)

The random-event wrapper `FUN_10b8_1c3e` (`stars.exe.export.c:77524`-`77532`) runs at the end of the production hub (§1 step 20), unless the "No Random Events" option is set. It calls four routines, in this order: the comet `FUN_10b8_33ee` (`:78573`-`78752`), the environment shift `FUN_10b8_37c8` (`:78809`-`78860`), the mineral deposit `FUN_10b8_38dc` (`:78864`-`78890`) and Mystery Trader creation `FUN_10b8_3976` (`:78894`-`79002`). Each routine rolls on its own. Nothing stops two, or even all four, from firing in the same year, and none of them has a per-race limit. The numbers below were read from the raw bytes of segment 24 (file offset `0x74640`), because the decompile hides some operands. The Trader's movement and its encounters with fleets are handled elsewhere and are described at the end of this section.

**"Year" in every gate.** Every year gate compares the counter `DAT_1128_0082`. The counter holds the **displayed year minus 2400**, which is also the number of turns generated so far:
- the year display adds `0x960` (2400) to it (`:7448`);
- a new game sets it to 0 (`:46264`);
- the master routine adds 1 to it as its very last step (`:72186`, §1 step 33).

The events run before that increment. So a gate such as "counter at least 10" first passes while the turn *from* year 2410 is being generated, and the player first sees the result in the 2411 turn. This base was read from the code, not checked in a live game.

**Target planet.** The comet, the environment shift and the deposit each pick one planet uniformly from **every planet in the galaxy**: owned or unowned, homeworld or not, seen or unseen. The pick is a draw of `random(planet count)`, where `DAT_1128_4b9c` is the planet-table length that the mining wrapper also walks. The planet is drawn before any gate is tested, and there is no re-draw. If the planet fails the event's protection test, the event simply does not happen that year.

The comet and the environment shift share one protection test. They skip a planet that meets all three of these conditions:
- it is owned;
- it holds 51 or more population units (5,100+ colonists; a unit is 100 colonists);
- the counter is below 20 (years 2400-2419).

The deposit event has no protection test.

**Comet strike (`FUN_10b8_33ee`).**
- **Chance:** 1 in 20 each year, from counter 10 onward.
- **Size:** one draw of `random(4)` sets the size class, so each class has a 25% chance.
- **Colonist loss:** the owner loses `25 + 20 x size` percent of the population (`:78668`-`78675`, raw `0x3591`-`0x35b5`). An Alternate Reality owner loses nothing.
- **Messages:** **every player** gets a message. The owner (unless Alternate Reality) gets 135-138 (the four size tiers of a notice that the object struck the owner's named planet, killing 25/45/65/85% of the colonists there). Everyone else, and an Alternate Reality owner, gets the matching message from 131-134.
- **Minerals:** `size + 1` minerals are hit, at most three. They are chosen in a random order that is a uniform permutation (raw `0x34cc`-`0x3504`). Each mineral that is hit:
  - gains 50-99 concentration points, plus a further 15-29 for a huge comet (65-128 in all);
  - has the result capped at **200** (raw `0x3629`);
  - gets 3,000-19,999 raw units toward its surface stock.

  All three minerals, hit or not, also get a base of 50-299 raw units (the "50-350" of older text was this range, misread). Each mineral's raw total is then divided by 16, rounded down, and added to the planet's surface stock **in kT** (raw `0x3648`-`0x366e`). That gives 190-1,268 kT for a mineral that was hit and 3-18 kT for one that was not.
- **Environment:** `size + 1` axes change, three at most. Each axis moves by 3-5 points (uniform), or 6-10 points for a huge comet (the sum of two 3-5 draws). The sign is drawn separately for each axis, 50/50. The change is applied to both the current and the original value, and each value is clamped to 1..99.

  **Which axes change is fixed by storage order: Gravity for a small comet, Gravity and Temperature for a medium one, all three for large and huge** (the loop indexes the axis directly, raw `0x3670`-`0x3707`). The axis names that fill the owner's `\e` fields come from a *separately shuffled* axis list (raw `0x3451`-`0x3496`, pushed as message parameters at `0x3542`-`0x3546`). So a small or medium comet's message can name an axis that did not change. This is a code-level finding and was not checked live.
- **Queue:** afterwards the planet's production queue is cleaned up (`FUN_10b8_371a`, `production-queue.md` §8).

| Size (index) | Chance | Owner's colonists lost | Minerals hit | Concentration gain (cap 200) | Surface kT added to a mineral that was hit | Axes changed | Points per axis |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small (0) | 25% | 25% | 1 | +50-99 | 190-1,268 | Gravity | ±3-5 |
| Medium (1) | 25% | 45% | 2 | +50-99 | 190-1,268 | Gravity, Temperature | ±3-5 |
| Large (2) | 25% | 65% | 3 | +50-99 | 190-1,268 | all three | ±3-5 |
| Huge (3) | 25% | 85% | 3 | +65-128 | 190-1,268 | all three | ±6-10 |

**Environment shift (`FUN_10b8_37c8`, raw `0x37c8`-`0x38d7`).**
- **Chance:** 1 in 20 each year. There is no minimum year, but the protection test above applies.
- **Axis:** exactly one axis changes, chosen by `random(3)` (uniform).
- **Message:** the owner gets message 253, naming that axis, before the change is made. Unowned planets change silently.
- **Size of the step:** a first draw of `3 + random(3)` gives 3, 4 or 5. If it gives 3, it is replaced by `6 + random(3)`, which gives 6, 7 or 8. So the step is:
  - 4 points: 1/3;
  - 5 points: 1/3;
  - 6, 7 or 8 points: 1/9 each.

  A step of 3 never occurs, and the mean is 5⅓.
- **Direction and range:** the sign is 50/50. The step is applied to both the current and the original value, clamped to 1..99.
- **Queue:** the queue cleanup runs afterwards.

**Mineral deposit (`FUN_10b8_38dc`, raw `0x38dc`-`0x3975`).**
- **Chance:** 1 in `(15 - s)` each year, where `s` is the galaxy-size index `DAT_1128_0074`. It applies only from counter 10 onward.
- **Mineral:** one mineral, chosen by `random(3)`.
- **Message:** the owner gets message 254, naming that mineral. The message is sent even when nothing changes.
- **Effect:** if the mineral's concentration is below 180, it gains 5-19 points, so the result is at most 198. Concentration of 180 or more is left as it is.
- **What it does not do:** it adds no surface minerals, has no protection test, and does **not** run the queue cleanup.

**How the size index is stored.** The New Game dialog sets `s` to the id of the checked size button minus 1000 (`:54126`-`54133`). Parsing the dialog resources in `stars.exe` (template `0x80d1` "New Game" and template `0x8186`) shows that ids 1000-1004 are **Tiny, Small, Medium, Large, Huge**. The batch-file reader accepts only 0-4 (`:52136`-`52144`). This is the same index that gives the galaxy diameter `(s+1) x 400` (`new-game-setup.md` §3). The resulting chances are below. They were not checked in a live game.

| Galaxy | Index | Chance per year |
|---|---|---|
| Tiny | 0 | 1 in 15 |
| Small | 1 | 1 in 14 |
| Medium | 2 | 1 in 13 |
| Large | 3 | 1 in 12 |
| Huge | 4 | 1 in 11 |

**Units and bounds these events use.**
- **Surface stock is in kT.** The three 32-bit fields at planet bytes `0x1c`/`0x20`/`0x24` hold the surface stock in kT:
  - the mining engine adds the kT it mines straight into them (`FUN_1028_3a74`, `:13565`);
  - galaxy creation seeds them in kT (`:50592`-`50617`);
  - the Trader's planet trade (below) takes its 3,500 or 5,000 kT price out of them.

  So the comet's "divide by 16" result is in kT.
- **Concentration is an unsigned byte** at planet bytes `9`-`0xb`.
  - An ordinary planet starts at 31-119, with some poor rolls lowered to 1-30 (`:50533`-`50585`, `new-game-setup.md` §3). So "stops near 100" was never right.
  - The mining engine takes its yield from the raw value, so 200 mines twice as much as 100. For depletion it treats anything above 100 as 100 (`:13575`-`13584`), and mining never takes a value below 1 (`population-growth.md` §5).
  - The comet's 200 is a real ceiling on the stored value, not a clamp on an intermediate result.
  - The deposit event's below-180 test keeps its result at 198 or less.

**Mystery Trader creation (`FUN_10b8_3976`, raw `0x3976`-`0x3bba`).**

*What the Trader is.* It is not a fleet and has no ships or design. It is one 18-byte special-object entry of type 3, which is category `0x60` in byte 1. The entry is created by `FUN_1118_0000(0, 3)` (`:114837`-`114931`) and has these fields:
- `+2`/`+4`: position;
- `+6`/`+8`: destination;
- low nibble of `+0xa`: speed as a warp number;
- `+0xc`: a bitmask of the races it has already served;
- `+0xe`: the one item it carries.

*Year gate and chance.* Creation needs counter ≥ 40 (year 2440 onward). The chance that year is checked in this order:
1. 1 in 2 if the counter modulo 100 is 71;
2. otherwise 1 in 3 if it is 33;
3. otherwise 1 in 4 if the counter modulo 128 is 49;
4. otherwise none at all in an odd year;
5. otherwise 1 in 7 (an even year).

For example: 2449 is 1 in 4, 2450 is 1 in 7, 2451 is none, 2471 is 1 in 2, and 2533 is 1 in 3.

*How many can exist.* The spawn does not check for an existing Trader, so **several can be alive at once**. The only limit is the special-object table: creation fails above 4,049 entries of all kinds, or when all 511 serial numbers for this type are in use (`:114866`, `:114894`). When a spawn fails, the roll is lost.

*Speed.* The starting speed is 8-12 (`8 + random(5)`).

*Position and destination.* A 50/50 draw chooses whether the Trader crosses the galaxy along x or along y:
- it starts on one edge and heads for the opposite edge;
- a second 50/50 draw decides which of the two edges is the start;
- the edge coordinate lies 20 ly inside the boundary: 1,020 or `1,000 + 400(s+1) - 20`;
- the other coordinate is uniform over 1,020 to `1,380 + 400s`, drawn separately for the start and the destination.

There is no rejection sampling and no proximity scoring. (This corrects the Cross-references claim below that the Trader's placement shares the wormhole sampler.)

*Broadcast.* Every player gets message 299 (an announcement that an unknown trading ship has been sighted entering the explored galaxy).

*Cargo.* The item is chosen as follows:
1. Start with a chance `k`: 5 while the counter is below 100, 3 below 250, and 2 after that.
2. Add 1 to `k` for speed 8-9, and subtract 1 for speed 11-12.
3. With probability `k`/10 the Trader carries no part. In 1 case in 6 of those, it carries the "auxiliary ships" item (value `0x1000`). Otherwise it carries **technology** (value 0).
4. Otherwise it carries one of the 13 item bits of `ship-design-and-components.md` §14a (bits 0-11 are the twelve rare parts, and bit 12 is the ships item), drawn with `random(13)`.
5. If that first draw is Anti Matter Torpedo, Multi Contained Munition, Genesis Device or Jump Gate, the code draws once more and keeps the second result.
6. If the second result is Multi Contained Munition before counter 120, Genesis Device before 150, or Jump Gate before 180, a 50/50 roll can turn the cargo into technology instead.

Within the part branch, each of the nine unrestricted values therefore comes up with probability 17/169 (about 10.1%). Each of the four restricted values comes up with probability 4/169 (about 2.4%), halved while its year gate applies.

**Mystery Trader movement (`FUN_10b0_0f9a(0)`, §1 step 15; raw segment 23, `0x110a`-`0x147f`, `0x11c6`-`0x12f3`, `0x12f6`-`0x13c4`).** Each year, before fleets move, each Trader does the following:
1. **Course change.** If its speed is 12 or less, it has a 1-in-25 chance to change course. When it does, its speed rises by 1 and every player gets message 304. In 1 case in 3 it also picks a new destination on a random one of the four edges, using the same edge rule as the spawn.
2. **Step.** It moves `speed²` light-years straight toward its destination. Each coordinate step is rounded to the nearest whole number, using ±0.5 constants at DS `0x1d42`/`0x1d4a` and a 0.0001 zero guard at `0x1d52`/`0x1d5a`.
3. **Arrival.** It arrives when the remaining distance is no more than `speed²`.
4. **After arrival.** If any other Trader exists, this one is removed. If it is the only Trader:
   - 50%: it is removed;
   - 50%: it makes "another pass". It moves to the destination, its speed becomes `max(speed - 1, 7)`, it gets a fresh random-edge destination, every player gets message 192, and it does not move again that year.

   The mask of races already served is never reset. Fleets heading for a Trader that has been removed get message 272 (§10).

**Mystery Trader encounters (`FUN_1118_0784(1)`, post-movement stage right after the battle pass, §1 step 23c; raw segment 36, `0x0784`-`0x1194`).** This corrects §9 and `fleet-movement-scanning-cargo.md`'s "Heavy-fleet transit arithmetic" note. The six-byte array those texts read as "gas tolerances" is the race's **six tech levels** (`0x59dc`, the same array §5's 6-entry table uses). The "26 or 10" is the **tech-level cap** (26 normally, 10 when per-race status bit 1 is set, the bit §9 discusses). The routine drifts nothing and relocates nothing.

The encounter rules for fleets apply to every fleet that is not already destroyed and stands **exactly** on a Trader's position:
1. **Too few minerals.** If the fleet carries less than 5,000 kT of Ironium, Boranium and Germanium together, it gets message 264, unless it was itself given out by a Trader. Nothing else happens, and the fleet can try again another year.
2. **Race already served.** If this Trader has already served the fleet's race, the fleet gets message 280 and nothing happens.
3. **Otherwise the trade goes ahead.** The Trader marks the race as served, and the **whole fleet is absorbed**: it is flagged destroyed with its ships and all its cargo. The reward is then decided as follows.

   - **Technology route.** This route is taken when the Trader carries technology, or carries a part the race already owns.
     - If every field is already at the cap, a 1-in-5 roll gives message 270 and no reward. Otherwise (the other 4 in 5) the reward goes to the part route.
     - Otherwise the number of advances N starts at `6 + floor((minerals - 5,000) / 1,200)`, capped at 10. It is then adjusted by the race's total level T across the six fields:
       - T ≥ 108: N = 1;
       - T 96-107: N = 2;
       - T 84-95: N - 3;
       - T 72-83: N - 2;
       - T 60-71: N - 1;
       - T < 60: unchanged.

       (Older text said "subtract 1" for 72-83; the raw bytes at `0x0e7c`-`0x0e85` subtract 2.)
     - The race gets message 265, or message 266 if it already owns all 13 item bits. The message quotes N.
     - N times, a field is chosen: 3 times in 4 a random field, kept if it is below the cap; otherwise the lowest field (the first of any tie). If the lowest field is already at the cap, the loop stops. The chosen field's banked research pool is **doubled and the full cost of its next level is added** (`FUN_10d8_4a86`). The research buy loop `FUN_10b8_4ce4(0)` then runs, so each step buys at least one level.
   - **Part route.** This route is taken when the Trader carries a part the race lacks, or when the technology route falls through to it.
     - The item is the Trader's part, or a random one of the 13 if it carried technology.
     - If the race already owns the item, the code draws again, up to 25 times. If every draw is owned, the item becomes the ships item.
     - A part is granted through `FUN_1118_1196`, which gives message 267, or 268 for the Mini Morph hull, or 271 for the Genesis Device. These are the same codes, without the +47 offset, that salvage turns into 314/315/318.
   - **Ships item.** **Computer players get nothing**: the fleet is still absorbed, and no message is sent.
     - A human player gets one of three built-in designs, stored as 147-byte templates in segment 2 at offset `0x138f + 147 x index`: "M.T. Lifeboat" (probability 1/4, or 1/3 after counter 100), otherwise "M.T. Scout" or "M.T. Probe", equally likely.
     - The ship count is 1, or 2 with probability 1/3. After counter 100, unless the game has only one human player, `random(counter/100 + 1)` more are added. The count is then capped at 5. For a Scout or Probe, a further `random(count + 1)` ships are added.
     - The race needs a free design slot among its 16, or an identical design already present, and fewer than 512 fleets. If so, it gets a new fleet at the absorbed fleet's position, flagged as Trader-given, and message 335. If not, it gets message 336 instead.
   - Message 269 is not produced by this routine.

**Encounter rules for planets (computer players only).** A planet can trade with a Trader when all of these hold:
- the planet is owned and has a starbase;
- its owner is a computer player with skill tier 2 or higher (bits 10-12 of the slot word, raw `0x08b7`-`0x08da`);
- it lies within 100 ly of the Trader;
- the Trader has not yet served that race;
- the planet's surface Ironium, Boranium and Germanium add up to at least the price.

The price is 3,500 kT for tier 2 and 5,000 kT for higher tiers. If the tier numbering follows the menu order, tier 2 is Tough and tier 3 is Expert.

The planet trade then works as follows:
1. **Part first.** If the Trader carries a part the race lacks, that part is granted. If the race already has it, the code draws again, up to 50 times, for one it lacks. A part granted this way sets the race's item bit silently.
2. **Technology otherwise.** If the race's tech total is below `6 x (cap - 1)` (150 normally), its lowest field is raised by one level six times, with no research cost.
3. **Nothing possible.** If neither step applies, nothing happens and no minerals are taken.
4. **Payment.** After a trade, the race is marked as served and the price is deducted from Germanium first, then Boranium, then Ironium.

   One exception: on the part path, the planet's **entire** surface stock is deducted, because that path jumps to the deduction (raw `0x103e` to `0x110b`) with the stock total still in the amount variable. This is a code-level finding and was not checked live.

**Residue.**
- The comet's axis mismatch, the part-path full-stock deduction and the tier names are read from code only.
- The year base and the size-index values were not checked in a live game.
- The `FUN_1030_821a(race, 0x4e, fleet)` call made when a fleet is absorbed was not decoded.

### 6. Mineral Alchemy auto-conversion (Alternate Reality): retracted, the mechanic does not exist in this build

**Verdict (AR-alchemy pass).** Alternate Reality races have **no automatic, non-queued conversion of resources into minerals.** An AR planet's resources are handled exactly like any other race's. The race's research percentage (race byte `DAT_1128_59fa`) is taken off the top unless the planet's "leftover only" flag is set. The rest pays for the production queue, and whatever is left goes to research (`FUN_10b8_0000`, `stars.exe.export.c:76381`-`76423` and `76600`-`76616`, into the per-race accumulator `DAT_1128_59fc`). The only way any race, AR included, turns resources into minerals is the queued Alchemy item (types 3/11, `production-queue.md` §7). AR's surface minerals rise without a build order only through its innate mining: an AR planet's effective mine count is √(population in hundreds), rounded by the shared FPU idiom (`FUN_1048_4cce`, raw bytes seg 10 `0x4d01`-`0x4d11`). ### 7. Research allocation

Several times per turn (the buy loop `FUN_10b8_4ce4` runs at §1 steps 12e, 20 and 23g, and only the step 20 call adds the year's income; the others spend what is already banked), per race, accumulated research resources are applied to whichever field(s) the player has selected (see `research-tech-tree.md`), advancing tech levels and firing a level-up notification. Within one run of the buy loop, a surplus left after one level is bought keeps buying further levels, so a turn's research spending is not strictly capped by the exact cost of the next level in the primary field — genuine overflow carries within the same turn into the next field or the next level. The loop's only repeat pass is narrower than a general leftover sweep: when more than one race is in the game, Super Stealth races receive their passive research bonus (per field, half the average all races spent there), and if any such bonus was granted the buy loop runs once more so those resources can buy levels the same turn (see `research-tech-tree.md` Open Questions, "Refinement to the second pass framing").

### 9. Fleet-linked habitat-tolerance drift (one specific primary trait)

**Superseded in full by §5a, "Mystery Trader encounters" (random-event pass).** There is no habitat-tolerance drift. The six per-race bytes at `0x59dc` that this section reads as "gas tolerances" are the race's **six tech levels**. The "26 or 10" threshold is the tech-level cap. The "tier" of 1-10 is the number of **tech advances** the Mystery Trader grants for an absorbed fleet carrying 5,000+ kT of minerals. The 1-in-5 branch for a race whose levels are all capped posts message 270 (the Trader has nothing new to teach); the other 4 in 5 go on to the part/ships reward. Nothing is relocated. The mineral-count arithmetic below stands, except that a tech total of 72-83 subtracts 2, not 1. The paragraphs below are kept only as history.

**Correction from the phase-order reconciliation (§1): the routine that triggers this mechanic, `FUN_1118_0784`, is the Mystery Trader encounter routine, not a wormhole cargo check.** It walks the special-object table for entries of category `0x60`, which §10's "Resolved" paragraph identified as the Mystery Trader, and looks for fleets sharing the Trader's position (`stars.exe.export.c:115309`-`115335`). The messages it posts confirm this: 264 (the Trader refused to meet the named fleet's captain, probably because the fleet carried too few minerals; for a fleet under 5,000 kT, `:115343`-`115346`), 265/266 (the fleet was taken in by the Trader, which granted a stated number of technology advances; `:115430`-`115436`), 270 (the Trader has nothing new to teach) and 280 (the Trader is still recovering from its previous deal with this player; the per-race "already dealt with the Trader" bit at `:115364`-`115368`). The 5,000 kT threshold, the 1,200 kT tier steps, the 26-or-10 tolerance-sum gate, the 1-in-5 branch and the drift loop described below are therefore parts of the Mystery Trader's mineral-for-technology trade (its reward rolls draw from the one-time component-grant bitmask of §5), and the phrases "wormhole", "wormhole malfunction" and "per-wormhole bitmask" used in this section and in `fleet-movement-scanning-cargo.md` should be read as "Mystery Trader". The routine runs only in the post-movement stage (§1 step 23c); its whole body is skipped when called from the pre-movement stage.

A per-turn pass, gated to run only when at least one race in the game has a specific primary racial trait, drifts that race's stored per-gas habitat-tolerance value by exactly **±1 per turn** (clamped to the 1-99 range) for fleets meeting certain field thresholds, under three combined random gates (roughly 1-in-3, 1-in-10, and 1-in-1000 chance checks together with threshold comparisons), notifying the race when a shift occurs. A related calculation can also adjust per-gas values directly. This reads as a "race's tolerance genome gradually shifts based on fleet exposure" mechanic tied to one specific PRT, not previously documented anywhere; which named trait this is was not confirmed (no string data survives to check against `race-traits.md`'s PRT table).

**Trigger identified, cross-confirmed against `fleet-movement-scanning-cargo.md`'s "Heavy-fleet transit arithmetic" note.** This is the *same* drift routine invoked from the fleet-vs-wormhole heavy-cargo check documented there: a fleet carrying 5000+ kT of minerals through/at a wormhole computes a tier (6-10, one step per further 1200 kT, adjusted down by the transiting race's own gas-tolerance-sum), and that tier becomes this pass's per-iteration count for the drift loop described above — heavier mineral cargo means more drift iterations for that race this turn. The "gated to run only when at least one race has a specific primary racial trait" condition in that same code path corresponds to a race whose *entire* 6-value gas-tolerance array already exceeds a threshold (a broadly gas-tolerant race), which is instead diverted to a separate 20%-chance fleet-relocation branch rather than the drift loop — so the drift mechanic itself runs for *ordinary* (non-exempt) races, triggered specifically by heavy mineral-cargo wormhole transits, not by a flat per-turn roll independent of fleet activity as the "per-turn pass" framing above might suggest.

**Resolved by a later pass, and the "specific primary racial trait" premise overturned — cross-referenced here from `fleet-movement-scanning-cargo.md`, which had already settled this independently.** Reading `FUN_1118_0784` (`stars.exe.export.c:115255`-`115814`) directly at the threshold-selection point (`stars.exe.export.c:115354`) shows the "26 or 10" gas-tolerance-sum threshold used by the "broadly gas-tolerant race" exemption above is not a PRT-index comparison at all: the threshold is 26 when the race's status bit is clear and 10 when it is set — it is chosen by **bit 1 (mask 2) of the same per-race status byte**, this document's §5 "fourth event" gating bit now identifies as a copy-protection/status field rather than a trait table (see §5's new finding there). No call to the confirmed PRT-index accessor (`FUN_10e0_222c`, reading byte 14 of the settings array at player-record offset `0x3e`) or to the lesser-trait tester (`FUN_10e0_226e`, offset `0x4e`) appears anywhere in this threshold-selection code path, and any race of any PRT can enter the "all six clear" exemption branch if its own rolled tolerance values happen to be wide enough. **This means the drift mechanic documented in this section is not gated by a Primary Racial Trait at all** — the premise this section originally set out to resolve does not hold up under direct inspection. `DAT_1128_5a16` bit 1's own write site was not traced (per this project's standing rule around the copy-protection-adjacent status bits on this same field), but by analogy with bit 2's confirmed role (§5) and this field's other observed uses — gating a reduced research-level cap at `stars.exe.export.c:80138`-`80145`, and a percentage adjustment inside population-growth-adjacent code at `20983`/`76404` — bit 1 is plausibly a second, related status/penalty flag on the same byte rather than an independent gameplay trait; this reading is offered as well-evidenced, not certain. This section's title and framing ("gated to run only when at least one race has a specific primary racial trait") should be read as superseded: the mechanic is a per-race tolerance-data comparison, modulated by this status-byte bit, not keyed to a named PRT.

### 10. The master routine's concrete identity, confirmed by inspection of the exported client

The 40-step pipeline of §1 is not spread across an unidentified set of routines: it is implemented, concretely, by a single function plus five sibling routines all living in one segment, previously catalogued in `code-coverage-report.md` and `new-game-setup.md` §7 under the mischaracterization "new-game start commit." That routine is confirmed (by its four call sites — the manual "Generate Turn" command and the batch 10/100/1000-turn commands of §8 above) to be the client's actual master turn-generation entry point, reused verbatim to produce a new game's first turn and every later turn. Of its five sibling routines in the same segment:

- One is the two-stage driver `FUN_10b0_0ebe` (§1 steps 12 and 23): a per-planet ownership-flag refresh, and, depending on the stage, the battle pass, the Mystery Trader encounter routine (§9 correction; the ~560-line routine formerly labelled a fleet-vs-wormhole cargo-transit routine), the four waypoint-task passes, colonisation and invasion resolution, research, and the cargo-transfer report.
- One implements the per-fleet docking/fuel pass (`FUN_10b0_1d6e`, §1 step 22): a fleet docked at a friendly starbase is refuelled to full, and any other fleet gains 50 mg per Anti-matter Generator and 200 mg per Fuel Transport or Super-Fuel Transport ship. (The earlier reading of this pass as ramscoop-engine fuel was wrong.)
- One hosts the already-documented **waypoint-task execution / Stargate cargo-transit-restriction** mechanic (`fleet-movement-scanning-cargo.md`'s "Waypoint-task feasibility" and Stargate sections), confirmed to be reached through this same master routine rather than a separately-running turn-loop function. **Reconciliation note:** this routine, `FUN_10b0_1f8c`, is the *fleet movement* pass (§1 step 16, which runs before production); the Stargate feasibility check and the forced cargo dump it contains are part of a fleet's movement leg, and the task execution proper (Transport, Scrap, Merge, Lay Mines, remote mining, Transfer) is `FUN_10b0_3f3a`, run four times by the stage driver. It also contains a further large per-fleet block with a per-slot random roll; this is the **warp-10 engine-strain attrition rule** (resolved in the "Final resolution" paragraph below). An early pass could not identify it and noted a superficial resemblance to §9's habitat-drift shape (per-gas-type loop, comparable odds); it is unrelated to that mechanic, whose traced call chain runs through segment 36 (`FUN_1118_0784`, see `fleet-movement-scanning-cargo.md`), so no correction to §9 follows from it.

  **The stochastic sub-block, first structural reading (the gate's meaning was later corrected, see "Final resolution" below).** This routine is `FUN_10b0_1f8c` (`stars.exe.export.c:73289`-`74079`); its per-slot random block sits at `stars.exe.export.c:73472`-`73560`. It is gated on the high nibble of one byte reachable from the fleet equalling hex `a0` (a value distinct from the hex `b0` checked nearby in the same function); this pass first read that byte as a hull "class" byte. For such a fleet, the block walks up to 16 design slots, skipping slots whose first component subtype is 7, 8, 9, 14 or 15, and for every remaining ship in a slot makes a flat 1-in-10 roll for that ship being lost this turn (the shared bounded-random helper `FUN_1040_1652`). Losses are tallied; if every slot's quantity is reduced to zero across the whole fleet, the fleet is flagged destroyed/removed (bit 4 of its status byte) and message type hex `e1` (225) is logged through the shared message-append routine; if only some ships are lost, the fleet's derived combat/capability statistics are recomputed (`FUN_1050_6e52`) rather than the fleet being removed. It is structurally distinct from all four random events catalogued in §5 and from §9's habitat-drift mechanic. The "which hull class is hex `a0`" question this reading raised is answered below: it is not a hull class at all but the Warp 10 setting of the fleet's next leg.

  **Follow-up pass — the exact byte address was pinned down precisely, but the cross-reference against `extracted-game-data/component-stats.tsv` this raised did not resolve the hull name.** The "offset 6" in the first reading was an artifact of the decompiler treating the pointer as an array of 4-byte elements, not a literal byte offset of 6: tracing the pointer chain from the fleet record shows the indexed pointer is 4 bytes past a block whose address the fleet record caches as a far pointer at byte offset hex `64` (`stars.exe.export.c:73375`-`73377`), so the byte tested is at offset hex `1c` (28 decimal) of that block — the same byte independently read at line `73380`. So the real target is **offset hex `1c`** of the block the fleet's cached pointer (called "the fleet's private array" below) points to, not offset 6.

  *(Superseded: the next three paragraphs record an intermediate hypothesis that the block was a design record or design-stack cache; the "Final resolution" paragraph shows it is the fleet's waypoint list.)* Whether the pointed-to block is the per-owned-design (147-byte, `ship-design-and-components.md` §1) record or the static per-hull-*type* definition table (`extracted-game-data/component-stats.tsv`'s "Ship hulls"/"Starbase chassis" combined 37-entry, 143-byte-stride table, per `ship-design-and-components.md`'s own §"hull-type index" note) was not conclusively determined this pass — the fleet record's cached far pointer could plausibly be either, and both are 143-147 bytes. Checking the raw offset against the static table's own documented layout does not yield a clean answer either way: the table's universal per-category header only defines offsets `0`-`7` (an ordinal plus six tech-prerequisite bytes) before the 32-byte name field begins at offset `8`, so offset `0x1c` (28) falls **inside that name-text region** for every one of the TSV's 32 extracted hull records (whose six prerequisite bytes are all `0`, confirming the name field truly starts at `8` for this category) — not a plausible location for a single classifying "hull class" byte. This suggests either (a) the private array is not this static table at all, or (b) the 143-byte hull/starbase-chassis table's internal layout diverges from the generic per-category header past the shared 8-byte prefix (consistent with the TSV's own header note that "hull records additionally carry a slot array" and other extra structure beyond the generic layout). Cross-referencing `component-stats.tsv` as instructed therefore did not identify a named hull this pass — it narrowed the question (the real offset is hex `1c`, not 6) without answering it, and is recorded here as a well-evidenced negative result rather than a resolution.

  **Resolved by tracing the pointer's origin at its call sites precisely, as instructed — and the premise of the original either/or question turns out to be false.** The private array (the fleet record's cached far pointer at byte offset hex `64`) is **neither** the static hull-definition table **nor** the per-owned-design 147-byte record directly — it is a small, **per-fleet, heap-allocated, growable array of design-stack entries**, private to that one fleet record, unrelated to either fixed table by storage location. This was established by tracing every site that allocates, resizes, or copies this exact field (all identifiable by the identical byte offset hex `64` into the same global owned-fleet array that `ai-opponent-behavior.md`'s "Process-of-elimination pass" independently confirms this project's fleet table to be):
  - **Fleet creation** (`FUN_1038_1f72`, `stars.exe.export.c:18648`-`18743`) allocates this field fresh for every new fleet via a generic array allocator (`FUN_1060_057e`, `stars.exe.export.c:18717`-`18720`) — 3 slots of 18 bytes each, plus a 4-byte header recording the per-slot size and a slot count/capacity — and immediately populates the first slot (4 bytes into the array) with the new fleet's map position and a small integer at array offset 8 that this pass read as the raw **design *index*** (0-25) of the design to build (`stars.exe.export.c:18722`-`18727`; the final resolution below shows it is the building planet's id). A design index, not a pointer, is exactly what one would expect this array to cache if it exists to let a fleet track *which* of a race's design slots each of its (possibly several, once merged) ship stacks belongs to — not to directly address either candidate table.
  - **Fleet splitting** (`FUN_1038_2180`, `stars.exe.export.c:18747`-`18801`) and **fleet merging** (`FUN_1038_1582`, `stars.exe.export.c:17908`-`18000`) both treat this field as fleet-owned memory needing a deep copy, not a shared reference: they read the per-entry size stored in the low byte of the block's own header (18, set at creation) to compute a new byte count, call a realloc-style allocator (`FUN_1060_053e`, `stars.exe.export.c:42459`-`42472`) to grow the *destination* fleet's own copy of the array, and then block-copy the *source* fleet's entries into it slot-for-slot (`stars.exe.export.c:17961`-`17979`, `18779`-`18797`). `FUN_1038_1582` (Merge Fleets) additionally compares the two fleets' array pointers for simple equality (`stars.exe.export.c:17953`), which this pass read as a same-design/different-design test — this by itself rules out the static hull table as the referent, since two different owned designs sharing one hull *type* would incorrectly compare equal under that scheme, while it is consistent with either the per-owned-design table (which this per-fleet array does *not* turn out to be) or, as traced here, a fleet-private cache addressed by design identity. If the pointer pointed into either shared, fixed-position table, none of this resize/deep-copy machinery would be needed at all — a bare pointer copy would do, exactly as the surrounding ~100 bytes of each fleet record *are* copied verbatim in the same functions (`stars.exe.export.c:17983`-`17989`).

  Net result: the attrition mechanic's tested byte at offset hex `1c` is read from inside this private per-fleet array (specifically, from 24 bytes past the start of its first 18-byte entry — which, given the entries' own 18-byte stride, actually falls at the start of the *second* entry, byte offset 6 within it), not from a byte that this project's two extracted tables (`component-stats.tsv`'s static hull table, or the per-owned-design record) can be indexed into directly. This also explains the immediately-preceding gate this same block applies (`stars.exe.export.c:73379`): the block is skipped when the fleet-record word at word index hex `31` — which mirrors this same array's own entry count — is below 2, so the second entry is only inspected once it exists (this pass read that as "at least two merged-in designs"; the final resolution reads it as "at least two waypoints"). The main question this pass was asked to settle — static table vs. per-owned-design record — is resolved: it is neither.

  **Follow-up pass — the exact line pair re-read directly.** `stars.exe.export.c:73378`-`73380` is a single compound skip-condition: the whole block is skipped if the low nibble of the first entry's byte at array offset 10 (the current task of waypoint 0) is 1 or 6 (Transport or Lay Mines), or if the entry count is below 2, or if the high nibble of the byte at array offset hex `1c` (byte 6 of the second entry) is zero. The array pointer is re-read from the fleet record (byte offset hex `64`) at the top of this statement (`stars.exe.export.c:73375`), and the entry-count gate and the byte-`1c` read sit in the *same* either-or chain. This directly confirms that the byte at offset hex `1c` is only consulted once the entry-count gate has established that a second entry exists.

  **Final resolution (segment-24 sweep, this pass): the private array is the fleet's waypoint list, "hull class `0xa0`" is Warp 10, and this block is the warp-10 engine-strain rule.** Three independent facts pin this down. (1) Fleet creation, `FUN_1038_1f72`, receives the *planet id* of the building planet as its second argument (from the queue-completion routine, `stars.exe.export.c:77146`, where it is passed the planet's own id), not a design index, and writes it into the first 18-byte entry beside the spawn position — so the entries are waypoint records (target object id, x/y, task/warp byte; `fleet-movement-scanning-cargo.md`), and the "design index" reading above was a mislabel. (2) `FUN_1080_13f8` (`:56865`-`56989`), called for a newly built ship when the starbase has a rally destination (`:77153`), sets the entry count to two and fills the second waypoint with the rally target and the highest warp the fuel allows, stored in the high nibble of that waypoint's byte 6 — which is byte `0x1c` of the array. The queue code tests that nibble for zero to choose between the notices saying the new ship was routed to its rally point (messages 49/50) and those saying it could not be routed for lack of fuel (51/52). (3) The attrition block's own entry-count-below-2 gate therefore means "no second waypoint", and its test of that high nibble against hex `a0` means **the fleet's next leg is ordered at Warp 10**. For each design slot with ships, unless the slot-0 component subtype is 7, 8, 9, 14 or 15 (the five engines rated safe at warp 10: Interspace-10, Enigma Pulsar, Trans-Star 10, Trans-Galactic Mizer Scoop and Galaxy Scoop in `component-stats.tsv`), **each ship** rolls a 1-in-10 chance of being destroyed (`:73493`-`73531`). If every ship in the fleet is lost the fleet is removed with message 225 (the whole fleet was lost to an engine/reactor accident caused by unsafe operation); if exactly one ship is lost, message 223 (one ship of the named fleet was destroyed when its engines failed while pushing to Warp 10); if several, message 224 (the same notice with the number of ships lost to engine strain). This is the same 10%-per-ship rule `fleet-movement-scanning-cargo.md` §1 states from public sources, and it now has its code location; there is no separate "hull class" mechanic. The neighbouring test just above it (`:73398`-`73409`) is the Cheap Engines rule from the same document: a leg faster than warp 6 by a Cheap Engines race has a 1-in-10 chance of the fleet not moving at all that turn (message 242, which blames uncooperative engines). Also in this movement pass (`:73449`-`73470`), a fleet of an Alternate Reality race carrying more than 10 units of colonists loses (cargo + 11) × 3 / 100 units of them (integer division; roughly 3%), with message 193 (a notice that the stress of warp acceleration killed that many of the fleet's colonists). **Now traced (this pass):** the cargo figure is the fleet's colonist cargo in units of 100 colonists (1 kT each); the message prints the unit count followed by two literal zeros, so it reads as a colonist count; and the loss is applied once per turn per fleet, in movement pass 0 only, to any AR fleet that is actually setting out (at least two waypoints, first task not Transport or Lay Mines, non-zero leg warp below the Stargate value, not stopped by the refusal or balky rolls) regardless of warp or any change of speed, whenever the load exceeds 10 units; fuel shortage does not prevent it. Full rule and examples: `fleet-movement-scanning-cargo.md` section 1.

  A structurally related, previously-uncatalogued branch sits immediately after this same gate (`stars.exe.export.c:73381`-`73394`) and is worth flagging here even though it is not part of the attrition mechanic itself: it re-reads the private array and, before falling through to the Warp-10 (hex `a0`) attrition check this section documents, tests bit 2 (mask 4) of the per-race status byte for the fleet's owning race — the identical per-race status byte and bit this document's §5 "fourth event" gating condition uses (see the new finding there). When that bit is set, and a turn-counter/race-index staggering condition additionally holds, the fleet has a 1-in-4 chance per relevant turn of being logged with message type `0x102` (`extracted-game-data/message-strings.txt` row 258, a notice that the fleet refuses to move because it doubts the player's authority) and is unconditionally skipped for the rest of this block regardless of whether that roll succeeds. This is a second, independent call site using the same status bit as §5's fourth event, discovered while re-reading this line pair; it is not part of the attrition rule and is not analyzed further here.
- One is a per-star pass with an RNG-gated conditional branch, taking a mode parameter that selects between two mutually-exclusive object categories at its two call sites. **Fully identified as `FUN_10b0_0f9a` (`stars.exe.export.c:72509`), called with mode 0 at line 72142 (before fleet movement) and mode 1 at line 72171 (after the production hub, before the post-movement stage that contains the battle pass; corrected by the §1 reconciliation, this text previously placed both calls after `FUN_10b0_1f8c`). ### 11. Segment 24 catalogue: the economy, terraforming, invasion and repair passes (added by the segment-24 sweep)

Segment 24 (NE segment 24, code file offset `0x74640`, length `0x539e`, 28 functions, `stars.exe.export.c:76294`-`80354`) had been filed in `code-coverage-report.md` as "production-queue item validation", which describes only its first three functions. **Colonisation and invasion (`FUN_10b8_1ea6`).** Pending landings are (planet, race, colonists) records. A landing is discarded with a message when: the race is Alternate Reality and the planet is already owned (message 87, which reports the colonists destroyed on landing; Alternate Reality can still found a colony on an *unowned* planet, message 11, "Orbital Construction Module". *AR-starbase pass: the exact test at `77756` is "landing race is PRT 8 and (the record's colonise bit is clear or the planet has any owner)". It never compares the owner with the landing race, so in principle it covers the race's own planets too. In practice no landing record for an owned planet can reach it. Records come only from `FUN_10b8_301e`, which always sets the colonise bit (`78376`), and it has two callers. The Colonize task (`75839`) creates a record only for a planet unowned at that moment; a colonise order on any owned planet, the race's own included, is cancelled first with message 82 (the planet is already inhabited) (`75855`-`75863`). The colonist-unload task (`75214`) creates records only for a planet owned by someone else, and an AR fleet is refused there with message 86 (the fleet's own crew refuses the order) (`75202`-`75210`). Unloading onto the race's own planet is an ordinary cargo transfer, not a landing. The landing pass runs right after the task pass that made the records (`FUN_10b0_0ebe`, `72497`-`72498`), and records are cleared after each pass (`78043`). So message 87 is a defensive branch, unreachable in normal play. When an AR race wins a landing, the resolver also installs its starbase design slot 0 ("Starter Colony") on the planet (`77946`-`77956`; see `population-growth.md` §3)*); the planet is unowned and the record lacks its colonize-order bit (message 2, a defensive case, because the normal record creator always sets that bit); or the planet has an owner and a starbase, which kills every landing (message 88; because the battle pass runs first in the same stage, destroying the starbase in battle opens the planet to invasion). Otherwise each race's landed colonists are scaled by a strength factor: **165% for War Monger, 110% for every other race** (Alternate Reality 0). On an unowned planet the largest force wins; an exact tie for first place destroys everyone (messages 5, 6); the winner's surviving colonists are scaled by (largest - runner-up) / largest, truncated, at least 1, which reproduces the tie-break already documented in §4. *Quirk (`77815`-`77843`, `77967`-`77971`):* races are scanned in index order keeping a running maximum, and the "runner-up" is the maximum the winner displaced, so it is the strongest contender with a **lower** race index than the winner, not the true second-largest force. A weaker contender with a higher index never becomes the runner-up; if every weaker contender has a higher index than the winner, the runner-up is 0 and the winner's colonists are not scaled at all. On an owned planet the defenders' strength is the planet's population, **doubled for Inner Strength**; if the attackers' combined strength is not at least the defenders', every attacker is destroyed (message 0 or 1 to each attacker, 3 or 4 to the defender), and the defenders' population falls by the proportional share; otherwise the strongest attacker takes the planet with survivors scaled by (attack - defence) / attack (message 12 to the winner, 13 to the other attackers, 7 to the former owner). A planet-defense coverage figure computed by `FUN_1038_0262` first destroys a share of the landing force (messages 1 and 4 quote it). After a change of owner the winner gets the default production queue (`production-queue.md` §10f); when there was a previous owner, the tech-salvage roll of §5 runs with that owner's tech levels as thresholds; and if the planet carries an **artifact** flag (bit `0x40` of the word at planet byte `0x1a`, cleared on use) and "No Random Events" is off, the new owner immediately receives `100 + random(0..300)` research resources in a random one of the six fields (scaled down when the founding population is under 1,000 colonists), message 94 (the colonists found an alien artifact that boosts research). The pass runs once before movement and once after (§11 order), which is the mechanism behind `research-tech-tree.md`'s "waypoint-zero invasions, then battles, then waypoint-one invasions".

**Repair (`FUN_10b8_2922`).** Each fleet that is not flagged destroyed has the damage figure of each of its 16 design stacks reduced, floor zero, by a yearly repair rate: **5** if the fleet moved this year; otherwise **10** in deep space; **15** orbiting a planet it does not own; **25** orbiting its own planet without a starbase; and at its own starbase **40**, or **100** when the starbase hull has a dock (a non-zero docking capacity, the same field messages 206/207 quote). Inner Strength doubles the location rate (not the transport bonus); a stack of Fuel Transport hulls (hull 25) adds 25 and Super-Fuel Transport (hull 26) adds 50 to the fleet's rate after that doubling. Starbases repair their own damage by **50 points** a year (**75** for Inner Strength) unless flagged as under construction. These figures are new to the specs; the units of the "damage figure" (bits 7-15 of each stack's damage word) were not converted to a percentage of armor.

**Repair, units and exact rule (resolved by a later pass; this supersedes the last sentence above and the two "under construction" / "not flagged destroyed" shorthands).** *Units.* The "damage figure" is the high nine bits of the stack's 16-bit damage word (fleet `+0x2c`, one word per design stack) and is measured in **1/500 of one ship's armor, i.e. fifths of a percent (0.2%) of armor, per damaged ship** (the low seven bits are the percentage of the stack's ships that carry that damage). Three independent readers agree on the 500 scale: the damage-total routine `FUN_1038_0526` (`:16809`-`16818`) computes `ships x (low7/100) x design armor (design +0x38) x (high9/500)`; the battle damage applier computes the damaged-ship count as `low7 x stack size / 100` (at least 1) and the per-ship damage as `high9 x armor / 500` (`FUN_10f0_5902`, `:102363`, `:102474`); and the colonisation/merge code re-normalises stacks with the same two divisors (`FUN_10b8_0e68`, `:77100`-`77130`). So the repair rates are in the same units: **5 = 1% of a damaged ship's armor per year, 10 = 2%, 15 = 3%, 25 = 5%, 40 = 8%, 100 = 20%**, i.e. repair rates of 1/2/3/5/8/20 percent. A rate is subtracted from the figure of every damaged stack of the fleet (`high9 -= rate`; if the rate is at least the figure, the whole word is cleared to zero, which also forgets the damaged-ship percentage), so all damaged ships of a stack heal equally and the percentage of damaged ships changes only by reaching zero. The figure is an *absolute* rate, not a percentage of the remaining damage, and is applied once per generation (step 25). *Which fleets.* Skipped entirely: fleets with flag byte `+5` bit `0x04` (removed from play) or bit `0x40` (set when the fleet was enrolled in a battle by `FUN_10f0_2c2a`, `:100387`, or hit a minefield, `FUN_10b0_312a`, `:74699`; cleared again in step 9, `:71957`), and fleets with no damaged stack. *Location rate* (`:78075`-`78140`, confirmed from the raw bytes of segment 24, offsets `0x2922`-`0x2b32`): bit `0x20` of the flag byte is set for every fleet at the start of the movement pass and cleared when the fleet actually moves (`:73370`, `:73684`), so "bit clear" means the fleet moved this year: rate 5. Otherwise: orbit field `0xffff` (deep space) 10; orbiting a planet owned by someone else, whether or not allied, 15; orbiting its own planet 25 when the planet has no starbase **or** the starbase took part in a battle this generation (planet byte `+0x2f` bit `0x40`, set by the battle pass at `:100343`/`:102712`, cleared on load, `:45637`); orbiting its own planet with a fresh starbase 40 if the starbase hull's dock-capacity field (hull `+0x34`: 0 for the Orbital Fort) is zero, 100 if it is not (Space Dock 200, the three stations 65,535). *Modifiers, in order:* Inner Strength (PRT index 4) **doubles the location rate only**; then the fleet-wide transport bonus is **added** after the doubling: 25 if any occupied stack is hull 25 (Fuel Transport), 50 if any is hull 26 (Super-Fuel Transport); with both present the bonus is 50, not 75, and it is per fleet, not per ship. The bonus applies even in a fleet that moved (5 + 25 or 5 + 50). Hull type, tech level, fuel and the number of ships otherwise play no part. *Starbase self-repair* (`:78150`-`78172`): every owned planet with a starbase (planet byte `+5` bit `0x02`) whose starbase did not fight this generation (`+0x2f` bit `0x40` clear) loses **50** damage units a year (**75** for Inner Strength); the starbase's damage is the high twelve bits of the planet word at `+0x2c` (low four bits = the starbase design slot) and uses the same 1/500-of-armor unit, so this is **10% (15% for Inner Strength) of the starbase's armor per year**. Ships repaired by a starbase dock use the fleet table above, not this one.

**Claim Adjuster terraforming (`FUN_10b8_2be8`).** Applies only when some player is Claim Adjuster (PRT 3). For each planet of such a race, once a year: on a random axis where the race is not immune and the planet's original value differs from the race's ideal, a 1-in-10 roll (and, for planets under 1,000 population units, a second roll scaled by population) moves the planet's **original** environment value one point toward the ideal (the population roll passes automatically at 1,000 population units, i.e. 100,000 colonists, and otherwise with probability population/1000), message 348 (the planet's underlying value on the named environment axis improved by 1%); then the planet's current environment is recomputed to the best values the race's terraforming technology allows and message 342 (the named planet was automatically terraformed to a stated habitability percentage) is posted. This is the code behind the trait's "free and instantaneous" terraforming and its 10%-a-year permanent drift (`race-traits.md` §2), and it is why the default-template copy skips terraform entries for the race.

**Fleet terraforming (`FUN_10b8_2e04`).** For each fleet in orbit of an owned planet whose terraforming capacity (`FUN_1080_1a34`) is positive: if the planet's owner is the fleet's owner, or the fleet's owner has rated the planet's owner as Friend, the fleet improves the planet toward the *fleet owner's* habitability; if not, and the planet has no starbase, it degrades the planet. One environment point moves per unit of capacity, clamped 1..99, and the messages are 300/301 (improved / cannot improve further) and 346/347 (degraded / cannot degrade further), sent to the fleet's owner and, when the value changed, the planet's owner.

**Mine sweeping (`FUN_10b8_465e`).** A fleet with a sweep rate (`FUN_1080_1ca2`) and a starbase with one (`FUN_1080_1d1c`) each remove mines from every minefield they sit inside whose owner they treat as an enemy (fleets: the battle-plan enemy test; starbases: any owner not rated Friend). The amount is their rate (at least 2; one third of the rate against the third field type), but never more than is needed to shrink the field until the sweeper's own position lies outside it, and never more than the field holds; the sweeper's owner gets message 194 (fleet) or 244 (starbase), and the field's owner message 190 (an unidentified party swept a stated number of mines from the field). A field reduced to nothing is deleted.

**Inner Strength colonist growth (`FUN_10b8_4b38`, `:79873`-`79947`; raw bytes at segment 24 offsets `0x4b38`-`0x4ce3`), rewritten this pass with the unit and the zero case settled.** Runs once per generation (step 19, after movement and before the production hub) and does nothing unless at least one race has Inner Strength (race setting 14 = 4). It walks the fleet table (stopping at the first empty slot) and, for every fleet that is not flagged destroyed, belongs to an Inner Strength race and has a non-zero colonist cargo (fleet record offset `0x58`; **unit: hundreds of colonists, 1 per kT of cargo space**, the same unit as a planet's population field and as the count printed in message 251, which appends two literal zeros):
1. The growth amount is the race's growth-rate setting times the colonist cargo, divided by 200 with integer division (truncating). The growth-rate setting is the owning race's **population growth slider** (race record byte `0x19`, 1-20; it is **not** scaled by any planet's habitability and nothing else modifies it). So the yearly growth is half the growth-rate setting, as a percentage of the load, rounded down: with a growth-rate setting (R) of 10, 40 units (4,000 colonists) gain 2 units (200 colonists); with R = 20, 100 units gain 10.
2. **Zero case.** When the formula gives 0 (growth rate times cargo below 200, e.g. a load of 19 units or fewer at R = 10) one random draw of 0, 1 or 2 is made: only a draw of 0 (1 chance in 3) sets the growth to 1 unit (100 colonists); a draw of 1 or 2 (2 chances in 3) leaves the fleet unchanged for the year. The earlier sentence "at least 1, with a 2-in-3 chance of no growth" therefore means: the floor of 1 applies only with probability 1/3; the expected gain of a small load is one third of a unit per year.
3. The growth amount is added to the colonist cargo through the ordinary cargo-add routine, which clamps to the hull's free cargo space and reports how much actually fitted. If any colonists fitted, message 251 (a notice that the fleet's on-board colonist count grew, giving the number added) reports that amount. If less than the full growth amount fitted (the hold is full or nearly) and the fleet is orbiting a planet (its waypoint-0 planet id is valid) **owned by the same race**, the excess that did not fit is added to that planet's population and message 344 is posted (a notice that breeding aboard the fleet overflowed its living space, giving the number of colonists beamed down and the planet they went to); if the planet belongs to someone else (or there is none) the excess is lost.
There is no random draw on the non-zero branch, no cap other than hull space, and no dependence on whether the fleet moved.
